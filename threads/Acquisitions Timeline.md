# Acquisitions Timeline

A chronological ledger of Berkshire's acquisitions and major investment positions
as they appear in the letters. Pre-1977 entries are reconstructed from references
in the [[1977]] letter; from 1977 forward, entries are added as each letter
reports them.

## Timeline

- **1965** — Buffett takes control of [[Berkshire Hathaway Textiles]].
- **1967** — Buys [[National Indemnity Company]] and National Fire & Marine for
  ~$8.6M, entering insurance. ([[1977]])
- **1969** — Buys the [[Illinois National Bank]] of Rockford. ([[1977]])
- **1970** — Founds Cornhusker Casualty (first "homestate" insurer). ([[1977]])
- **1971** — Buys Home & Automobile Insurance; founds Lakeland Fire & Casualty. ([[1977]])
- **1972** — [[Blue Chip Stamps]] buys [[See's Candies]]. ([[1977]])
- **1973** — Buys into [[Washington Post]] (stock). ([[1977]])
- **1976** — Begins buying [[GEICO]] during its near-bankruptcy. ([[1977]])
- **1977** — Buys 220,000 shares of [[Capital Cities]] for $10.9M; buys Cypress
  Insurance (Worker's Comp, South Pasadena) for cash; founds Kansas Fire &
  Casualty. Buffalo Evening News bought by Blue Chip. ([[1977]])
- **1978** — Merges with [[Diversified Retailing Company]] (Dec 30), raising the
  [[Blue Chip Stamps]] stake to ~58% and bringing in [[Associated Retail Stores]].
  New equity position in [[SAFECO]] (953,750 shares). ([[1978]])
- **1979** — [[Precision Steel]] acquired (via Wesco). By now Berkshire/Blue
  Chip/Wesco had bought 13 businesses outright from private owners and started 6
  others, all from the textile base. ([[1979]])
- **By Dec 31, 1980** — Must divest the [[Illinois National Bank]] (Bank Holding
  Company Act). By 1979 the Fed's officer/director rules tilt the plan from spin-off
  toward an outright *sale* of 80–100%. ([[1978]], [[1979]])
- **1980** — Increased [[GEICO]] stake to 7.2M shares (~33%), cost $47M (about half
  invested 1976, most of the rest in 1980). [[Illinois National Bank]] **divested**
  (Dec 31) via Rockford Bancorp exchange offer. New stock positions: Alcoa,
  Cleveland-Cliffs, Pinkerton's, R.J. Reynolds, Times Mirror, National Detroit.
  Issued $60M 12¾% notes due 2005. ([[1980]])
- **1981** — **No acquisitions.** Berkshire "came quite close to a major purchase"
  (a business and manager Buffett liked) but the price "would have left our owners
  worse off." New/expanded stock positions: R.J. Reynolds (mkt $83.1M), Arcata, GATX.
  ([[1981]])
- **1982** — No acquisitions; a large committed deal collapsed "for reasons totally
  beyond our control." **Berkshire–[[Blue Chip Stamps]] merger** announced for 1983.
  Formal acquisition "want ad" published (see [[Acquisition Criteria]]). New stock
  positions: Time Inc. (mkt $79.8M), Crum & Forster (cash substitute). ([[1982]])
- **1983** — Acquired **90% of [[Nebraska Furniture Mart]]** (the Blumkin family) —
  Berkshire's first major consumer-retail operating business since See's. Completed
  the full **[[Blue Chip Stamps]] merger** (Berkshire → 100% of Blue Chip, 80% of
  Wesco). ([[1983]])
- **1984** — Major new stock position in **Exxon** (~$175M, 4th-largest holding).
  Bought ~$139M of distressed **[[WPPSS Bonds]]**. *Subsequent event:* agreed to buy
  3M shares of **[[Capital Cities]]** at $172.50, contingent on its ABC acquisition.
  ([[1984]])
- **1985** — A landmark acquisition year: **[[Capital Cities]]/ABC** (3M shares,
  ~$517.5M, financing the ABC deal); **[[Scott Fetzer]]** (~$320M; 17 businesses);
  **[[Fireman's Fund]]** 7% quota-share. *Sold:* **[[General Foods]]** to Philip
  Morris (+$338M gain). **Textile business shut down**; **[[Illinois National Bank]]**
  (Rockford) fully sold. Acquisition "want ad" minimum raised to $10M after-tax
  earnings. ([[1985]])
- **1986** — **[[Fechheimer Brothers]]** acquired (~84% at a $55M whole-business
  valuation; closed 6/3/86) — the Cincinnati uniform maker that came in via Chairman
  Bob Heldman answering the annual-report "want ad." Also disclosed: a ~50% stake in
  **[[NHP]], Inc.** (multi-family housing) bought in 1985 for $23.7M. **No new
  marketable equities** met the tests; main capital moves were paying down debt and
  buying ~$700M of tax-exempt bonds. Want-ad minimum after-tax earnings now formally
  $10M. ([[1986]])
- **1987** — **No operating-business acquisitions.** The marquee move is a **$700M
  [[Salomon]] Inc 9% convertible preferred** (convertible at $38; a bet on CEO
  [[John Gutfreund]]). Other notable commitments: **Texaco** post-bankruptcy bonds
  (~$104M), **added [[WPPSS Bonds]]** ($240M cost), an **Allegis** arbitrage position
  (~$76M), and ~$900M of medium-term tax-exempts. No major common-stock purchases
  through the October crash (positions were either permanent or arbitrage). Berkshire
  issued $250M of 2018 debentures to keep a "loaded gun" for future elephants. ([[1987]])
- **1988** — Two big **stock** commitments (held "forever"): **[[Coca-Cola]]**
  (14,172,500 shares, cost $592.5M — buying continued into 1989) and **[[Freddie Mac]]**
  preferred (cost $71.7M, the legal maximum, via [[Mutual Savings and Loan]]).
  **[[Fechheimer Brothers]]** made a "fairly good-sized" bolt-on acquisition (okayed
  unseen). Big arbitrage: **RJR Nabisco** ($281.8M → $64M gain). *Subsequent event:* 80%
  of **[[Borsheim's]]** (Omaha jewelry, the Friedman family) acquired early 1989.
  Berkshire **listed on the NYSE** (Nov 29, 1988) and was rated **AAA** by S&P. ([[1988]])
- **1989** — **[[Borsheim's]]** (80%) closed early in the year. Three new **convertible
  preferreds**: **[[Gillette]]** ($600M, convert $50), **[[USAir]]** ($358M, $60), and
  **[[Champion International]]** ($300M, $38) — joining [[Salomon]] as the "four
  preferreds." **[[Coca-Cola]]** position nearly doubled to 23,350,000 shares (cost
  $1.024B). Sold low-coupon [[WPPSS Bonds]] and most medium-term tax-exempts. **Sat out
  arbitrage.** Issued **$902.6M of zero-coupon convertible debentures** (~$400M proceeds).
  Jet upgraded to a $6.7M used plane ("The Indefensible"). ([[1989]])
- **1990** — **"Lethargy bordering on sloth"**: Berkshire touched only one of its six big
  holdings. The exception and year's major move: **[[Wells Fargo]]** — ~10% of the bank for
  **$290M** (mostly in 1990, during a California-real-estate panic), backing
  [[Carl Reichardt]] and Paul Hazen. Added to **RJR Nabisco** bonds ($440M total). The
  **[[Gillette]]** preferred was **called**, converting April 1, 1991 into 12M common
  shares. No operating-business acquisitions. ([[1990]])
- **1991** — One operating acquisition: **[[H. H. Brown]]** (closed 7/1/91), the leading
  North American work-shoe and boot maker, ~$25M pre-tax earnings, run by CEO
  **[[Frank Rooney]]** — sourced when a Berkshire-shareholder friend (John Loomis) tipped
  Rooney off on a Florida golf course, after the selling investment bank failed to think of
  Berkshire. New marketable stock: **[[Guinness]]** ($264.8M cost) — Berkshire's first
  significant **foreign** investment. New fixed-income/preferred positions: an **[[American Express]]** "PERC" ($300M, 8.85%, mandatory conversion), a **[[First Empire State]]** 9%
  convertible preferred ($40M, backing CEO [[Bob Wilmers]]), and added **ACF Industries**.
  The **[[Gillette]]** preferred converted (April 1) into 24M common shares. *Eliminated:*
  an RJR Nabisco issue (exchange-offer/call). *Sold (and regretted):* the residual ~7M
  shares of **[[Fannie Mae]]** — see [[Mistakes Ledger]]. The acquisition **want-ad** (six
  criteria) is re-run. ([[1991]])
- **1992** — Operating acquisition: **82% of [[Central States Indemnity]]** (December), an
  Omaha credit-card payment-protection insurer (~$90M premiums, ~$10M profit) bought from
  35-year friend **[[Bill Kizer]]** (family keeps 18%) — "a prototype of what we now look
  for," and an explicit sequel to the 1967 National Indemnity deal. **Five add-on
  acquisitions** by subsidiaries, the largest being **[[H. H. Brown]]'s** purchase of
  **Lowell Shoe** (Nursemates; $90M sales). New marketable stock: **[[General Dynamics]]**
  (14.1%, cost $312.4M) — bought first as a Dutch-tender [[Arbitrage|arbitrage]], then
  converted to a long-term holding on admiration for CEO [[Bill Anders]]. Also **more than
  doubled [[Freddie Mac]]** (to 8.2%) and added to [[Wells Fargo]] and [[Guinness]]. *Sold:*
  RJR Nabisco (bonds); some [[WPPSS Bonds]] called. The **zero-coupon convertible
  debentures** (issued 1989) were **called** for January 4, 1993 redemption. ([[1992]])
- **1993** — Operating acquisition: **[[Dexter Shoe]]** (closed November 7), bought from
  founders **[[Harold Alfond]]** and **[[Peter Lunder]]** for **25,203 Berkshire shares**
  (~$478M with the Jan debenture conversion) — Berkshire's third shoe business after
  [[H. H. Brown]] and [[Lowell Shoe]], and a rare **stock-funded** purchase (later Buffett's
  most-regretted deal; see [[Mistakes Ledger]], [[Issuing Shares for Acquisitions]]).
  *Partial sale:* **one-third of [[Capital Cities]]** (1M shares) sold at **$630** (cost
  $172.50), a $297M after-tax gain — a rare trim of a major holding. Added to [[Wells Fargo]]
  (to ~12.2%); [[General Dynamics]] returned large amounts of capital (cost basis cut from
  $312M to $95M). ([[1993]])
- **1994** — A quiet year for whole-business deals: only **one minor acquisition** (a "small
  retail shoe chain"). The action was in **marketable stocks** — Berkshire enlarged
  **[[Coca-Cola]]** (to 100M shares) and **[[American Express]]** (to ~10%, ~$1.36B cost,
  combining the converted 1991 PERC with open-market buying) and added new positions in
  **[[Gannett]]** and **[[PNC Bank]]**; **[[GEICO]]** ownership crossed **50%**. *Sold:* most of
  **[[General Dynamics]]** (off the year-end list) and (the prior year) Cap Cities shares that
  kept rising — Buffett's "silver medal" mistake. **[[USAir]]** preferred written down to
  $89.5M after its dividend was suspended. Governance housekeeping: shareholders asked to
  **authorize preferred stock** for future acquisitions (prompted by a failed family-business
  merger). ([[1994]])
- **1995** — A landmark acquisition year. **[[GEICO]]** taken to **100%** ($2.3 billion for the
  remaining ~50%, closing just after year-end) — the 7th-largest U.S. auto insurer, run by
  **[[Tony Nicely]]** and **[[Lou Simpson]]**. Two retail "ABWA" deals via stock exchange:
  **[[Helzberg's Diamond Shops]]** (134 jewelry stores; CEO [[Jeff Comment]]; sourced by a
  street-corner meeting with [[Barnett Helzberg]]) and **[[R.C. Willey Home Furnishings]]** (Utah
  market leader; CEO [[Bill Child]]; sourced by Irv Blumkin of NFM). Together the three roughly
  **double Berkshire's revenues**. *Portfolio:* **[[Capital Cities]]** exchanged for **[[Disney]]**
  stock + cash (early 1996); [[American Express]] increased to ~10%. *Preferreds wound down:*
  [[Champion International]] called/converted, [[Salomon]] partial redemption ($140M), [[First Empire State]] to be called 3/31/96 (convert & keep). Corporate: the **[[Class B Shares]]**
  recapitalization proposed. ([[1995]])
- **1996** — Two operating acquisitions "both possessing exactly the qualities we seek." The large
  one: **[[FlightSafety International]]** (~$1.5 billion; world leader in pilot training; founder
  **[[Al Ueltschi]]**, 79) — Berkshire's **first capital-intensive business**, structured to
  minimize share issuance (51% cash / 41% A / 8% B), sourced by a shareholder's tip relayed through
  [[Salomon]]. The small one: **[[Kansas Bankers Surety]]** ($75M; manager **[[Don Towle]]**),
  placed with **[[Wesco Financial|Wesco]]**. Corporate: **[[GEICO]]** consolidated as a 100%
  subsidiary (Jan 2, 1996; $478.4M step-acquisition writedown); the early-1996 [[Capital Cities]]→[[Disney]] exchange completed (Disney now an itemized $1.72B holding). *Portfolio:*
  new position in **[[McDonald's]]** ($1.27B cost); [[Coca-Cola]] split 2:1 (to 200M sh); all
  major holdings otherwise untouched ("we make more money snoring than when active"). Corporate
  finance: **517,500 [[Class B Shares|Class B]] shares** sold ($565M) and a **$500M [[Salomon]]-
  exchangeable note** issued. ([[1996]])
- **1997** — Two operating acquisitions agreed, both via the "**any more at home like you?**"
  referral chain and both meeting the criteria. **[[Star Furniture]]** (Houston; CEO **[[Melvyn Wolff]]** and sister Shirley Toomin; **closed July 1, 1997**) — sourced through Blumkins → R.C.
  Willey ([[Bill Child]]) → Star. **[[International Dairy Queen]]** (5,792 DQ stores + Orange Julius
  + Karmelkorn; John Mooty, CEO Mike Sullivan; **closed early 1998**) — FlightSafety-style cash-or-
  stock terms, 45% taking cash. *Portfolio:* the **[[Salomon]]→[[Travelers Group]]** merger turned
  the Salomon stake into 23.7M Travelers shares; **net portfolio sales ~5%** (Disney and Wells Fargo
  trimmed) in a bond/stock realignment. *Unconventional:* **111.2M oz of silver**, **oil
  derivatives** (14.0M barrels remaining), and **$4.6B of long-term zero-coupon Treasuries** (see
  [[Unconventional Commitments]]). USAir preferred **called for redemption March 15, 1998** (now
  in-the-money). ([[1997]])
- **1998** — Two **elephants**, both larger than anything before. **[[General Re]]** (~**$22
  billion**, all stock; closed **Dec 21**) — 100% of the largest U.S. P&C reinsurer plus 82% of
  **Cologne Re**; CEO **[[Ron Ferguson]]**; bought for Berkshire's capital strength + volatility
  tolerance. **[[Executive Jet]]** / NetJets (**$725M**, equal cash and stock; closed **Aug 7**) —
  the fractional-jet leader founded by **[[Rich Santulli]]**; sourced via H.H. Brown's [[Frank Rooney]]. The stock issued for these deals inflated per-share *book* value (Berkshire trades above
  book) without a matching intrinsic-value gain. *Portfolio:* **sold [[McDonald's]]** ("a very big
  mistake"); slightly added [[American Express]]; **>$15B cash** at year-end awaiting the next
  elephant; Gen Re's ~250-stock equity portfolio liquidated on the merger ($935M tax). ([[1998]])
- **1999** — Two acquisitions, **both for cash, "issuing no Berkshire shares"** (the preferred
  method). **[[Jordan's Furniture]]** (New England's "shoppertainment" leader; Barry and Eliot
  **[[Tatelman Brothers|Tatelman]]**; closed **Nov 13**) — the **fourth** furniture chain via the
  Blumkin/Child/Wolff referral chain (now #1 in furniture across NE, UT, ID, TX, MA, NH). And a ~**$2
  billion** stake in **[[MidAmerican Energy]]** — Berkshire's **first electric utility**, sourced by
  director **[[Walter Scott Jr]]**, structured around PUHCA to hold ~**76% of the equity but <10%
  of the vote** (CEO **[[David Sokol]]**); Buffett signals "the amounts involved could be large" for
  future utility deals. *Portfolio:* few changes — Buffett "feel[s] much better about the businesses
  than their stocks" and stays out of tech; with the A shares below $45,000 he **weighs (but defers)
  a buyback**. ([[1999]])
- **2000** — **Berkshire's biggest single-year acquisition haul: eight businesses, ~$8B, 97% cash**
  (shares +⅓ of 1%) — Buffett's "brick, carpet, insulation and paint" year. **[[MidAmerican Energy]]** (76%) closed; new deals: **[[CORT Business Services]]** (~$386M cash → [[Wesco Financial|Wesco]]; rent-to-rent furniture), **[[U.S. Liability]]** (excess & surplus lines;
  half-stock/half-cash; via [[Ron Ferguson]]), **[[Ben Bridge Jeweler]]** (West Coast; half/half;
  via [[Barnett Helzberg]]), **[[Justin Industries]]** (~$570M cash; Western boots + **Acme Brick**),
  **[[Shaw Industries]]** (87.3%; world's largest carpet maker; Berkshire's largest non-insurance
  business), **[[Benjamin Moore]]** ($1B cash; paint), and **[[Johns Manville]]** (~$1.8B all-cash;
  insulation — after the LBO buyer's financing fell through). Tailwinds: sellers feared slowdowns and
  the **junk-bond market dried up**, hobbling leveraged bidders. *Portfolio:* **sold nearly all
  [[Freddie Mac]] & [[Fannie Mae]]**; took **15% positions in several mid-sized companies**; added
  high-yield and mortgage-backed bonds. ([[2000]])
- **2001** — **All-cash again.** The 2000-initiated **[[Shaw Industries]]** and **[[Johns Manville]]** deals closed (Shaw's 12.7% minority then bought for 4,740 A-share equivalents →
  **100%**). Four new businesses: **[[MiTek]]** (connector plates for roof trusses; the "55
  managers buy 10%" deal), **[[XTRA]]** (truck-trailer leasing, from Julian Robertson's Tiger Fund
  liquidation), **[[Larson-Juhl]]** (custom picture frames; Craig Ponzio; closed early 2002), and
  **[[Fruit of the Loom]]** (bought out of bankruptcy via its debt, conditioned on John Holland
  staying CEO). A bolt-on: **[[H. H. Brown]]** bought Acme Boot's inventory and trademarks ($700K).
  *Distressed debt:* the **Berkadia/[[FINOVA Group]]** workout with **[[Leucadia National]]** ($5.6B,
  90% Berkshire-guaranteed). *Portfolio:* quiet — new disclosed positions **[[Moody's]]** (24M sh)
  and **[[H&R Block]]** (16M sh); no group felt undervalued. ([[2001]])
- **2002** — Two 2001-pending deals closed (**[[Larson-Juhl]]**/Albecca, **[[Fruit of the Loom]]**).
  Three new operating businesses: **[[CTB]]** (poultry/hog/egg/grain equipment; Vic Mancinelli),
  **[[Garan]]** (children's apparel/Garanimals; Seymour Lichtenstein), and the year's largest,
  **[[The Pampered Chef]]** (direct-sales kitchenware; **[[Doris Christopher]]**; $700M sales).
  Via **[[MidAmerican Energy]]** (Berkshire equity → **80.2%**): two **gas pipelines** — **Kern
  River** and **[[Northern Natural Gas]]** (16,600 mi, from a failing Dynegy/ex-Enron) — taking MEHC
  to ~8% of U.S. gas transport. **[[HomeServices of America]]** doubled to $37B of transactions
  (Prudential California Realty the largest add-on). *Portfolio:* new **[[M&T Bank]]** position
  ([[Bob Wilmers]]); **junk bonds/loans sextupled to $8.3B**. *Exiting:* **[[General Re Securities]]**
  derivatives book wound down (see [[Derivatives]]). ([[2002]])
- **2003** — Two large acquisitions. **[[Clayton Homes]]** (manufactured-housing leader; sourced via
  University of Tennessee students giving Buffett **[[Jim Clayton]]**'s autobiography; CEO **[[Kevin Clayton]]**; Clayton then bought bankrupt rival Oakwood's assets). **[[McLane]]** (**$23B** sales
  grocery/non-food distributor, bought from **Wal-Mart**; sourced by **[[Byron Trott]]**; handshake,
  no due diligence). Shaw bolt-on: **Dixie Group** carpet ops. *Portfolio:* new **[[PetroChina]]**
  (1.3%, $488M cost) and HCA; Wells Fargo added; **~$12B of foreign-currency contracts** (the
  dollar bet — see [[Trade Deficit and the Dollar]]). *Wound down:* Berkadia/[[FINOVA Group]]
  guarantee extinguished; junk bonds now "unattractive." ([[2003]])
- **2004** — **"I struck out."** No major acquisitions; year-end cash swelled to ~**$43B**. Bolt-ons
  only: **[[Clayton Homes]]** completed its **Oakwood Homes** purchase (now the industry's largest)
  and **[[Shaw Industries]]** added Dixie Group's carpet operations. *Portfolio:* new
  **[[White Mountains Insurance]]** stake (~16%; the [[Jack Byrne]] connection); the dollar/FX
  position grew to ~**$21.4B** (see [[Trade Deficit and the Dollar]]). ([[2004]])
- **2005** — **Five acquisitions, none for stock.** **[[Medical Protective]]** (medical-malpractice
  insurer), **[[Forest River]]** (RV maker; Pete Liegl's two-page fax), **[[Business Wire]]** (Cathy
  Baron Tamraz's letter), **[[Applied Underwriters]]** (81%; payroll + workers' comp, via Ajit Jain),
  and — via **[[MidAmerican Energy]]** — **[[PacifiCorp]]** (six-state Western utility, ~$5.1B; "we'll
  be looking for more"). Bolt-ons: Shaw (~$550M, vertical integration), XTRA, **[[Clayton Homes]]**
  (Karsten). *Portfolio:* Gillette → **[[Procter & Gamble]]** in the merger; Amex spun off
  **[[Ameriprise Financial]]**; new **[[Anheuser-Busch]]** and **[[Wal-Mart]]** stakes; Wells Fargo
  increased. ([[2005]])
- **2006** — **Berkshire goes global.** The three 2005 deals closed (PacifiCorp, Business Wire,
  Applied Underwriters, ~$6B). **[[ISCAR]]** — **80% for $4B**, the **first non-U.S. acquisition**
  (Israel; Eitan Wertheimer's letter; family keeps 20%). **[[TTI]]** (Fort Worth electronic-components
  distributor; Paul Andrews Jr.). The **[[Equitas]]** retroactive reinsurance of Lloyd's pre-1993
  liabilities ($7.12B premium / $13.9B cover). Tuck-ins: Fruit bought **[[Russell Athletic|Russell]]**
  (~$1.2B) + VF intimates; MiTek (14 bolt-ons), CTB, Shaw, Clayton. *Portfolio:* new
  **[[ConocoPhillips]]**, **[[Johnson & Johnson]]**, **[[POSCO]]**, **[[Tesco]]**, **[[US Bancorp]]**,
  **[[USG Corporation]]** (19%); direct FX bet **nearly wound down** (+$2.2B cumulative). ([[2006]])
- **2007** — The **[[Marmon Group]]** (Christmas Day; **60% for $4.5B**, rest within six years —
  Berkshire's largest cash purchase yet; the Pritzker family; Frank Ptak; Byron Trott) and
  **[[Richline Group]]** (jewelry supplier; Dennis Ulrich). **[[BoatU.S.]]** added to the smaller
  insurers. *Portfolio:* big new stakes in **[[Burlington Northern Santa Fe]]** (17.5%) and
  **[[Kraft Foods]]** (8.1%), plus **[[Sanofi-Aventis]]**; **[[PetroChina]] sold** for **$4B** (~$3.5B
  gain). One direct currency position: the **Brazilian real**. ([[2007]])
- **2008** — **Crisis deployment, not whole-business buying.** With markets in "disarray" (a
  "tailwind in our purchases"), Berkshire made **$14.5B of fixed-income-plus-equity commitments "on
  terms unavailable in normal markets"**: **[[Goldman Sachs]]** ($5B of 10% perpetual preferred +
  warrants), **[[General Electric]]** ($3B, same structure), and **$6.5B into the Mars/[[Wrigley]]**
  buyout. The **[[Marmon Group]]** purchase closed (Berkshire → **64%**, buying the rest over six
  years). Via **[[MidAmerican Energy]]**, the **[[Constellation Energy]]** rescue broke up at a
  profit (+$1,092M). **[[ISCAR]]** bought **Tungaloy** (Japan); tuck-ins at MiTek, Benjamin Moore,
  Acme Brick, Forest River, Marmon and CTB. New: **[[Berkshire Hathaway Assurance|BHAC]]** (muni
  bond insurer) activated, and a **10% stake in [[BYD]]** (China). *Portfolio:* new **[[Swiss Re]]**
  stake; **[[Moody's]]** and **[[Burlington Northern Santa Fe|BNSF]]** crossed 20% (equity
  accounting); sold parts of [[Johnson & Johnson]], [[Procter & Gamble]], [[ConocoPhillips]] to fund
  the crisis deals. ([[2008]])
- **2009** — **The biggest deal yet: [[Burlington Northern Santa Fe]].** Berkshire agreed to buy the
  **~77.5% of BNSF it didn't already own for ~$26B** (an "all-in wager on the economic future of the
  United States"; CEO **[[Matt Rose]]**), paying ~70% cash / 30% stock (issuing ~95,000 shares,
  6.1%) — "**Charlie and I enjoy issuing Berkshire stock about as much as we relish prepping for a
  colonoscopy**." BNSF closes February 2010 and anchors a new capital-intensive "regulated utility &
  railroad" sector. Also: a **$3B [[Dow Chemical]]** convertible preferred (funding its Rohm & Haas
  deal — the fifth crisis-era non-traded security with Dow/GE/Goldman/Swiss Re/Wrigley), and a **50%
  stake in [[Berkadia Commercial Mortgage]]** (ex-Capmark, with [[Leucadia National]] — "Son of
  Berkadia"). Small bolt-ons for cash. *Portfolio:* largest **sales** were [[ConocoPhillips]],
  [[Moody's]], [[Procter & Gamble]], [[Johnson & Johnson]] (to raise BNSF/Dow/Swiss Re cash); **BYD**
  surged. ([[2009]])
- **2010** — **[[Burlington Northern Santa Fe|BNSF]] closes (February)** — Berkshire's largest
  acquisition, anchoring a new "regulated & capital-intensive" sector with [[MidAmerican Energy]].
  **[[Marmon Group]]** taken toward **80%** (scheduled $1.5B purchase of 17% from the Pritzkers; rest
  in 2013/2014). Bolt-ons across the construction businesses (MiTek ×5, Acme's Alabama brick maker
  ~$50M) and **[[McLane]]'s entry into wine & spirits** (Empire Distributors, then Horizon). *No new
  whole-company elephant* ("our elephant gun has been reloaded… my trigger finger is itchy").
  *Portfolio:* new **[[Munich Re]]** stake (10.5%); the crisis non-traded securities ([[Swiss Re]],
  [[Goldman Sachs]], [[General Electric]]) headed for redemption. **[[Lou Simpson]]** retired;
  **[[Todd Combs]]** hired as a new investment manager. ([[2010]])
- **2011** — **[[Lubrizol]]** acquired (~$9B, **closed September 16**) — a worldwide specialty-chemicals
  maker run by **[[James Hambrick]]**; three bolt-ons ($493M) immediately agreed. **[[Marmon Group]]**
  taken to **80%** (the contractual 16% bought for $1.5B, with a "meaningless" $614M accounting
  write-off). Year-end bolt-on **Princeton Insurance** (med-mal, NJ; under [[Medical Protective]]).
  *Marketable-security moves:* **[[IBM]]** ($10.9B, 5.5% — Buffett's first major tech stock),
  **[[Bank of America]]** ($5B preferred + warrants for 700M shares at $7.14), and a $1B add to
  **[[Wells Fargo]]**. *Called away:* the **[[Swiss Re]]/[[Goldman Sachs]]/[[General Electric]]**
  crisis securities ($12.8B). Berkshire announced its **first buyback authorization** (≤110% of book).
  **[[Ted Weschler]]** joins [[Todd Combs]] as an investment manager. ([[2011]])
- **2012** — **No "elephant"** (Buffett "came up empty-handed") but a **record bolt-on year**: **$2.3B
  for 26 companies**, no shares issued. Late in the year, **Guard Insurance** (workers' comp) and the
  rebranding push at **[[HomeServices of America]]** (bought 67% of the Prudential/Real Living
  franchises). Over fifteen months Berkshire bought **28 daily newspapers for $344M** (see [[Evolving Views — Newspapers]]). *Announced (early 2013):* the **$12B [[H.J. Heinz]]** deal with **[[Jorge Paulo Lemann|3G Capital]]** (50/50; ~$4B common + $8B of 9% preferred + warrants). *Portfolio:* new
  itemized holdings **DIRECTV** (the first [[Todd Combs]]/[[Ted Weschler]] pick to cross $1B) and
  **Phillips 66** (via the [[ConocoPhillips]] spin-off); added to [[Wells Fargo]] and [[IBM]]. ([[2012]])
- **2013** — **~$18B of acquisitions.** **All of [[NV Energy]]** ($5.6B, via [[MidAmerican Energy]];
  88% of Nevada) and **a major interest in [[H.J. Heinz]]** (closed June; $8B preferred + $4.25B
  common, partnered 50/50 with **[[Jorge Paulo Lemann|3G Capital]]**). **$3.5B** spent taking
  **[[Marmon Group]]** (to 100%) and **[[ISCAR]]** (Wertheimer family's 20% put) to full ownership —
  +$300M pre-tax, +$800M cash, plus a "nonsensical" $1.8B accounting write-down. **25 bolt-ons** for
  **$3.1B** ($1.9M to $1.1B each). **[[Berkshire Hathaway Specialty Insurance|BHSI]]** founded (June)
  — Berkshire's move into commercial insurance. *Portfolio:* Big Four ownership all increased; no big
  new stock position. ([[2013]])
- **2014** — **A record bolt-on year: 31 deals for $7.8B**, the largest being **[[Duracell]]** (bought
  from **[[Procter & Gamble]]** via a tax-efficient stock exchange; closes 2H 2015; placed under
  Marmon). **[[Van Tuyl Automotive]]** (78 dealerships, $9B sales, #5 U.S. auto group) → Berkshire
  Hathaway Automotive. Via BHE: **[[AltaLink]]** (Alberta electric transmission, C$3B). *Portfolio:*
  Buffett **swapped Phillips 66 and Graham Holdings stock for operating businesses** (ending the
  Graham Holdings/[[Washington Post]] round-trip); Big Four ownership all rose. 3G/Mars/Leucadia
  partnerships flagged for future deals (Berkshire financed Burger King's Tim Hortons acquisition).
  ([[2014]])
- **2015** — **[[Precision Castparts]]** — agreed for **>$32 billion of cash**, **Berkshire's largest
  acquisition ever** (closes early 2016 → "Powerhouse Six"); aerospace-components leader under Mark
  Donegan, sourced by [[Todd Combs]]. The **[[H.J. Heinz]]** partnership **merged with [[Kraft Foods| Kraft]] → [[Kraft Heinz]]** (Berkshire ~27%, cost $9.8B, + $8B preferred). **29 bolt-ons** for $634M.
  *Portfolio:* Big Four ownership all rose again (Amex 15.6%, Coke 9.3%, IBM 8.4%, WFC 9.8%); Combs and
  Weschler each now manage ~$9B. ([[2015]])
- **2016** — **No "elephant"; the action is in marketable stocks.** **[[Precision Castparts]]** (~$32B)
  and **[[Duracell]]** *closed* (both 2014/2015 deals), adding a first full earnings year in 2017. The
  year's marquee moves were **portfolio** purchases marking two reversals of long-held aversions: a major
  new stake in **[[Apple]]** (61.2M shares, 1.1%, ~$6.7B cost — Berkshire's first big technology-hardware
  holding) and a basket across **all four major airlines** (**[[Delta Air Lines|Delta]]**, **[[Southwest Airlines|Southwest]]**, **[[United Continental Holdings|United Continental]]**, **[[American Airlines|American]]**; see [[Evolving Views — Airlines]]), plus a new **[[Charter Communications]]**
  position. **[[Bank of America]]**'s warrant (700M shares) sat **$10.5B in the money**. **[[Clayton Homes]]** continued bolt-ons (site-builders); **[[MidAmerican Energy|HomeServices]]** bought four
  realtors (incl. Houlihan Lawrence). At [[General Re]], **[[Tad Montross]] retired** ([[Kara Raiguel]]
  succeeding). Big Four ownership: Amex 16.8%, Coke 9.3%, IBM 8.5%, WFC 10.0%; **[[Todd Combs]]** and
  **[[Ted Weschler]]** each now manage **>$10B**. ([[2016]])
- **2017** — **An acquisition drought (prices at an "all-time high") with one stand-alone deal.** Berkshire
  bought a **38.6% partnership interest in [[Pilot Flying J]]** (the Haslam family's travel-center leader,
  ~$20B volume; contracted to **80% in 2023**). **Bolt-ons:** [[Clayton Homes]] bought two home builders
  (**Oakwood Homes**, **Harris Doyle**); [[Shaw Industries]] bought **U.S. Floors** (→ $5.7B sales);
  **[[HomeServices of America]]** bought **Long and Foster** (#3), **Houlihan Lawrence**, and **Gloria
  Nilson** (→ 40,950 agents, $127B of sides); [[Precision Castparts]] bought Germany's **Wilhelm Schulz**.
  *Portfolio:* **[[Bank of America]]** warrant **exercised** → 700M common shares (6.8%); **[[Apple]]**
  built ~4× to a top-three holding (3.3%); new itemized **[[Bank of New York Mellon]]** and **[[General Motors]]**; **[[BYD]]** entered the top 15. **Cash $116B**; the **record $10.2B [[AIG retroactive reinsurance|AIG]]** reinsurance written. *(Corporate: [[Ajit Jain]] & [[Greg Abel]] named Vice Chairmen,
  early 2018.)* ([[2017]])
- **2018** — **No acquisitions of note; the year of stock-buying.** "Prices are sky-high for businesses
  possessing decent long-term prospects," so Berkshire bought **~$43B of marketable equities** (selling
  only $19B): **[[Apple]]** built to the **#1 holding** (5.4%, $40B), **[[Bank of America]]** to 918.9M
  shares (9.5%), and a new **[[JPMorgan Chase]]** stake (~$5.6B). The board **scrapped the 120%-of-book
  buyback cap** (see [[Evolving Views — Buybacks]]). Bolt-ons only. *Corporate:* **[[Tony Nicely]] retired**
  as GEICO CEO ([[Bill Roberts]] succeeding); **[[Kraft Heinz]]** took a large intangibles write-off
  (Berkshire's ~$3B share). The five jointly-controlled/grove framework introduced. ([[2018]])
- **2019** — **"We found no companies of size to purchase."** No whole-business acquisitions; prices too
  high. The marquee deployment was a **$10 billion financing of [[Occidental Petroleum]]** (8% preferred +
  warrants, to fund its Anadarko bid). New equity stake: **[[Visa]]**. Berkshire repurchased **$5 billion**
  of its own stock (~1% — its largest buyback to date; see [[Evolving Views — Buybacks]]). **[[Apple]]**
  swelled to ~30% of the $248B portfolio; **[[Kraft Heinz]]** fell below cost. *Corporate:* Buffett
  disclosed his **will** and named Berkshire "100% prepared" for the founders' exit; **[[Ajit Jain]]** and
  **[[Greg Abel]]** to take more AGM questions. ([[2019]])
- **2020** — **The COVID year: no acquisitions, record buybacks, big portfolio turnover.** Berkshire made
  **no sizable acquisitions** and repurchased a **record $24.7B (5.2%)** of its own stock. *Portfolio:*
  **sold all four airlines** at a loss in the travel collapse ([[Evolving Views — Airlines]]) and exited
  most banks (**Wells Fargo, JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs**), while **adding [[Bank of America]]** and opening
  new positions in **[[Chevron]]**, **[[Verizon Communications|Verizon]]**, a **pharma basket** (AbbVie,
  Merck, Bristol-Myers, Pfizer), and **~5% stakes in the five [[Japanese Trading Houses]]** (yen-funded).
  The **$10B [[Occidental Petroleum]]** preferred was marked down to $9B. **[[Precision Castparts]]** took
  an ~$11B write-down. *Corporate:* **[[Carl Ice]]→[[Katie Farmer]]** as BNSF CEO; the "Big Four" /
  five-buckets / "$154B of PP&E" framings introduced. ([[2020]])
- **2021** — **"Little action" — repurchases over acquisitions.** No notable acquisitions; Buffett found
  "little that excites us" as low rates pushed all asset prices up. Berkshire instead repurchased **9% of
  its shares over 2020–21 for $51.7B** (+$1.2B more by Feb 2022). *Portfolio:* mostly steady — **[[Apple]]**
  rose to 5.55% on its own buybacks; the **[[Japanese Trading Houses]]** (Itochu/Mitsubishi/Mitsui) became
  itemized top-15 holdings; **[[Chevron]]** and the **[[Occidental Petroleum]]** preferred held. Confirmed
  **[[Pilot Flying J|Pilot]]** would go to **80% early 2023**. *Corporate:* tribute to the late **[[Paul Andrews Jr|Paul Andrews]]** (TTI) — and the reminder that TTI's 2009 Fort Worth board visit had led to
  the BNSF acquisition. ([[2021]])
- **2022** — **[[Alleghany Corporation]]** acquired (~$11.6B cash) — a P/C insurer/reinsurer under
  returning CEO **[[Joe Brandon]]**, Berkshire's biggest deal since Precision Castparts; float rose to
  **$164B**. *Portfolio:* massively enlarged **[[Chevron]]** (a top-five holding) and **[[Occidental Petroleum]]** common (passing ~20% → equity-method; Berkshire its largest owner); new positions in
  **[[HP Inc]]** and **[[Paramount Global]]** (Berkshire the largest owner of each). Buybacks **1.2%** of
  shares. Confirmed **[[Pilot Flying J|Pilot]] → 80%** (closing Jan 2023). ([[2022]])
- **2023** — **No acquisitions of size; positions added.** With Berkshire's $561B net worth ~6% of the S&P
  500's total, Buffett notes "no possibility of eye-popping performance." Berkshire *increased* two
  "indefinite" holdings — **[[Occidental Petroleum]]** (to **27.8%** + warrants; no control sought) and the
  five **[[Japanese Trading Houses]]** (to **~9% each**, after a Tokyo trip with **[[Greg Abel]]**;
  yen-bond financed) — while leaving [[Coca-Cola]] and [[American Express]] untouched. **[[Pilot Flying J| Pilot]]** consolidated at **80%** (Jan 2023; Berkshire later went to 100%). *Corporate:* **[[Charlie Munger]] died** (Nov 28, 2023); Abel affirmed "ready to be CEO… tomorrow." ([[2023]])
- **2024** — **A net seller; one control purchase.** No outside acquisition of size, but Berkshire bought
  in the **[[MidAmerican Energy|BHE]]** minority to reach **100%** (~$3.9B). In the market it was a heavy
  **net seller** — marketable equities fell **$354B → $272B** (large **[[Apple]]** and **[[Bank of America]]** sales), lifting cash/T-bills to a record. The five **[[Japanese Trading Houses]]** had their
  9.9% ceiling relaxed (ownership to rise; held "for many decades"). *Corporate:* **[[Pete Liegl]]**
  ([[Forest River]]) died; Buffett affirmed **[[Greg Abel]]** would soon become CEO and write the letters.
  ([[2024]])
