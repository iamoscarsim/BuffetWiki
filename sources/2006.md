# 2006 Shareholder Letter

## Summary

A **record year**: net worth grew **$16.9 billion** — "a record for a one-year gain… more than has
ever been booked by any American business" (ex-mergers) — lifting per-share book value **18.4%** to
**$70,281** (42 years, 21.4% compounded). Buffett's "confession": insurance "benefited from a large
dose of luck: **Mother Nature… went on vacation**," so the super-cat red ink of 2004–05 "turned
black — very black." Berkshire now has **217,000 employees** and revenues approaching **$100
billion**. The standout was again **[[GEICO]]**: policies grew **5.7M → 8.1M (+42%)** from 2003 while
headcount fell 3.5% (**+47% productivity**), funding ad spend of **$631M** (from $31M in 1995). Don
Keough's quip: shareholders should "**immediately change the names of their present children to Tony
or Antoinette**."

**Acquisitions — going global.** The three 2005-pending deals closed (PacifiCorp, [[Business Wire]],
[[Applied Underwriters]], ~$6B). The highlight was Berkshire's **first non-U.S. acquisition**:
**80% of [[ISCAR]]**, the Israeli cutting-tools maker, for **$4 billion** — sourced by a 1¼-page
letter from chairman **[[Eitan Wertheimer]]** (CEO Jacob Harpaz; the Wertheimer family keeps 20%).
Also **[[TTI]]**, a Fort Worth electronic-components distributor ($112K → $1.3B of sales under
**[[Paul Andrews Jr]]**; brought by John Roach of ex-[[Justin Industries]] fame; "a deal before
lunch"). Tuck-ins: **Fruit of the Loom** bought **[[Russell Athletic|Russell Corp]]** (~$1.2B
athletic apparel) and VF's intimate-apparel unit (together +$2.2B sales, 23,000 employees); MiTek
(now 14 bolt-ons), CTB, Shaw, Clayton. Still hunting **elephants**.

**Insurance — the 40th anniversary, and Equitas.** On the 40th anniversary of buying NICO from Jack
Ringwalt ($8.6M; the "expired parking meter" handshake), float reached **$50.9 billion**, plus
**$7 billion** from the year's landmark deal: a huge **retroactive reinsurance** policy for
**[[Equitas]]** (the vehicle holding pre-1993 **[[Lloyd's of London|Lloyd's]]** liabilities for
27,972 "names"). Berkshire took **$7.12B** of cash/securities to pay claims up to **$13.9B** — "the
world's best mattress" (Scott Moser). The DCRA accounting means retroactive deals "always produce
underwriting losses," now ~**$450M/yr**, "a hurdle… I believe we will clear in many, if not most,
years." Everything "went right" — a **$3,838M underwriting profit** (Gen Re +$526M, B-H Reinsurance
+$1,658M, GEICO +$1,314M, Other Primary +$340M): "you won't soon see another [view] like it."
Buffett is "prepared to lose **$6 billion** in a single event" if priced right, but **sharply reduced
wind exposure** as capital flooded super-cat — and warns Katrina is "anything close to a worst-case
event" amid climate uncertainty.

**Manufacturing/Service/Retail.** Revenues **$52,660M**; **25%** on tangible net worth / 10.8% on
carrying value. **Bob Shaw retired** (75) at Shaw, succeeded by **[[Vance Bell]]**; housing weakness
slowed carpet. **MiTek** is "developing into a mini-conglomerate" (its stock $10,000 → $71,699). The
**[[Buffalo Evening News|Buffalo News]]/newspaper-decline essay** (see [[Evolving Views — Newspapers]]):
"Survival of the Fattest" gave way to structural decline — "**the days of lush profits from our
newspaper are over**" — though Berkshire will stick with it (Stan Lipsey; editor Margaret Sullivan).
**NetJets ([[Executive Jet]]) turned around**: worldwide pre-tax **$143M**, with **Europe finally
profitable** under Mark Booth (Rich Santulli's turnaround).

**Regulated utilities ([[MidAmerican Energy]], 86.6%).** Added **PacifiCorp** (Pacific Power + Rocky
Mountain Power, ~1.7M customers). **[[HomeServices of America]]** volume **fell 9% to $58B** (profits
−50%) as the housing slowdown hit — Buffett diagnoses the coming subprime reckoning ("a rolling loan
gathers no loss"; the "Scarlett O'Hara" / teaser-rate trap), while still seeking more brokerage
deals.

**Finance.** The **last word on [[General Re Securities]]**: down to 197 contracts, cumulative loss
**−$409M** (only $5M in 2006) — "All's well that ends." Value Capital wound up. **[[Clayton Homes]]**
earned **$513M** pre-tax (+ an $86M financing fee; $10B receivables) in an industry at 1962-low
volumes. Leasing (CORT/Paul Arnold, XTRA/Bill Franz) solid.

**Investments.** Common stocks cost $22,995M / market **$61,533M**. New positions:
**[[ConocoPhillips]]**, **[[Johnson & Johnson]]**, **[[POSCO]]** (Korean steel), **[[Tesco]]** (UK
retail), **[[US Bancorp]]**, **[[USG Corporation]]** (19%); plus two undisclosed ($1.9B, "still
buying… then I would have to kill you"). The largest holdings' per-share earnings rose 18%/9%/8%/11%
(Amex/Coke/P&G/Wells Fargo). The **direct foreign-currency position is nearly closed** (+$186M in
2006; **+$2.2B cumulative** since 2002), with Buffett now seeking currency exposure through **foreign
equities** instead (the spread turned negative in 2005); the trade-deficit "reverse compounding"
warning deepens (the U.S. "investment income" account went negative for the first time since 1915).
Buffett notes he **personally writes derivatives** — 62 contracts, no counterparty credit risk,
"hundreds of millions" in profits from mispricing (foreshadowing the equity-index puts).

**Succession.** Three CEO candidates are ready; but "we are **not as well-prepared on the investment
side**" — Lou Simpson is only six years younger — so Berkshire will **hire a younger CIO candidate
(or several)** "genetically programmed to recognize and avoid serious risks." Buffett, 76, "feel[s]
terrific… It's amazing what Cherry Coke and hamburgers will do."

## Key figures

- **Book value/share:** $70,281, **+18.4%** (net worth **+$16.9B**, a U.S. record); 42 years,
  **21.4% compounded**. Per-share investments **$80,636**; non-insurance pre-tax EPS **$3,625**
  (+38%).
- **Insurance:** **underwriting profit $3,838M** (Gen Re +$526M / B-H Reins +$1,658M / GEICO
  +$1,314M / Other Primary +$340M); float **$50,887M** + ~$7B Equitas. **Equitas:** $7.12B premium,
  $13.9B cover. GEICO policies 8.1M (+42% over 3 yrs); ads $631M.
- **ISCAR:** 80% for **$4B** (first foreign acquisition). **TTI** acquired. Fruit bought **Russell**
  (~$1.2B) + VF intimates.
- **Mfg/Svc/Retail:** revenues **$52,660M**, **25%** on tangible net worth. **Clayton** $513M
  pre-tax. **NetJets +$143M** (Europe profitable). **Gen Re Securities** wound down (cum. −$409M).
- **Common stocks:** cost $22,995M / market **$61,533M** (Amex $9,198M, Coca-Cola $9,650M, Wells
  Fargo $7,758M, P&G $6,427M, Moody's $3,315M, PetroChina $3,313M, WaPo $1,288M; new ConocoPhillips
  $1,291M, J&J $1,409M, POSCO $1,158M, Tesco $1,820M, US Bancorp $1,123M, USG $936M).
- **FX:** direct position nearly closed; **+$2.2B** cumulative since 2002. Foreign-exchange profit
  $186M in 2006.
