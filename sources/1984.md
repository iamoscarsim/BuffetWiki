# 1984 Shareholder Letter

## Summary

Written February 25, 1985. Net worth grew $152.6M ($133/share), but only **13.6%**
— "mediocre" against Berkshire's 22.1% twenty-year record (book value now
$1,108.77). Buffett's "academic voice" warning about a mushrooming capital base is
"now giving way to a reportorial voice": earning even 15% over the next decade would
need ~$3.9 billion of profit and "a few big ideas — small ones just won't do."

Three major essays anchor the letter. **Buybacks & greenmail** (extending
[[Evolving Views — Buybacks]]): repurchases below intrinsic value get "$2 of present
value for $1" *and* signal a pro-shareholder management, raising the price/value
ratio the market grants — "the key word is 'demonstrated.'" Buffett condemns
**greenmail** as "odious and repugnant." He celebrates the buybacks of GEICO,
Washington Post, General Foods, and Exxon, and explains the GEICO/General Foods
**proportional redemptions** (taxed as dividends) and an accounting dispute with
Peat Marwick's New York office (no effect on intrinsic value).

The **loss-reserving** essay (see [[Loss Reserving]]) is a candid lesson on why a
P&C insurer's income statement is "only a first rough draft of earnings": IBNR,
delayed claims, the "rented suit" story, "the corpse is supposed to file the death
certificate," and "you can be broke but flush." Buffett publishes a table showing
Berkshire's own reserves were repeatedly *too optimistic* and apologizes for
misstating earnings (and overpaying taxes).

The **dividend-policy** essay (see [[Evolving Views — Dividends]]) distinguishes
**"restricted"** earnings (consumed by an asset-heavy business just to stand still —
Con Ed's "Dig We Must") from **"unrestricted"** earnings, which should be retained
*only* if each retained dollar will create at least a dollar of market value (the
perpetual-bond-coupon analogy). He skewers the "schizoid" CEO who demands payout
analyses from subsidiaries but not himself, and the "Pro-Am golf" effect where a
great core business camouflages disastrous capital allocation elsewhere: "if
earnings have been unwisely retained, it is likely that managers, too, have been
unwisely retained."

The **WPPSS bond** purchase ($139M, contractually earning $22.7M after tax, ~16.3%)
showcases Buffett's **bond-as-a-business** lens and his [[Concentration]] philosophy
(Billy Rose: "If you have a harem of forty women, you never get to know any of them
very well"), plus the principle of making "intelligent-but-with-some-chance-of-
looking-like-an-idiot" decisions because he and Munger answer as owners (47% of
stock), not employees. He cites Graham — "Investment is most intelligent when it is
most businesslike" — and reiterates deep distrust of long bonds given runaway-
inflation risk.

Operating: **[[Nebraska Furniture Mart]]** hit $115M sales from one store (Mrs. B,
91, got an honorary NYU doctorate; "sell cheap and tell the truth"; the $55M
handshake with no audit; Levitz comparison shows 16.5% vs 35.6% opex). **[[See's Candies]]**
held flat poundage with modest price rises. **[[Buffalo Evening News]]** was very
profitable; Buffett explains the economics of a **dominant newspaper** (even a
"third-rate" paper prospers if dominant — though quality earned that dominance),
seeding [[Evolving Views — Newspapers]]. Berkshire's own insurance combined ratio
was "a humbling **134**"; [[Financial Strength]] ("the day may have arrived") drove
growth in structured settlements via Columbia Insurance. A **subsequent event**:
Berkshire agreed to buy 3 million shares of **[[Capital Cities]]** at $172.50,
contingent on its ABC acquisition — backing Tom Murphy and Dan Burke.

## Key figures
- Net worth +$152.6M (+13.6%); book value/share **$1,108.77** (20 yrs, 22.1% comp.).
- Berkshire combined ratio **134**; industry 117.7.
- Total common stocks: cost **$585M**, market **$1,269M**. GEICO market $397M (36%).
- New/large holdings: Exxon ($175M, 4th largest), ABC ($47M), Northwest Industries;
  Washington Post $150M.
- NFM sales **$115M** (opex 16.5% vs Levitz 35.6%); See's per-lb realization $5.49.
- WPPSS bonds: $139M cost, $22.7M after-tax interest (~16.3%).
- Cap Cities (subsequent event): 3M shares at $172.50, contingent on ABC deal.
