# 2004 Shareholder Letter

## Summary

A "**lackluster**" year: net worth grew **$8.3 billion**, lifting per-share book value **10.5%** to
**$55,824** (40 years, 21.9% compounded) — but **falling short** of the S&P's 10.9% return (the
first such lag in years). Buffett pins it on himself, not the operating CEOs: "I didn't do that job
very well last year." He "struck out" on multi-billion-dollar acquisitions and "found very few
attractive securities," leaving Berkshire with **$43 billion of cash** — "not a happy position."
An aside on the market: over the 35 years since the 1960s, the S&P returned 11.2%/yr, yet only one
year before 2004 landed anywhere near "normal" (8–14%); investors' enemies are "**excitement and
expenses**" — "be fearful when others are greedy and greedy only when others are fearful."

**Regulated utilities ([[MidAmerican Energy]], 80.5%).** The portfolio (UK 3.7M customers, Iowa
698,000, Kern River + Northern Natural pipelines = 7.9% of U.S. gas) earned well, but a **$579M
write-off of a California zinc-recovery project** dominated. The lesson: "**stay with simple
propositions**" — if ten independent variables each have a 90% chance, success is only 35%; "look
for… **mono-linked chains**" (see [[Circle of Competence]]). A wry $72.2M gain on an Enron
receivable bundled with Northern Natural was claimed as Omaha's "**war reparations**." HomeServices
did **$59.8B** of transactions.

**Insurance — the NICO discipline essay.** Float reached **$46.1 billion** (from $20M in 1967).
Buffett devotes the section to *how* Berkshire beats a commodity industry ("we wait in vain for
'I'd like a National Indemnity policy, please'"):
- **The "Portrait of a Disciplined Underwriter."** A 25-year table of **[[National Indemnity Company]]** shows premium volume deliberately sliding from $366M (1986) to $55M (1999) — "we
  consistently priced to make a profit, not to match our most optimistic competitor. We never left
  customers — but they left us." The enabler is a **no-layoff promise** that removes employees'
  incentive to rationalize bad pricing (the [[Institutional Imperative]] resisted) and the [[Tom Murphy]] "an employee is a $3 million decision" discipline against overstaffing in good times.
  Star CEO **[[Don Wurster]]** ("the graduate") "will accept a walk rather than swing at a bad
  pitch," holding **$950M** of negative-cost float.
- **[[GEICO]]** — the low-cost route. A capsule history (Leo Goodwin's $200,000 start in 1936;
  State Farm's George Mecherle; the 1976 near-failure; [[Jack Byrne]]'s rescue; the diversification
  detour; **[[Tony Nicely]]** since 1993) ending with a projected **~6% market share in 2005**.
  GEICO **re-entered New Jersey** (commissioner Holly Bakke), reaching 140,000 policyholders fast.
  2004: ~$1B saved for customers, profit-sharing **$191M / 24.3%**, underwriting profit **$970M**.
- **Reinsurance.** Four Florida hurricanes (~$25B industry loss) cost Berkshire **$1.25B**, yet the
  reinsurance book did well: Gen Re (Joe Brandon's restored discipline, +$3M, offset by pre-Brandon
  adverse development) and [[Ajit Jain]] ("underwrites huge risks no other reinsurer will accept").
  Berkshire is prepared for a "$100 billion event" (its share 3–5%): "**Berkshire's checks will
  clear**."
- **Float scorecard:** total **$46,094M**, underwriting profit **$1,551M** (Gen Re $3M, B-H
  Reinsurance $417M, GEICO $970M, Other Primary $161M). "Paid more than $1.5B to hold ~$45.2B."

**Finance.** **[[General Re Securities]]** down to **2,890 contracts** (from 23,218 peak; −$44M).
**[[Clayton Homes]]** thrived counter-cyclically: Berkshire's relending grew **$2B → $7.35B** (at
cost +1%); receivables headed to ~$9B; Clayton **completed the Oakwood acquisition** (now the
industry's largest), employees 7,136 → 11,837; CEO **[[Kevin Clayton]]** "a prototype Berkshire
manager." Leasing (XTRA, CORT) rebounded; Berkadia "down to tag ends."

**Manufacturing/Service/Retail.** Revenues **$44,142M**; **21.7%** on tangible net worth / 9.9% on
carrying value, with "only minor financial leverage." MiTek absorbed a **100% steel-cost spike**
(Gene Toombs building "a mini-Berkshire"); Shaw a $300M fiber-cost hit yet **25.6%** on tangible
equity. Fruit unit sales +14%; Ben Bridge same-store +11.4%; R.C. Willey's Las Vegas stores (Bill
Child, Scott Hymas) confounded Buffett's skepticism. At **[[FlightSafety International]]**, founder
**[[Al Ueltschi]]** handed the CEO role to **[[Bruce Whitman]]** (ROTE up to 15.1%); **NetJets**
([[Executive Jet]]) U.S. contracts grew 3,877 → 4,967 (Marquis Jet's 25-hour card a factor) but
Europe still lost money.

**Investments & the Big Four.** Common stocks cost $9,056M / market **$37,717M**; new position
**[[White Mountains Insurance]]** ($1,114M, 16%). The signature essay: the **"Big Four"** — American
Express, Coca-Cola, Gillette, Wells Fargo — $3.83B invested (dollar-weighted mid-1992), now
throwing off **$1.2B** of look-through earnings (31.3% of cost) and $434M of dividends. The
**foreign-currency/dollar position grew** (to ~$21.4B), with the "**sharecropper society**" trade-
deficit warning expanded (see [[Trade Deficit and the Dollar]]).

## Key figures

- **Book value/share:** $55,824, **+10.5%** (net worth +$8.3B); 40 years, **21.9% compounded** —
  **lagged the S&P (10.9%)**. Year-end cash ~**$43B**.
- **Insurance float $46,094M**; **underwriting profit $1,551M** (Gen Re $3M / B-H Reins $417M /
  GEICO $970M / Other Primary $161M). GEICO profit-sharing $191M / 24.3%.
- **MidAmerican (80.5%):** EBIT $605M after a **−$579M zinc write-off**; $1.478B junior debt to
  Berkshire (paid down $100M). HomeServices $59.8B transactions.
- **Manufacturing/Service/Retail:** revenues **$44,142M**, **21.7%** on tangible net worth / 9.9%
  on carrying value.
- **Clayton:** relending $2B → **$7.35B**; Oakwood acquired; employees → 11,837.
- **Common stocks:** cost $9,056M / market **$37,717M** (Amex $8,546M, Coca-Cola $8,328M, Gillette
  $4,299M, Wells Fargo $3,508M, WaPo $1,698M, Moody's $2,084M, PetroChina $1,249M, **White Mountains
  $1,114M new**, M&T $723M, H&R Block $703M). **"Big Four"** look-through ~$1.2B. **FX ~$21.4B.**
