# 2012 Shareholder Letter

## Summary

A **$24.1 billion** gain for shareholders ($1.3B of it spent on buybacks), lifting per-share book
value **14.4%** to **$114,214** (48 years, **19.7% compounded**) — yet Buffett calls it **"subpar"**:
for the **ninth time in 48 years** book value trailed the S&P (+16.0%), since "**we do better when
the wind is in our face**." The five-year-win streak (43 straight) may finally snap. A second
disappointment: "**my inability to make a major acquisition** — I pursued a couple of elephants, but
came up empty-handed."

**The Heinz deal (announced early 2013).** Berkshire agreed to buy **50% of a holding company that
will own all of [[H.J. Heinz]]**, partnering with **[[Jorge Paulo Lemann]]**'s 3G group. Each side
puts in ~$4B of common equity; Berkshire *also* takes **$8 billion of 9% preferred** (with a
redemption premium and warrants for 5% of the common) — a ~$12B total commitment, the template for
Berkshire's later 3G partnerships. "Back to work; Charlie and I have again donned our safari
outfits."

**The "powerhouse five."** BNSF, [[ISCAR]], [[Lubrizol]], [[Marmon Group]] and [[MidAmerican Energy]] delivered **$10.1 billion** pre-tax (+$600M) — versus $393M from the only one owned eight
years earlier (MidAmerican). The $9.7B earnings gain came "with only minor dilution." A **record
year for bolt-ons**: $2.3B for **26 companies**, no shares issued ("Charlie and I love these
acquisitions").

**Insurance "shot the lights out."** A **tenth consecutive underwriting profit** ($1.6B) atop
**$73 billion of free float** — "having your cake and eating it too." **[[GEICO]]** lifted share
**2.5% → 9.7%** (premium $2.8B → $16.7B since the 1995 control purchase); "when I count my blessings,
I count GEICO twice." But GEICO suffered its **largest single loss ever** — **Hurricane Sandy**
(46,906 vehicles, 3× its Katrina loss) — and a $410M one-time accounting charge masked the real
result (GAAP underwriting profit $680M). **Guard Insurance** (workers' comp, ~$300M premiums) was a
late-2012 bolt-on.

**Succession & the Big Four.** **[[Todd Combs]] and [[Ted Weschler]]** each beat the S&P by
double digits and had their funds raised to ~$5B each ("we hit the jackpot"; "you can rest easy when
they take over"). Berkshire's ownership of the **"Big Four"** ([[American Express]] 13.7%,
[[Coca-Cola]] 8.9%, [[IBM]] 6.0%, [[Wells Fargo]] 8.7%) rose via purchases and the investees' own
buybacks — "**Too much of a good thing can be wonderful**" (Mae West). Berkshire's 2012 share of
their earnings was $3.9B (only $1.1B reported as dividends; $2.8B "every bit as valuable"; unrealized
gains on the four $26.7B). Employment hit a record **288,462**; HQ stayed at **24**. Capex set a
record **$9.8B** (88% in the U.S.).

**Dividends — the landmark essay.** Buffett explains why Berkshire pays no dividend though it
"relishes" dividends from its holdings (see [[Evolving Views — Dividends]]): the priority order is
(1) reinvest in existing businesses (moat-widening — owning many fields lets Berkshire "**water the
flowers and skip over the weeds**"), (2) acquisitions that raise per-share value, (3) buybacks below
intrinsic value, and only then a payout. He demonstrates with a "sell-off" math example that a
retained-earnings-plus-occasional-share-sale approach beats a fixed dividend for a company
compounding above book — provided the stock trades above book (Berkshire's does). "Wishing makes
dreams come true only in Disney movies; it's poison in business."

**"We Buy Some Newspapers… Newspapers?"** Berkshire bought **28 dailies for $344M** over fifteen
months even while reaffirming the industry's secular decline (see [[Evolving Views — Newspapers]]).
The thesis: papers retain primacy only in **local news** to "tightly-bound communities," must adopt
a **paywall** (the Arkansas *Democrat-Gazette* and WSJ models), and must *not* cut news content
or frequency. **[[Stan Lipsey]] retired** as Buffalo News publisher — "the News might now be extinct
were it not for Stan" (the 1977–82 Sunday-edition war recounted).

**Regulated, capital-intensive ([[Burlington Northern Santa Fe|BNSF]] + [[MidAmerican Energy]]).**
BNSF interest coverage 9.6×; it carries ~15% of all U.S. intercity ton-miles (500 miles per gallon
of diesel) and will spend ~$4B in 2013 (≈2× depreciation, "more than any railroad has spent in a
single year"). MidAmerican's utilities span ten states; it has **6% of U.S. wind capacity** and,
with three projects under construction, will own ~**14% of U.S. solar** — a renewables portfolio
costing **$13 billion**. **[[HomeServices of America]]** (now ~16,000 agents; $42B of home sales,
+33%) bought 67% of the Prudential/Real Living franchises and will rebrand as **Berkshire Hathaway
HomeServices** (Ron Peltier). MidAmerican earnings to Berkshire **$1,323M**; BNSF net **$3,372M**.

**Manufacturing, Service & Retailing.** Revenues **$83,255M**; pre-tax $6,131M. Common stocks: cost
$49,796M / market **$87,662M**, with two notable additions — **DIRECTV** (the first Combs/Weschler
holding to cross the $1B itemization threshold) and **Phillips 66** (via the [[ConocoPhillips]]
spin-off). Derivatives: the credit-default book will expire in 2013 at a ~$1B pre-tax profit; the
equity-index puts' settlement liability fell to $3.9B (GAAP liability cut to $7.5B). Buffett's
liquidity creed: redundant cash layers "reduce our returns in 99 years out of 100. But we will
survive in the 100th while many others fail."

## Key figures

- **Book value/share:** $114,214, **+14.4%** (net worth +$22.8B after $1.3B of buybacks); 48 years,
  **19.7%**. Per-share investments $113,786 (+15.7%); non-insurance pre-tax EPS $8,085 (+15.7%).
- **Insurance:** **10th straight underwriting profit** ($1,625M: BH Reins $304M, Gen Re $355M, GEICO
  $680M [after a $410M accounting charge], Other Primary $286M); **float $73,125M** (10-yr UW profit
  $18.6B). GEICO share **9.7%** (premium $16.7B); Ajit float $35B.
- **Heinz:** 50% of the holding co. with 3G (~$4B common + **$8B of 9% preferred** + warrants).
- **Powerhouse five** pre-tax **$10.1B**; **26 bolt-ons** for $2.3B; capex record **$9.8B**.
- **BNSF:** revenues $20,835M, net $3,372M (coverage 9.6×). **MidAmerican (89.8%):** earnings to
  Berkshire $1,323M; renewables portfolio → **$13B** (6% of U.S. wind, ~14% of U.S. solar).
- **Common stocks** (cost $49,796M / market **$87,662M**): Wells Fargo $15,592M, Coca-Cola (~$14.5B),
  IBM (~$13B), Amex (~$8.7B), Wal-Mart $3,741M, P&G $3,563M, Munich Re $3,599M, US Bancorp $2,493M,
  Sanofi $2,438M, Tesco $2,268M, **DIRECTV $1,150M (new; Combs/Weschler)**, **Phillips 66 $1,097M
  (new)**, POSCO $1,295M. Newspapers: 28 dailies for $344M.
