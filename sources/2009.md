# 2009 Shareholder Letter

## Summary

A snap-back year: net worth grew **$21.8 billion**, lifting per-share book value **19.8%** to
**$84,487** (45 years, **20.3% compounded**). The headline event is the **[[Burlington Northern Santa Fe]]** acquisition, which added ~65,000 shareholders, prompting Buffett to restate the
**owner-related business principles** and run "a freshman orientation session for our BNSF
newcomers." He notes that on a *market-price* basis Berkshire compounded **22%** (an 801,516% gain
over 45 years vs. 434,057% on book) — better than book because the stock once traded at a discount
to under-earning textile assets and now trades at a premium to first-class businesses. The 45-year
table's three messages: Berkshire has beaten the S&P in **all 41 rolling five-year periods**; its
"**defense has been better than its offense**" (it beat the index in all eleven down years); but the
size handicap is "**certain to continue**" — "huge sums forge their own anchor."

**"What We Don't Do."** Built on Munger's inversion ("All I want to know is where I'm going to die,
so I'll never go there"): (1) avoid businesses "whose futures we can't evaluate" (foreseeable
*growth* — autos in 1910, aircraft in 1930 — does not make *returns* predictable); (2) "**we will
never become dependent on the kindness of strangers** — too-big-to-fail is not a fallback position."
In September 2008 Berkshire was "**a supplier of liquidity and capital to the system, not a
supplicant**," pouring **$15.5 billion** in at the peak ($9B to bolster three American businesses,
$6.5B for [[Wrigley]]); it keeps $20B+ of cash "earning a pittance… But we sleep well." (3)
Extreme [[Decentralization]] (now ~257,000 employees); (4) no wooing of Wall Street.

**Insurance.** Float has grown from **$16M (1967) to $62 billion**, with a **seventh consecutive
underwriting profit** ($1,559M: [[General Re]] $477M, [[Ajit Jain|BH Reinsurance]] $349M, [[GEICO]]
$649M, Other Primary $84M); float $61,911M. **[[GEICO]]** (Tony Nicely, 66, who "still tap-dances to
the office") lifted share to **8.1%** (from 2.5% at the 1996 control purchase), the **#3** auto
insurer; its float grew $2.7B → $9.6B; profitable in 13 of 14 Berkshire years. The "**painful
confession**": Buffett's own **[[GEICO]] credit-card** idea was "**a very expensive business fiasco
entirely of my own making**" — $6.3M of operating losses plus a $44M loss selling the $98M
receivables book at 55¢ (GEICO's managers had warned him; "I subtly indicated that I was older and
wiser. I was just older"). **[[Ajit Jain]]** (30 people; discovered by [[Mike Goldberg]], "St.
Mike," in 1985) negotiated a **life-reinsurance contract that could yield $50 billion of premium**
over ~50 years; "if Charlie, I and Ajit are ever in a sinking boat… **swim to Ajit**." [[General Re]] (Tad Montross) had an outstanding year and finally took **100% of Cologne Re**.

**Regulated utility & the railroad.** [[MidAmerican Energy]] (now **89.5%**) earned **$1,071M** to
Berkshire; over three years its Iowa and Western utilities earned $2.5B while spending **$3B on wind
generation**. **[[HomeServices of America]]** turned a "modest sum" ($43M) and bought a Chicago
firm. Buffett reverses his old aversion to capital-intensive businesses: with Berkshire generating
"ever-increasing amounts of cash," he is now "**quite willing to enter businesses that regularly
require large capital expenditures**" if they earn decent returns on the incremental capital. **BNSF
will be reported in the "regulated utility" section** — both rest on a "**social compact**" with
regulators (essential service + far-sighted heavy investment in return for fair allowed returns);
the sector will absorb "**tens — yes, tens — of billions of dollars of incremental equity capital**"
([[Matt Rose]] runs BNSF).

**Manufacturing, Service & Retailing.** Revenues **$61,665M**; pre-tax $2,058M — almost all units
hit by the recession. **[[McLane]]** set a record ($344M pre-tax on $31.2B sales; Grady Rosier).
[[Marmon Group]] hit a record **13.5% pre-tax margin** despite a 27% sales drop (Frank Ptak);
[[ISCAR]] kept earning while absorbing Tungaloy. The construction-linked four (Shaw, Johns Manville,
Acme Brick, MiTek) earned just **$227M** — down 82.5% from $1.295B in 2006. **The big problem was
[[Executive Jet|NetJets]]**: an 11-year aggregate pre-tax loss of $157M and debt ballooning $102M →
$1.9B; "it's clear that I failed you in letting NetJets descend into this condition." **[[David Sokol]]** became CEO in August, cut debt to $1.4B, and turned a staggering 2009 loss of **$711M**
into profitability — without compromising Santulli's safety standards ("we eat our own cooking").

**Finance.** **[[Clayton Homes]]** (Kevin Clayton) remains profitable as rivals Fleetwood, Champion
and Oakwood all went bankrupt (industry output 382,000 in 1999 → 60,000 in 2009; U.S. housing starts
554,000, the lowest in 50 years — "**paradoxically… good news**" for clearing the housing overhang).
Buffett presses the **mortgage-rate differential** ("Berkshire has a dog in this fight"): factory-built
buyers pay ~9% versus ~5¼% for agency-guaranteed site-built homes, throttling sales of an otherwise
cheaper product. New: at year-end Berkshire became a **50% owner of [[Berkadia Commercial Mortgage]]**
(ex-Capmark, the #3 commercial-mortgage servicer) with [[Leucadia National]] — "**Son of Berkadia**."

**Investments.** Common stocks cost $34,646M / market **$59,034M**. **[[BYD]]** is the star — 225M
shares (9.9%), cost $232M, now worth **$1,986M**. Plus **non-traded securities** in **[[Dow Chemical]], [[General Electric]], [[Goldman Sachs]], [[Swiss Re]] and [[Wrigley]]** (aggregate cost
$21.1B / carry $26.0B; throwing off **$2.1B/yr** in dividends and interest), bought in the prior 18
months. Largest 2009 sales: [[ConocoPhillips]], [[Moody's]], [[Procter & Gamble]], [[Johnson & Johnson]] (to raise cash for Dow, Swiss Re and BNSF). "**When it's raining gold, reach for a bucket,
not a thimble**" — Buffett wishes he'd bought more cheap corporate/muni bonds in 2008. "A climate of
fear is [the investor's] best friend."

**Derivatives & governance.** Few changes; ~10% of equity puts had maturities shortened and strikes
cut (no cash changed hands). Derivatives "float" ~$6.3B; reported gains swung wildly by quarter
(−$1.517B, +$2.357B, +$1.732B, +$1.052B). "A CEO **must not delegate risk control**." Buffett blasts
failed-bank boards: shareholders lost >$500B in the four largest fiascos while reckless CEOs/directors
"largely went unscathed" — they need "**meaningful sticks**," not just carrots. **"An Inconvenient
Truth (Boardroom Overheating)"**: the BNSF deal forced Berkshire to issue ~95,000 shares (6.1%) —
"Charlie and I enjoy issuing Berkshire stock about as much as we relish prepping for a colonoscopy" —
the springboard for a fresh **[[Issuing Shares for Acquisitions|share-issuance]]** essay (the A&B
$100/$80 math; the Chihuahua-vs-St.-Bernard bank-merger story; "**Don't ask the barber whether you
need a haircut**"; hire a second, against-the-deal advisor).

## Key figures

- **Book value/share:** $84,487, **+19.8%** (net worth +$21.8B); 45 years, **20.3% compounded**
  (book) / **22%** (market; 801,516% total). Year-end cash **$30.6B** ($8B earmarked for BNSF).
- **Insurance:** **7th straight underwriting profit** ($1,559M: Gen Re $477M, BH Reins $349M, GEICO
  $649M, Other Primary $84M); **float $61,911M**. GEICO share **8.1%** (#3); float $9.6B.
- **Acquisitions:** **BNSF** (~$26B for the ~77.5% not owned; closes Feb 2010); 50% of **Berkadia
  Commercial Mortgage** (with Leucadia). NetJets returned to profit under Sokol.
- **MidAmerican (89.5%):** earnings to Berkshire **$1,071M**; $3B on wind over three years. BNSF to
  join the "regulated utility" reporting sector.
- **Mfg/Svc/Retail:** revenues **$61,665M**, pre-tax $2,058M. McLane record $344M; construction four
  $227M (−82.5% vs 2006). **NetJets** 2009 loss $711M → profitable; debt $1.9B → $1.4B.
- **Confessed:** the GEICO **credit-card** fiasco (~$50M total); letting NetJets's debt balloon;
  not buying *more* cheap bonds in 2008.
- **Common stocks** (cost $34,646M / market **$59,034M**): Coca-Cola $11,400M, Wells Fargo $9,021M,
  Amex $6,143M, P&G $5,040M, Kraft $3,541M, POSCO $2,092M, Wal-Mart $2,087M, **BYD $1,986M** (cost
  $232M), Sanofi $1,979M, ConocoPhillips $1,926M, J&J $1,838M, US Bancorp $1,725M, Tesco $1,620M.
  **Non-traded** (Dow, GE, Goldman, Swiss Re, Wrigley): cost $21.1B / carry $26.0B; $2.1B/yr income.
