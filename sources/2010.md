# 2010 Shareholder Letter

## Summary

Per-share book value rose **13%** to **$95,453** (46 years, **20.2% compounded**). The highlight was
the **February close of [[Burlington Northern Santa Fe]]** — "**working out even better than I
expected**," lifting Berkshire's "**normal" earning power by ~40% pre-tax / >30% after-tax**; the
$22B of cash used was "quickly replenished." Buffett estimates Berkshire's **normal earning power**
at ~**$17B pre-tax / $12B after-tax**. The letter is unusually optimistic on America ("our citizens
now live an astonishing six times better than when I was born"; "**America's best days lie ahead**")
and on capital investment: $6B of property/equipment spending in 2010 (90% in the U.S.), a record
**$8B planned for 2011**. A new **five-year-periods** performance table (42 periods) shows the
relative edge narrowing with size.

**Intrinsic value — today and tomorrow.** Buffett frames Berkshire's value as three pillars. (1)
**Investments** — $158B at year-end (float funds $66B); per-share investments grew $66 (1970) →
$94,730 (2010), 19.9%/yr over 40 years but only **6.6% in 2000–2010** as cash shifted to whole
businesses. (2) **Non-insurance earnings** — per-share pre-tax $2.87 (1970) → **$5,926** (2010),
**21%/yr** over 40 years (stock price +22.1%/yr). (3) A subjective third pillar: **how well retained
earnings will be deployed** ("a dollar… in the hands of Sears Roebuck's or Montgomery Ward's CEOs…
had a far different destiny than… a dollar entrusted to Sam Walton"). "**Our elephant gun has been
reloaded, and my trigger finger is itchy.**"

**Berkshire's advantages & culture.** Skilled managers who are "**volunteers, not mercenaries**"
("hire well, manage little"); **unrestricted capital allocation** ("the world is our oyster" —
unconstrained by industry, comparing businesses against each other and against marketable
securities); and a "hard-to-duplicate culture" (directors own >$3B, no D&O insurance; HQ rent
$270,212). Buffett mocks his own 1965 textile blunder ("a final burst of brilliance, I went out and
bought another textile company. Aaaaaaargh!").

**The [[GEICO]] case study (book vs. intrinsic value).** The 60-year Davy/janitor origin story, then
the math: the 1996 purchase of the second 50% for $2.3B implied a $4.6B value on a $1.9B tangible
net worth — a **$2.7B goodwill** estimate (97% of GEICO's then-$2.8B premiums). Premiums are now
**$14.3B**, yet the goodwill sits on the books at **$1.4B**, while its real economic value is
**~$14 billion** — "the gift that keeps giving." Share **8.8%** (from 2.0% when Tony Nicely took
over in 1993); a personal-lines agency sold 769,898 new policies (+34%).

**Insurance.** Float grew $39M (1970) → **$65,832M** (2010); an **eighth consecutive underwriting
profit** ($2,013M: [[General Re]] $452M, [[Ajit Jain|BH Reinsurance]] $176M, [[GEICO]] $1,117M,
Other Primary $268M) totaling **$17B over eight years** — versus State Farm's >$20B underwriting
*loss* over the last decade. **[[Ajit Jain]]** built a **$30B-float** operation "from a standing
start in 1985"; "even kryptonite bounces off Ajit." **[[General Re]]** (Tad Montross) prompts the
"**four disciplines**" of sound underwriting — know the exposures; evaluate the odds conservatively;
price for profit; and "**the willingness to walk away**" (most insurers flunk the fourth).

**Regulated, capital-intensive businesses (new sector: [[Burlington Northern Santa Fe|BNSF]] +
[[MidAmerican Energy]]).** Both rest on a "**social compact**": essential service and far-sighted
heavy investment in exchange for fair regulated returns; both carry large non-recourse debt (BNSF's
2010 interest coverage 6:1). BNSF moves ~28% of U.S. rail ton-miles (>11% of all intercity freight)
over 23,000 miles of track; it will spend $2B above depreciation in 2011. MidAmerican (Berkshire
**89.8%**) serves 2.4M electric customers (largest in Iowa/Wyoming/Utah) and moves 8% of U.S. gas;
its pipelines now rank #1 (Kern River) and #2 (Northern Natural); Iowa rates flat since 1999; it
will have **2,909 MW of wind** by end-2011 ($5.4B invested/committed — "more than any other regulated
electric utility").

**Manufacturing, Service & Retailing.** Revenues **$66,610M**; pre-tax $4,274M. Four record-breakers:
**[[TTI]]** (+58% earnings; Paul Andrews), **[[Forest River]]** (~$2B sales; Pete Liegl), **[[CTB]]**
($106M pre-tax; $160M of dividends already paid on the $140M 2002 cost), **[[H. H. Brown]]** (Jim
Issler; Frank Rooney, 89). **[[Executive Jet|NetJets]]** swung **+$918M** to a $207M pre-tax profit
under **[[David Sokol]]** (now charged a $38M fee for Berkshire's credit guarantee). **[[Marmon Group]]** (130 businesses) to be taken to **80%** via the scheduled $1.5B purchase of 17% from the
Pritzkers (rest in 2013/2014). [[McLane]] entered **wine & spirits** (Empire Distributors; David
Kahn). [[ISCAR]] +159%. The construction four (Shaw, Johns Manville, Acme Brick, MiTek) earned
$362M pre-tax vs $1.3B in 2006 (employment −9,400) but kept investing ("our time horizon is
forever").

**Finance.** [[Clayton Homes]] built **23,343 homes (47% of the industry's 50,046)**; its 200,804
originated mortgages (avg FICO **648**, 47% ≤640) lost just 1.17%–1.86% a year through the crisis —
"if home buyers throughout the country had behaved like our buyers, America would not have had the
crisis." Buffett presses the FHA/Freddie/Fannie bias against factory-built homes. XTRA and CORT
recovered.

**Investments, derivatives & succession.** Common stocks cost $33,733M / market **$61,513M**; new
**[[Munich Re]]** (10.5%, $2,924M). Investees' undistributed earnings >$2B. The five crisis
non-traded securities begin to roll off — **[[Swiss Re]]** redeemed early 2011, **[[Goldman Sachs]]**
and **[[General Electric]]** preferreds likely called in 2011 (~$1.4B of redemption premiums, but
"unwelcome"); offsets are eventual higher cash yields and rising dividends ([[Wells Fargo]], Coca-Cola
— "**Time is the friend of the wonderful business**"). Derivatives down to **203 contracts**: the
high-yield credit book paid $2.5B of losses but still earned an underwriting profit (the right
premium was charged); eight equity puts were unwound (+$222M gain); 39 remain ($4.2B premiums,
carried at $6.7B vs. $3.8B settlement value). The **"net income is almost always meaningless at
Berkshire"** essay (realized vs. unrealized gains) and a renewed **Black-Scholes** critique ("we
would rather be approximately right than precisely wrong"). **[[Lou Simpson]] retired** (at 74, "an
age Charlie and I regard as appropriate only for trainees"); **[[Todd Combs]]** hired as a new
investment manager ("a 2-year-old Secretariat, not a 10-year-old Seabiscuit"). "**Life and Debt**":
to finish first you must first finish — "any series of positive numbers… evaporates when multiplied
by a single zero."

## Key figures

- **Book value/share:** $95,453, **+13%** (46 years, **20.2% compounded**). Normal earning power
  ~**$17B pre-tax / $12B after-tax**. Investments **$158B**; year-end cash **$38B**.
- **Insurance:** **8th straight underwriting profit** ($2,013M: Gen Re $452M, BH Reins $176M, GEICO
  $1,117M, Other Primary $268M); float **$65,832M** ($17B of UW profit over 8 years). GEICO share
  **8.8%**; economic goodwill ~**$14B** (carried at $1.4B).
- **BNSF (closed Feb):** +~40% pre-tax to normal earning power; revenues $16,850M, net $2,459M.
  **MidAmerican (89.8%):** earnings to Berkshire **$1,131M**; 2,909 MW of wind / $5.4B by end-2011.
- **Mfg/Svc/Retail:** revenues **$66,610M**, pre-tax $4,274M. **NetJets** +$918M swing → $207M
  pre-tax. **Marmon** to 80% (~$1.5B for 17% from the Pritzkers).
- **Derivatives:** 203 contracts; high-yield credit paid $2.5B losses (still a UW profit); 39 equity
  puts left ($4.2B premiums; carried $6.7B vs $3.8B settlement value).
- **Common stocks** (cost $33,733M / market **$61,513M**): Coca-Cola $13,154M, Wells Fargo $11,123M,
  Amex $6,507M, P&G $4,657M, Kraft $3,063M, **Munich Re $2,924M (new)**, J&J $2,785M, US Bancorp
  $2,105M, Wal-Mart $2,105M, ConocoPhillips $1,982M, POSCO $1,706M, Sanofi $1,656M, Tesco $1,608M,
  BYD $1,182M (fell). The five **non-traded** crisis securities begin rolling off in 2011.
