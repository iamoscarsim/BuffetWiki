# 2022 Shareholder Letter

## Summary

Berkshire's market value rose **+4.0%** against an S&P that fell **−18.1%**; over 1965–2022 it compounded
**19.8%** (S&P 9.9%); overall 1964–2022 gain **3,787,464%**. **Operating earnings set a record at $30.8
billion** — the figure Buffett urges shareholders to watch — while GAAP results swung "acrobatically"
(quarterly GAAP of +5.5, −43.8, −2.7, +18.2 vs. steady operating earnings of 7.0, 9.3, 7.8, 6.7), proof
that "**the GAAP earnings are 100% misleading when viewed quarterly or even annually**" ([[Operating Earnings vs GAAP Net Income]]).

**"What we do."** Two forms of ownership — controlled businesses (run on **trust** more than rules; "our
tolerance for personal misconduct is zero") and passive stock stakes. "**Charlie and I are not
stock-pickers; we are business-pickers.**" A blunt report card: "In 58 years… most of my capital-
allocation decisions have been no better than so-so," rescued at times by "very large doses of luck"
(USAir, Salomon). Satisfactory results came from "**about a dozen truly good decisions — that would be
about one every five years.**"

**The Secret Sauce.** The signature essay. Berkshire finished buying **400M shares of [[Coca-Cola]] in
August 1994 for $1.3 billion**; the dividend grew from **$75M (1994) to $704M (2022)** and the stake was
worth **$25 billion** at year-end. **[[American Express]]** is "much the same story": ~$1.3B completed in
1995, dividends **$41M → $302M**, worth **$22 billion**. Each is now ~**5% of Berkshire's net worth**. By
contrast, a $1.3B 30-year bond would still be $1.3B (0.3% of net worth) paying a flat ~$80M. "**The weeds
wither away in significance as the flowers bloom. Over time, it takes just a few winners to work wonders**"
(see [[Concentration]], [[The Power of Retained Earnings]]).

**Alleghany, and float.** Berkshire bought **[[Alleghany Corporation]]** (~$11.6B), a P/C insurer
"captained by **[[Joe Brandon]]**" (the former General Re CEO, returning) — its value enhanced because
Berkshire's financial strength lets the insurers invest as rivals cannot. **[[Insurance Float|Float]] rose
$147B → $164 billion** (up **8,000-fold** since 1967).

**Buybacks defended.** Berkshire repurchased **1.2%** of itself in 2022 (and Apple/Amex buybacks lifted
Berkshire's stakes "without any cost to us"). The auto-dealership analogy shows value-accretive
repurchases harm no one — and Buffett delivers the year's most-quoted line: "**When you are told that all
repurchases are harmful to shareholders or to the country, or particularly beneficial to CEOs, you are
listening to either an economic illiterate or a silver-tongued demagogue (characters that are not mutually
exclusive)**" (see [[Evolving Views — Buybacks]]). A warning follows that even *operating* earnings can be
manipulated — "'Bold imaginative accounting'… has become one of the shames of capitalism."

**58 years — the ten behemoths.** At year-end 2022 Berkshire was the **largest owner of eight S&P 500
giants**: [[American Express]], [[Bank of America]], [[Chevron]], [[Coca-Cola]], **[[HP Inc]]**,
[[Moody's]], [[Occidental Petroleum]], and **[[Paramount Global]]** — plus 100% of **[[Burlington Northern Santa Fe|BNSF]]** ($5.9B) and 92% of **[[MidAmerican Energy|BHE]]** ($4.3B), leaving Berkshire "**more
broadly aligned with the country's economic future than… any other U.S. company**." Forward principles:
"Our CEO will always be the **Chief Risk Officer** — a task it is irresponsible to delegate"; future CEOs
will own Berkshire stock "bought with their own money"; "**At Berkshire, there will be no finish line.**"

**Federal taxes.** Over the decade ending 2021, Berkshire paid **$32 billion** of corporate income tax —
"**almost exactly a tenth of 1%**" of all Treasury receipts; had ~1,000 companies matched it, "**no other
businesses nor any of the country's 131 million households would have needed to pay any taxes**." (The
$100-bill-stack imagery: $32B stacked rises 21 miles.) Buffett expects to "pay much more… during the next
decade. We owe the country no less" — the **[[The American Tailwind|American Tailwind]]** again: "I have
yet to see a time when it made sense to make a long-term bet against America."

**"Nothing Beats Having a Great Partner."** A page of **[[Charlie Munger]]** aphorisms (from a recent
podcast): "All I want to know is where I'm going to die, so I'll never go there"; "A great company keeps
working after you are not"; "A string of wonderful numbers times zero will always equal zero"; "Warren and
I **hated railroad stocks for decades, but the world changed**… better late than never"; and his
decision-clincher, "**Warren, think more about it. You're smart and I'm right.**" Buffett adds his own
rule: "Find a very smart high-grade partner — preferably slightly older than you — and then listen very
carefully to what he says." (Poignant in hindsight: Munger's last full letter alongside Buffett.)

## Key figures

- **Market value +4.0%** (S&P −18.1%); 1965–2022 compounded **19.8%**; 1964–2022 gain **3,787,464%**.
- **Record operating earnings $30.8B** (quarterly 7.0/9.3/7.8/6.7); GAAP swung +5.5/−43.8/−2.7/+18.2.
- **[[Alleghany Corporation]]** acquired (~$11.6B; [[Joe Brandon]]). **Float $147B → $164B** (8,000× since 1967).
- **The Secret Sauce:** Coca-Cola (cost $1.3B → $25B value; div $75M→$704M) and Amex (cost $1.3B → $22B;
  div $41M→$302M), each ~5% of net worth.
- **Buybacks 1.2%** of shares. **Federal tax $32B** over the decade (~0.1% of all U.S. receipts).
- **Largest owner of eight S&P 500 giants** (Amex, BofA, Chevron, Coke, **HP Inc.**, Moody's, Occidental,
  **Paramount**) + 100% BNSF ($5.9B) + 92% BHE ($4.3B).
