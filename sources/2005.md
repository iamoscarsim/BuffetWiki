# 2005 Shareholder Letter

## Summary

"A **decent year**." Net worth grew **$5.6 billion**, lifting per-share book value **6.4%** to
**$59,377** (41 years, 21.5% compounded), edging the S&P (+4.9%). Berkshire now owns **68 distinct
businesses**, and Buffett offers two per-share scorecards: **investments $74,129/share** (28.0% CAGR
since 1965; 13.0% over the last decade) and **non-insurance pre-tax earnings $2,441/share** (17.2%;
30.2% over the decade) — the crossover reflecting "our ever-increasing emphasis on business
acquisitions." Despite **$3.4 billion** of hurricane losses (Katrina $2.5B; Rita and Wilma $0.9B),
insurance still produced **costless float**, thanks above all to **[[GEICO]]** — productivity up
**32% in two years** (headcount −4% while policies +26%), market share **5.6% → 6.1%**, advertising
up to **$502M** (from $31M in 1996). "If you have a new son or grandson in 2006, name him **Tony**."

**Acquisitions — five, none for stock** ("when a management proudly acquires another company for
stock, the shareholders of the acquirer are concurrently selling part of their interest in
everything they own"):
- **[[Medical Protective]]** (MedPro) — a 106-year-old medical-malpractice insurer (Fort Wayne; CEO
  Tim Kenesey); reserves raised $125M post-purchase.
- **[[Forest River]]** — RV maker, $1.6B sales; entrepreneur **[[Pete Liegl]]** (sourced by a
  two-page fax; "it was easier to sell my business than to renew my driver's license").
- **[[Business Wire]]** — press-release distributor (150 countries, 25,000 clients); president
  **[[Cathy Baron Tamraz]]** wrote after a WSJ article; founder Lorry Lokey (1961).
- **[[Applied Underwriters]]** (81%) — payroll + workers'-comp for small businesses (Sid Ferenc,
  Steve Menzies); came via an [[Ajit Jain]] reinsurance contract.
- **[[PacifiCorp]]** (via **[[MidAmerican Energy]]**) — a major six-state Western electric utility,
  1.6M customers (~$5.1B: $3.4B Berkshire common + $1.7B MidAmerican debt); "we'll be looking for
  **more**" utility deals.
Bolt-ons: Shaw (~$550M, vertical integration), XTRA, Clayton.

**Insurance.** Float reached **$49 billion**. The underwriting scorecard turned on the hurricanes:
**total +$53M** (Gen Re **−$334M**, B-H Reinsurance **−$1,069M**, GEICO **+$1,221M**, Other Primary
**+$235M**). On mega-cats, Buffett invokes **Pascal's wager** about climate uncertainty ("our
ignorance means we must follow the course prescribed by Pascal"): write mega-cat coverage **only at
far higher prices** and within bearable aggregate exposure — while keeping "both the ability and the
appetite to be the largest writer of mega-cat coverage in the world" (see [[Super-Catastrophe Reinsurance]]). MedPro's post-purchase reserve add illustrates that "**surprises in insurance are
far from symmetrical**."

**Regulated utilities.** The **repeal of PUHCA** (Aug 8, 2005) let Berkshire convert its MidAmerican
preferred into common — **83.4% of votes** (economic interest 80.5%), and **consolidate** the
company. Buffett stresses the four-party "partnership" (Berkshire, **[[Walter Scott Jr]]**,
**[[David Sokol]]**, **[[Greg Abel]]**) is unchanged: "we will make major moves only when we are
unanimous." **[[HomeServices of America]]** did **$64B** of transactions (19,200 agents); a cooling
housing market "should lead to additional acquisition possibilities."

**Finance.** **[[Clayton Homes]]** is "the star" (Kevin Clayton): it now services **$17B** of
manufactured-housing loans (from $5.4B at purchase) and owns a **$9.6B** servicing portfolio,
prospering as rivals fled; it bought Karsten (West Coast). The painful counterpoint is
**[[General Re Securities]]**: a **−$104M** loss in 2005 (cumulative **−$404M**), the contract count
down to **741** (from 23,218) — including the liquidation of a **100-year contract**. Buffett:
"Fault me for **dithering** (Charlie calls it thumb-sucking)… we are a **canary in this business
coal mine** and should sing a song of warning as we expire," noting derivatives outstanding are now
"a multiple of what existed in 1998" (see [[Derivatives]]).

**Manufacturing/Service/Retail.** Revenues **$46,896M**; **22.2%** on tangible net worth / 10.1% on
carrying value. Fruit of the Loom reached **48.7%** of mass-market men's & boys' underwear (from
44.2% in 2002) and **24.7%** of women's & girls' intimates. Ben Bridge +6.6%, R.C. Willey +9.9%
(Reno opened, Sacramento next). FlightSafety expanded (Farnborough; 42 centers); **NetJets
([[Executive Jet]]) "I was dead wrong"** — it lost money again (U.S. into the red; Europe improved).
At **[[See's Candies]]**, **[[Chuck Huggins]] retired** after 34 years (>10× profit growth);
**[[Brad Kinstler]]** takes over.

**The "widening the moat" essay.** "Every day… the competitive position of each of our businesses
grows either weaker or stronger." When short- and long-term conflict, "**widening the moat must take
precedence**" — managers who hit short-term targets at the expense of costs, customers or brand get
"behind the eight-ball" (autos and airlines the cautionary tales). See [[Economic Moat]].

**Investments & Gillette→P&G.** Common stocks cost $15,947M / market **$46,721M**. **Gillette merged
into [[Procter & Gamble]]** (a $5.0B GAAP gain "meaningless… ignore it"); American Express spun off
**[[Ameriprise Financial]]**; new positions in **[[Anheuser-Busch]]** and **[[Wal-Mart]]**; Wells
Fargo (Dick Kovacevich) substantially increased. "Expect no miracles… they may double in ten years."
A coda on the **[[Gillette]]→Jim Kilts** turnaround (Duracell had destroyed billions; "it's
difficult to **overpay the truly extraordinary CEO**… but this species is rare") and the
[[Executive Compensation|comp]] farce of "**Ratchet, Ratchet and Bingo**" and "**Fred Futile**."

## Key figures

- **Book value/share:** $59,377, **+6.4%** (net worth +$5.6B); 41 years, **21.5% compounded**.
  Per-share investments **$74,129**; non-insurance pre-tax earnings **$2,441**.
- **Hurricane losses ~$3.4B** (Katrina $2.5B). **Insurance float $49,287M**; underwriting profit
  **+$53M** (Gen Re −$334M / B-H Reins −$1,069M / GEICO +$1,221M / Other Primary +$235M). GEICO
  share 5.6% → 6.1%; advertising $502M.
- **MidAmerican** → consolidated (83.4% votes / 80.5% economic) after PUHCA repeal; PacifiCorp
  pending. HomeServices $64B transactions.
- **Clayton:** services **$17B** of loans; owns **$9.6B** portfolio; earnings $416M.
- **Gen Re Securities:** −$104M (cumulative −$404M); 741 contracts left.
- **Mfg/Svc/Retail:** revenues **$46,896M**, 22.2% on tangible net worth / 10.1% carrying.
- **Common stocks:** cost $15,947M / market **$46,721M** (Amex $7,802M, Wells Fargo $5,975M,
  Coca-Cola $8,062M, **P&G $5,788M**, Moody's $2,948M, PetroChina $1,915M, **Anheuser-Busch $1,884M
  new**, WaPo $1,322M, **Ameriprise $1,243M new**, **Wal-Mart $933M new**, M&T $732M).
