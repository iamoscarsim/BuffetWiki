# 2007 Shareholder Letter

## Summary

Net worth grew **$12.3 billion**, lifting per-share book value **11%** to **$78,008** (43 years,
21.1% compounded). Of 76 operating businesses, only the **housing-linked** ones (brick, carpet, real
estate brokerage) stumbled — "minor and temporary." But the letter's backdrop is the **gathering
subprime crisis**: the "weakened lending practices" of 2006 are now producing "**staggering
problems**" at major financial institutions (John Stumpf of Wells Fargo: "the industry has invented
new ways to lose money when the old ways seemed to work just fine"). Americans believed "**HPA** —
house price appreciation — would cure all problems"; as prices fall, "**you only learn who has been
swimming naked when the tide goes out**." Insurance had an "excellent year," but "**that party is
over**" — 2008 margins "will fall significantly."

**Yardsticks.** Investments total **$141 billion** (funded by $59B of float); per-share investments
reached **$90,343** (27.1% CAGR over 42 years), and non-insurance per-share pre-tax earnings
**$4,093** (17.8%). But "Berkshire's past record can't be duplicated or even approached… our base…
is now far too large."

**Acquisitions.** On **Christmas Day** Buffett "finally earned [his] paycheck" with the **largest
cash purchase in Berkshire's history**: **60% of the [[Marmon Group]]** (the **[[Jay Pritzker|Pritzker]]
family**'s 125-business conglomerate — Union Tank Car's 94,000 rail cars; $7B sales) for **$4.5
billion**, with the rest to follow within six years on an earnings formula. The seed was planted in
1954 (Jay Pritzker's Rockwood Chocolate cocoa-bean arbitrage); CEO **[[Frank Ptak]]**; facilitated by
**[[Byron Trott]]** (code name "Indy 500"). Also **[[Richline Group]]** (jewelry supplier; Dennis
Ulrich, met at a Ben Bridge vendor lunch).

**"The Great, the Good and the Gruesome."** The landmark essay on **capital intensity vs. returns**
(see [[The Great the Good and the Gruesome]]). A **great** business has an enduring moat *and* earns
high returns on little capital: **[[See's Candies]]** is the prototype — bought for $25M earning
~$5M pre-tax on $8M of capital (~60%), it has since produced **$1.35 billion** of cumulative pre-tax
profit on just **$32M** of added capital. A **good** business (e.g. **[[FlightSafety International]]**,
regulated utilities) must "put up more to earn more" — FlightSafety reinvested $509M to lift pre-tax
earnings $111M → $270M. A **gruesome** business "grows rapidly, requires significant capital… and
then earns little or no money" — **airlines** ("a farsighted capitalist… at Kitty Hawk… would have
done his successors a huge favor by shooting Orville down"; the [[USAir]] scar). Three "savings
accounts."

**Confession.** Three "unforced errors": he nearly balked at See's over $5M; he turned down Tom
Murphy's $35M Dallas-Fort Worth NBC station (now worth ~$800M+ — "my brain had gone on vacation");
and worst, **Dexter** — "**$433 million in Berkshire stock (25,203 A shares)… made the cost to
Berkshire shareholders not $400 million, but rather $3.5 billion**. In essence, I gave away 1.6% of a
wonderful business — one now valued at $220 billion — to buy a worthless business" (see [[Dexter Shoe]], [[Mistakes Ledger]]).

**Insurance.** Float **$58,698M**; a second straight catastrophe-light year produced a **$3,374M
underwriting profit** (Gen Re +$555M, BH Reinsurance +$1,427M, GEICO +$1,113M, Other Primary +$279M).
**[[GEICO]]** "possesses the widest moat of any of our insurers" — share **2.5% (1995) → 7.2%** (ads
$31M → $751M; sales $12B), now expanding into motorcycle/ATV/RV/commercial auto. **[[General Re]]**
("the **Tiffany of reinsurers**," whose reputation "outlived its factual underpinnings" — a flaw "I
completely missed" in 1998) is restored by Brandon and Montross ($23B home-grown float). The smaller
insurers added **[[BoatU.S.]]** (Bill Oakerson; 650,000 boat owners).

**Manufacturing/Service/Retail.** Revenues **$59,100M**; **23%** on tangible net worth / 9.8% on
carrying value. Housing hit Shaw (−27%), Acme Brick (−41%), Johns Manville (−38%), MiTek (−9%). See's
profits **+50%** in two years under **[[Brad Kinstler]]**. Borsheim's +15.1% (**[[Susan Jacques]]**,
"$4-an-hour saleswoman" to CEO). NFM's Omaha and KC stores each ~$400M (the Blumkin brothers).
**[[ISCAR]]** opened a **Dalian, China** plant ("moved the brains to the tungsten"). Flight services
set a record (+49% to $547M); NetJets (487 U.S. / 135 European planes, ~90% large-cabin share).

**MidAmerican (87.4%).** Western utilities (post-PacifiCorp) earned $692M; the $35.05/share purchase
anecdote. **[[HomeServices of America]]** slow (18,800 agents). **[[Clayton Homes]]** hit a record
**31% market share** in a shrinking industry; its $11B loan book (300,000 borrowers) "performing
well" despite the real-estate-finance turmoil — a quiet rebuke to subprime lending.

**Investments.** Common stocks cost $39,252M / market **$74,999M**. **New positions: [[Burlington Northern Santa Fe]] (17.5%, $5,063M)** and **[[Kraft Foods]] (8.1%, $4,059M)**, plus
**[[Sanofi-Aventis]]**; J&J built to $4,287M; Wells Fargo to 9.2% ($9,160M). The big-four moats all
"widened." **[[PetroChina]] sold** for **$4 billion** (cost $488M; ~$3.5B gain; the $1.2B tax "paid
all costs of the U.S. government… for about four hours").

**Derivatives.** Now **94 contracts** in two categories: **54 credit-default** contracts on
high-yield indices (premiums **$3.2B**; worst case +$4.7B; liability $1.8B) and **equity-index put
options** on four indices (S&P 500 + three foreign; **$4.5B** of premiums; **European-style**,
expiring **2019–2027**; liability $4.6B). Both "hold the money" (**no counterparty risk**) but mark
to market quarterly, so reported earnings will swing "$1 billion or more" — "our philosophy in
derivatives" is the same volatility-for-value trade as in super-cat (see [[Derivatives]]).

**Dollar & succession.** The dollar fell further; the one direct currency position was the **Brazilian
real** (direct FX +$2.3B over five years; Amazon euro bonds an aside). Three CEO candidates remain
ready; the investment-manager search continues.
