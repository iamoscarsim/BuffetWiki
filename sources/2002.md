# 2002 Shareholder Letter

## Summary

"In all respects 2002 was a banner year." Net worth grew **$6.1 billion**, lifting per-share
book value **10.0%** to **$41,727** (38 years, 22.2% compounded). The gain beat the S&P 500
(−22.1%) by **32.1 points** — but Buffett calls the margin "**aberrational**," achievable only
because Berkshire now holds a smaller share of its assets in stocks (helping in down markets,
hurting in bull runs); the long-run aim is "an average annual advantage of a **few points**."
Four things went right at once: non-insurance operations thrived ("a decade ago Berkshire's
non-insurance pre-tax earnings were **$272 million** a year; now we earn that sum **monthly**"),
insurance float grew to **$41.2 billion** at just **1%** cost, important new businesses were
acquired, and the marketable portfolio outperformed.

**Pro-forma honesty, inverted.** In an era of "earnings far in excess of those allowed by
auditors," Buffett "make[s] a little history": on a pro-forma basis Berkshire earned *less*
than reported. Two breaks flattered 2002 — no megacatastrophe, and favorable bond-market
conditions for the finance unit — worth perhaps **$500M** of pre-tax earnings that won't
recur. His management model is **Eddie Bennett**, the batboy who hopped to whichever team was
winning: "to be a winner, work with winners."

**Acquisitions.** Two 2001-pending deals closed (**[[Larson-Juhl]]**/Albecca and **[[Fruit of the Loom]]**, ~33% of U.S. men's & boys' underwear, John Holland CEO). Two smaller ones:
**[[CTB]]** (worldwide leader in poultry/hog/egg/grain equipment; Vic Mancinelli) and
**[[Garan]]** (children's apparel; Garanimals; Seymour Lichtenstein). The largest 2002-initiated
deal was **[[The Pampered Chef]]** — direct-sales kitchenware founded by **[[Doris Christopher]]** on a $3,000 loan against her life insurance, now **$700M** of sales through
67,000 kitchen consultants (CEO Sheila O'Connell Cooper). Through **[[MidAmerican Energy]]**
(MEHC; Berkshire's equity now **80.2%**), two **gas pipelines**: **Kern River** (Wyoming→
S. California; a $1.2B doubling underway) and **[[Northern Natural Gas]]** (16,600 miles, bought
from a failing Dynegy — an Omaha "homecoming," the pipeline having passed through InterNorth →
**Enron** under Ken Lay). MEHC now has **$18B of assets** and Berkshire's largest non-insurance
earnings stream; **[[David Sokol]]** and **[[Greg Abel]]** are "huge assets." **[[HomeServices of America]]** doubled to **$37B** of transactions (Prudential California Realty the biggest add-on).

**Insurance.** Float grew **$5.7B to $41,224M** (GEICO $4,678M / Gen Re $22,207M / Other Reins
$13,396M / Other Primary $943M) at a **1% cost** — even after **$1.75B** of "charges" (Gen Re's
further prior-year reserve correction + the annual retroactive-reinsurance amortization), the
underlying book approached no-cost.
- **[[General Re]] — "I was dead wrong."** Buffett confesses that the discipline and conservative
  reserving he'd observed for decades had "substantially changed": Gen Re was "grossly mispricing
  its current business" and had amassed a **nuclear/chemical/biological (NCB)** aggregation that
  "the World Trade Center attack alone would have threatened [its] existence." 2002 took an
  **additional $1.31B** reserve charge ("I wish I didn't know now what I didn't know then"). Under
  **Joe Brandon** and **Tad Montross**, pricing discipline and NCB limits were restored; Gen Re is
  now the **sole across-the-board AAA** reinsurer among the giants (three rivals downgraded).
  "**Cheap reinsurance is a fool's bargain.**"
- **[[GEICO]]** "should pinch ourselves": premiums **$6.9B** (from $2.9B at the 1996 full
  acquisition), saving policyholders ~$1B/year, profit-sharing **19%** for 19,162 associates,
  internet new business **+75%**. **[[Tony Nicely]]** has "been in love with GEICO for 41 years."
  A cautionary footnote: a 1981–83 GEICO umbrella/product-liability dalliance took $72,000 of net
  premium and produced **$94.1M** of losses, **$90.3M** of it uncollectible from "deadbeat
  reinsurers."
- **[[Ajit Jain]]** "the major reason our float cost so little": **$13.4B** float, ~20 staff,
  a **$534M underwriting profit** (after a $428M retroactive-reinsurance charge). "If we ever
  put a photo in a Berkshire annual report, it will be of Ajit. In color! … bow deeply."
- Smaller insurers: float +38%, $32M underwriting profit — but **California workers' comp** had
  "terrible results" and missed reserves; "until we figure out how to get this business right, we
  will keep it small."

**Derivatives — "financial weapons of mass destruction."** The letter's landmark essay (see
**[[Derivatives]]**). Berkshire is unwinding **[[General Re Securities]]** (acquired unwanted with
Gen Re; lost **$173M** pre-tax in 2002; 14,384 contracts, 672 counterparties, $6.5B receivables).
Buffett's critique: **mark-to-model "degenerates into mark-to-myth,"** marking errors are
asymmetric (favoring the bonus-seeking trader/CEO), collateral-call triggers create downgrade
"**spirals**," and **daisy-chain linkage** concentrates credit risk in a few dealers (the **LTCM**
1998 near-miss the proof). "Like Hell, both [reinsurance and derivatives] are easy to enter and
almost impossible to exit."

**Investments.** Common stocks: cost $9,164M / market **$28,363M**. Over-$500M: Coca-Cola $8,768M
(fell), Amex $5,359M, Wells Fargo $2,497M, Gillette $2,915M, WaPo $1,275M, Moody's $991M, H&R
Block $643M, and a new **[[M&T Bank]]** $532M. Still "very few [stocks] that even mildly interest
us" — the bubble "hangover may prove to be proportional to the binge." But **"junk" bonds and
loans sextupled to $8.3 billion**. Over 38 years, Berkshire's own equity gains have beaten losses
~**100 to one**.

**Governance, audit, options.** A long **[[Corporate Governance]]** essay (Luke 16:2 stewardship;
"boardroom atmosphere"; a 62-year mutual-fund case study showing "independent" directors fail at
picking managers and cutting fees; CEO comp the "acid test," "More!" → "**Less!**"). Berkshire
will add codified-"independent" directors but prizes **true ownership**, pays directors "a
pittance," and carries **no D&O insurance**. The **Audit Committee** should ask auditors **four
questions** (would you have done it differently? would an investor get the plain-English truth?
etc.). On **[[Executive Compensation|options expensing]]**: the Arthur Andersen PSG's 1992 stand,
FASB's 7-0 vote, the Senate's **88-9** vote against, Levitt's regret, and **498 of 500** S&P
firms choosing not to expense. EBITDA is "particularly pernicious"; "there is seldom just **one
cockroach in the kitchen**."

## Key figures

- **Book value/share:** $41,727, **+10.0%** (net worth +$6.1B); 38 years, **22.2% compounded**;
  beat S&P (−22.1%) by **32.1 pts** ("aberrational").
- **Operating earnings to Berkshire $3,903M**; **total earnings $4,286M** (incl. $383M cap gains).
- **Float $41,224M** (+$5.7B), **cost ~1%**; $1.75B of Gen Re-reserve + retroactive charges.
  Gen Re further reserve charge **$1.31B**. GEICO UW profit **$416M**; Ajit's group UW profit
  **$534M**; Other Primary +$32M; **General Re UW loss −$1,393M**.
- **General Re Securities** lost **$173M** pre-tax (winding down; $6.5B receivables).
- **Common stocks:** cost $9,164M / market **$28,363M** (Coca-Cola $8,768M, Amex $5,359M, Wells
  Fargo $2,497M, Gillette $2,915M, WaPo $1,275M, Moody's $991M, H&R Block $643M, **M&T Bank $532M
  new**). **Junk bonds/loans $8.3B** (6×).
- **Acquisitions:** Larson-Juhl & Fruit of the Loom closed; CTB, Garan, The Pampered Chef; MEHC's
  Kern River & Northern Natural Gas pipelines (MEHC → 80.2%).
