# 2024 Shareholder Letter

## Summary

Buffett's last letter as CEO-author before handing the pen to **[[Greg Abel]]**. Market value rose
**+25.5%** (S&P +25.0%); over 1965–2024 Berkshire compounded **19.9%** (S&P 10.4%); overall 1964–2024 gain
**5,502,284%**. **Operating earnings set a record at $47.4 billion** (vs. $37.4B), even though "53% of our
189 operating businesses reported a decline in earnings" — the gains came from **insurance** (led by GEICO)
and a "predictable large gain in **investment income**" as the swelling T-bill pile finally paid well.

**"Mistakes — Yes, We Make Them."** A candor manifesto: Buffett has used "mistake" or "error" **16 times in
his 2019–23 letters** ("many other huge companies have never used either word"), and names the **cardinal
sin** as Munger's "**thumb-sucking**" — delaying the correction of a known mistake. The maxim (from
[[Tom Murphy]], 60 years ago): "**praise by name, criticize by category**." And the succession line: "**At
94, it won't be long before Greg Abel replaces me as CEO and will be writing the annual letters.**"

**Pete Liegl — One of a Kind.** A tribute to **[[Pete Liegl]]**, founder of **[[Forest River]]** (the RV
maker), who "died in November, still working at 80." Buffett retells the 2005 deal: Liegl named his own
price, asked for a **$100,000 salary** ("I wouldn't want to make more than my boss") plus a 10%-of-
incremental-earnings bonus, and over 19 years "**shot the lights out**." The lesson on personnel: "I never
look at where a candidate has gone to school. Never!" (Ben Rosner "never went past 6th grade"; Bill Gates
left Harvard) — "a very large portion of business talent is **innate**, with nature swamping nurture."

**"Surprise, Surprise! An Important American Record is Smashed."** In 2024 Berkshire paid the IRS **$26.8
billion** in four payments — "**about 5% of what all of corporate America paid**," and **more federal income
tax than any company in U.S. history** (more than the trillion-dollar tech titans). Cumulative payments
since 1965 now exceed **$101 billion** — made possible because Berkshire has paid **only one dividend ever**
(Jan 3, 1967: 10¢/A share, $101,755 — "Now it seems like a bad dream"). The imagery: a $1M check every 20
minutes all year would still not cover it. "**So thank you, Uncle Sam… Spend it wisely.**" (See [[The American Tailwind]].)

**"Where Your Money Is."** Berkshire's "ambidextrous" equity activity: **189 controlled subsidiaries**
("a few rare gems, many good-but-far-from-fabulous, and some laggards… nothing that is a major drag") and
a partial-ownership portfolio (Apple, Amex, Coca-Cola, Moody's) worth **$272 billion** at year-end. Buffett
pushes back on the "extraordinary cash position" chatter: marketable equities fell from **$354B to $272B**
(the big **[[Apple]]/[[Bank of America]]** sales), but "the value of our non-quoted controlled equities
increased… and remains far greater," and "**we will forever deploy a substantial majority of [your money]
in equities**." A currency warning: "**Paper money can see its value evaporate if fiscal folly prevails**…
Fixed-coupon bonds provide no protection against runaway currency."

**Property-Casualty Insurance.** The "rare — very rare" collect-now, pay-later model (asbestos claims still
paid 50 years on) means a long-tail insurer "may report large but fictitious profits… for many years —
even decades… particularly dangerous if the CEO is an optimist or a crook." Over the past two decades
Berkshire's insurance generated **$32 billion of after-tax underwriting profit** (~3.3¢ per dollar of
sales), and **float grew from $46 billion to $171 billion**. Under **[[Ajit Jain]]** the operation
"blossomed from an obscure Omaha-based company into a world leader." **[[Mike Goldberg]]** (who recruited
Jain) said it best: "**We want our underwriters to daily come to work nervous, but not paralyzed**" — "**not
a business for optimists**." Berkshire's edge: it can absorb extreme losses "without blinking" and is **not
dependent on reinsurers** (a durable cost advantage). (See [[Insurance Float]].)

**GEICO and the giants.** "**In five years, [[Todd Combs]] has reshaped [[GEICO]] in a major way**" —
modernizing underwriting and efficiency; "a long-held gem that needed major repolishing," with a
"spectacular" 2024 improvement. **BHE** ([[MidAmerican Energy|Berkshire Hathaway Energy]]) was taken to
**100%** late in the year (~$3.9B; $2.9B cash + B shares).

**Japan, increased.** The five **[[Japanese Trading Houses]]** (Itochu, Marubeni, Mitsubishi, Mitsui,
Sumitomo) now cost **$13.8B** against a year-end value of **$23.5B**; the companies **agreed to relax the
9.9% ceiling**, so Berkshire's ownership "will likely… increase somewhat." Buffett expects "**Greg and his
eventual successors will be holding this Japanese position for many decades**." Funded with yen debt (a
near currency-neutral position; cumulative $2.3B after-tax FX gain, $850M in 2024); 2025 expected dividend
income ~$812M vs. ~$135M of yen-interest cost.

**Closing.** Sister **[[Bertie Buffett|Bertie]]** (now 91) returns to the meeting; the new book *60 Years
of Berkshire Hathaway* features Charlie's photos and stories. Buffett's verdict on the whole enterprise:
"**Berkshire today is far more youthful than it was in 1965**… And we are not finished."

## Key figures

- **Market value +25.5%** (S&P +25.0%); 1965–2024 compounded **19.9%**; 1964–2024 gain **5,502,284%**.
- **Record operating earnings $47.4B**: insurance-UW **$9,020M**, insurance-investment income **$13,670M**,
  BNSF $5,031M, BHE $3,730M, other controlled $13,072M, non-controlled $1,519M, other $1,395M.
- **Record federal tax $26.8B** (~5% of all U.S. corporate tax; most ever by any U.S. company); cumulative
  **>$101B**; only one dividend ever (1967). **Greg Abel** "will replace me as CEO… and write the letters."
- **Insurance:** float **$46B → $171B** over two decades; **$32B** after-tax UW profit (3.3¢/$). **GEICO**
  reshaped by **[[Todd Combs]]**. **BHE taken to 100%** (~$3.9B).
- **Marketable equities $354B → $272B** (big **[[Apple]]**/**[[Bank of America]]** sales); record T-bill
  cash — but "majority… forever in equities." **[[Japanese Trading Houses]]** cost $13.8B / value $23.5B
  (9.9% ceiling relaxed). **[[Pete Liegl]]** ([[Forest River]]) tribute.
