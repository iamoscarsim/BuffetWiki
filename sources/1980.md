# 1980 Shareholder Letter

## Summary

Written February 27, 1981. Operating earnings rose to $41.9M (from $36.0M) but
return on beginning equity slipped to **17.8%** (from 18.6%). The letter's
intellectual centerpiece is **[[Look-Through Earnings]]**, developed at length under
"Non-Controlled Ownership Earnings." Buffett walks through the three accounting
regimes (consolidation >50%, equity method 20–50%, dividends-only <20%) to make a
striking point: because Berkshire's insurance assets concentrate in sub-20%
holdings that pay low dividends, "the part of 'our' earnings that these companies
retained last year... exceeded the total reported annual operating earnings of
Berkshire Hathaway." Conventional accounting shows "less than half of our earnings
'iceberg'... above the surface." The value of retained earnings, he insists, is
determined by the *use* to which they're put, not by ownership percentage — "if a
tree grows in a forest partially owned by us, but we don't record the growth in our
financial statements, we still own part of the tree." He would rather have
uncredited earnings well-used by a manager he didn't hire than credited earnings
squandered "by another management — even if we are that management." A "short
commercial" praises **share repurchases** by investees as a superb use of capital
when stock sells below intrinsic value — seeding [[Evolving Views — Buybacks]].

**[[Inflation]]** dominates "Results for Owners." Only purchasing-power gains are
real earnings: the **hamburger parable** (forgo 10 hamburgers, get back dividends +
proceeds that buy 8 → no real income, "you may feel richer, but you won't eat
richer"). Inflation plus taxes raises the **hurdle rate** of return needed to
deliver any real return to taxable owners; at 12% inflation a 20%-ROE business
paying out fully still erodes a 50%-bracket owner's capital. True indexing requires
ROE to rise with prices *without added capital* — which almost no business, "and
Berkshire Hathaway isn't one of them," can do.

The **[[GEICO]]** section is the letter's showpiece: 7.2M shares (~33%), cost $47M,
**market $105.3M**, with estimated look-through earning power of ~$20M vs. ~$3M
dividends. Buffett distinguishes GEICO from true turnarounds — it was "an
extraordinary business franchise with a localized excisable cancer" (like
[[American Express]] after the 1964 salad-oil scandal), saved by [[Jack Byrne]]'s
"managerial brilliance" — and delivers the famous verdict: "when a management with a
reputation for brilliance tackles a business with a reputation for poor fundamental
economics, it is the reputation of the business that remains intact." GEICO had
also shrunk its shares from 34.2M to 21.6M via buybacks.

A long, prescient **insurance-conditions** section warns that the accounting fiction
of carrying long bonds at amortized cost masks net-worth-destroying losses, and that
"cash-flow" / "asset-maintenance" underwriting (writing terrible business just to
hold investable assets) will prolong the industry's pricing agony — combined ratio
rising 100.6 → 103.5. Berkshire's **[[Financial Strength]]** (strongest net worth
relative to premium volume among large stock P&C groups) gives it "more staying
power and more options," though Buffett admits its own bond maturities remain too
mismatched. **Textiles**: Waumbec terminated, New Bedford looms cut a third — "your
Chairman made a costly mistake in not facing the realities... sooner." The
**[[Illinois National Bank]]** disposition completed December 31, 1980 via a clever
exchange offer (Rockford Bancorp shares for Berkshire shares). Berkshire sold $60M of
12¾% notes due 2005 to keep "financial firepower" for opportunities. The letter
closes with a moving eulogy for **[[Gene Abegg]]**, who died July 2, 1980 at 82 —
the straight-shooting banker who "never forgot he was handling other people's money."

## Key figures
- Operating earnings $41.9M; **ROE 17.8%** (down from 18.6%).
- Book value/share: $19.46 → **$400.80** (16 yrs, 20.5% compounded).
- Total common stocks: cost **$325.2M**, market **$529.7M**.
- GEICO: 7.2M shares (~33%), cost $47M, market $105.3M; est. look-through earnings ~$20M.
- Insurance combined ratio: industry 100.6 → ~103.5.
- $60M of 12¾% notes due 2005 issued.
- New holdings: Alcoa, Cleveland-Cliffs, National Detroit, Pinkerton's, R.J.
  Reynolds, Times Mirror; SAFECO 1.25M sh (mkt $45M); Washington Post mkt $42.3M.
