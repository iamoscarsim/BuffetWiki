# 2023 Shareholder Letter

## Summary

The letter opens with a one-page eulogy — **"Charlie Munger — The Architect of Berkshire Hathaway"** —
**[[Charlie Munger]] having died November 28, 2023, 33 days short of 100**. Buffett credits Munger with
the 1965 instruction that reshaped Berkshire ("**abandon everything you learned from your hero, Ben
Graham**… add wonderful businesses purchased at fair prices"), calling him "**the architect of the present
Berkshire**" to Buffett's "general contractor." Market value rose **+15.8%** (S&P +26.3%); 1965–2023
compounded **19.8%** (S&P 10.2%); overall 1964–2023 gain **4,384,748%**.

**Bertie.** Buffett frames the letter around his sister **[[Bertie Buffett]]** — smart, sensible, a
decades-long Berkshire holder — as the model shareholder he writes for: someone who "instinctively know[s]
that pundits should always be ignored."

**Operating Results, Fact and Fiction.** GAAP "net earnings" were **$90B (2021), −$23B (2022), $96B
(2023)** — "**worse-than-useless**," because they include unrealized gains that "can exceed $5 billion a
day." Berkshire's preferred **operating earnings**: **$27.6B / $30.9B / $37.4B** — a 2023 record. "Galileo's
experience… should have taught us not to mess with mandates from on high. But… we can be stubborn." (See
[[Operating Earnings vs GAAP Net Income]].)

**What We Do, and the size problem.** Own all or part of businesses with "good economics that are
fundamental and enduring," run by trustworthy managers (Hugh McCulloch, 1863: "**Never deal with a rascal
under the expectation that you can prevent him from cheating you**"). But Berkshire's **$561 billion GAAP
net worth — the largest of any American business — is now ~6%** of the entire S&P 500's net worth, so
"**we have no possibility of eye-popping performance**": only a handful of U.S. companies can "move the
needle," and "outside the U.S. there are essentially no candidates." The realistic aim: "a bit better than
the average American corporation" with "**materially less risk of permanent loss of capital**."

**Our Not-So-Secret Weapon.** Markets "can — and will — unpredictably seize up" (1914, 2001, 2008);
Berkshire's ability to "immediately respond… with both huge sums and certainty of performance" is the
edge. Markets now show "**far more casino-like behavior**" ("The casino now resides in many homes"). The
unchanging rule: "**Never risk permanent loss of capital.**" "**Berkshire is built to last.**" (See
[[Financial Strength]] — EBITDA "a banned measurement at Berkshire.")

**Non-controlled businesses we're comfortable with.** **[[Coca-Cola]]** and **[[American Express]]** were
again untouched ("our own Rip Van Winkle slumber… now lasted well over two decades"); Berkshire's share of
2023 Amex earnings now **exceeds the $1.3B cost** of the whole stake. "**When you find a truly wonderful
business, stick with it… one wonderful business can offset the many mediocre decisions that are
inevitable.**" Two more "indefinite" holdings were *increased* in 2023: **[[Occidental Petroleum]]** —
**27.8%** of the common plus warrants, though "Berkshire has no interest in purchasing or managing
Occidental" (a paean to **Vicki Hollub** and U.S. **shale-driven energy independence**: production 8M
BOEPD in 1975 → 5M by 2007 → **>13M now**) — and the five **[[Japanese Trading Houses]]** (Itochu,
Marubeni, Mitsubishi, Mitsui, Sumitomo), lifted to **~9% each** after a Tokyo visit by Buffett and
**[[Greg Abel]]** (cost ¥1.6T, value ¥2.9T; **+$8B (61%)** unrealized gain; financed largely with **¥1.3T
of yen bonds**; pledge to stay below 9.9%).

**The Scorecard.** 2023 operating earnings **$37,350M** (vs. $30,853M): insurance-underwriting **$5,428M**
(vs. −$30M), insurance-investment income **$9,567M** (vs. $6,484M — the T-bill pile "finally began to pay
us far more than the pittance"), railroad **$5,087M** (down), utilities/energy **$2,331M** (down), other
$14,937M.

**BNSF — a disappointment.** "**Rail is essential to America's economic future**," but [[Burlington Northern Santa Fe|BNSF]] earnings fell more than Buffett expected (revenues down; Washington-mandated wage
increases "far beyond the country's inflation goals"); its **margins have slipped relative to all five**
peers since the 2010 purchase. The capital reality: BNSF carries 23,759 miles of track at **$70B on the
books** but "would cost at least **$500 billion to replicate**," and has spent **$22B over depreciation**
in 14 years. Still: "**A century from now, BNSF will continue to be a major asset of the country and of
Berkshire.**"

**BHE — "an even more severe… disappointment," and an owned mistake.** The century-old utility "social
compact" (a fixed-but-satisfactory regulated return) "**has been broken in a few states**," raising "the
specter of zero profitability or even bankruptcy" (PG&E in California, Hawaiian Electric). **[[MidAmerican Energy|BHE]]** booked a best-estimate for **forest-fire losses** (PacifiCorp), and Buffett confesses: "**I
did not anticipate or even consider the adverse developments in regulatory returns and… I made a costly
mistake in not doing so.**" Some utilities may be forced to the **public-power model** (as Nebraska chose
in the 1930s). (See [[Mistakes Ledger]].)

**Insurance — "it carried the day in 2023."** Record sales, float, and underwriting profits; P/C premium
volume has grown **5,000-fold ($17M → $83B)** since 1967. The defining trait of a Berkshire underwriter:
"**they can't be optimists at the office.**" "Our position would not be what it is if **[[Ajit Jain]]** had
not joined Berkshire in 1986" — his crew "is to P/C insurance what Cooperstown's honorees are to baseball."

**"What is it with Omaha?"** Introducing the three who now steer Berkshire: **[[Greg Abel]]** ("runs all
non-insurance operations… and in all respects is **ready to be CEO of Berkshire tomorrow**"), **[[Ajit Jain]]**, and — absent — **Charlie**. All shaped by Omaha, as was Bertie (who in 1980 put her money in
Berkshire and a mutual fund and "**made no new trades during the next 43 years**… she became very rich").

- **Record operating earnings $37.35B** (vs. $30.85B); GAAP net "earnings" $96B (meaningless). **GAAP net
  worth $561B** — largest of any U.S. company (~6% of the S&P 500's total).

  $5,087M, utilities/energy $2,331M, other $14,937M.

- **[[Charlie Munger]] died Nov 28, 2023** ("the architect"). **[[Greg Abel]]** "ready to be CEO… tomorrow."
