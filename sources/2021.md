# 2021 Shareholder Letter

## Summary

A letter built not around an earnings recap but around **what shareholders own**. Berkshire's market value
rose **+29.6%** (vs. the S&P's +28.7%); over 1965–2021 it compounded **20.1%** (S&P 10.5%); overall
1964–2021 gain **3,641,613%**. "**Charlie and I are not stock-pickers; we are business-pickers**" —
Berkshire owns wonderful businesses (whole or in part), and "it is also far easier to exit from a mistake
when it has been made in the marketable arena."

**"Surprise, Surprise."** Four facts Buffett highlights: (1) Berkshire owns **more U.S.-based
infrastructure (property, plant & equipment) than any other American company — $158 billion** ("Berkshire
always will be building"). (2) Berkshire is a huge **taxpayer** — $3.3B in 2021 (of $402B in total U.S.
corporate receipts) — illustrated by the **"silent partner" history**: in the nine years after the 1955
Berkshire/Hathaway textile merger, net worth craters $51.4M → $22.1M and the company paid the Treasury
just **$337,359 ("a pathetic $100 per day")**; after the 1965 course correction, Berkshire now pays "**~$9
million daily**" ("When you see the flag, say thanks"). (3) **[[Insurance Float|Float]] reached $147
billion** (from $19M in 1967), "cost… less than nothing" over 55 years and "**very sticky**." (4) The
**[[Ajit Jain]] hiring story** (1986): asked his insurance experience, Jain said "None"; "I said,
'Nobody's perfect,' and hired him… as perfect a choice as could have been made."

**Our Four Giants.** Most of Berkshire's value sits in four businesses: (1) the **insurance** cluster
(100%-owned; the float engine, backed by extraordinary capital); (2) **[[Apple]]** — the "runner-up Giant"
at **5.55%** (up from 5.39% — "we spent no Berkshire funds… Apple's repurchases did the job"); GAAP counts
only the **$785M of dividends**, but Berkshire's "share" of Apple's earnings was **$5.6 billion** (Tim
Cook praised); (3) **[[Burlington Northern Santa Fe|BNSF]]** — record **$6 billion** earnings (143M train-
miles, 535M tons; "the number one artery of American commerce"); and (4) **[[MidAmerican Energy|BHE]]** —
record **$4 billion**, up **30-fold** from $122M in 2000 (now **91.1%** owned; built by Sokol and **[[Greg Abel]]** into a wind/solar/transmission "utility powerhouse"). A pointed aside on the "old-fashioned sort
of earnings we favor" (after all real costs): "**bull markets breed bloviated bull**…" — deceptive
"adjustments" have grown "more frequent and more fanciful."

**Investments.** The portfolio (cost $104.6B / market **$350.7B**) is led by **[[Apple]] $161.2B (5.6%)**,
**[[Bank of America]] $46.0B (12.8%)**, **[[American Express]] $24.8B (19.9%)**, Coca-Cola $23.7B, Moody's
$9.6B, Verizon, US Bancorp, BYD $7.7B, Chevron $4.5B, GM, BNY Mellon, and the three itemized **[[Japanese Trading Houses]]** (Itochu, Mitsubishi, Mitsui — each ~5.5%). The **$10B [[Occidental Petroleum]]**
preferred (now $10.7B) sits in "Others." **[[Kraft Heinz]]** (26.6%, equity-method, $13.1B) and **[[Pilot Flying J|Pilot]]** (38.6%, $45B revenue — to **80% early 2023**) are noted separately.

**U.S. Treasury Bills and repurchases.** Berkshire held **$144 billion of cash** (ex-BNSF/BHE), **$120B in
T-bills** ("financing about ½ of 1% of the publicly-held national debt"), against a pledged **$30B floor**
— "never dependent on the kindness of strangers." Buffett restates the **$114.75 / three Cities Service
shares, March 11, 1942** origin (the Dow closed at 99 — "**Never bet against America**"), and that he has
always kept **≥80% in equities**. With acquisitions and stocks both pricey ("**low long-term interest
rates push the prices of all productive investments upward**"), the chosen path was **repurchases**:
Berkshire bought back **9% of its shares over 2020–21 for $51.7 billion** (+$1.2B since year-end), lifting
continuing owners' stake ~10% — and raising **float per A-share 25%** ($79,387 → $99,497). (See [[Evolving Views — Buybacks]].)

**"A Wonderful Man and a Wonderful Business."** A tribute to **[[Paul Andrews Jr|Paul Andrews]]** (died
2021), founder of **[[TTI]]** — fired by General Dynamics in 1971, he bet $500 on himself (Tex-Tronics,
first-year sales $112,000; now **$7.7B**, >1M items). He sold to Berkshire in 2006 because "**you are the
only guy left**" (not a synergy-slashing competitor or an exit-minded LBO). Under Berkshire, TTI grew
2,387 → 8,043 employees, earnings +673%. The serendipity: TTI brought Berkshire's October-2009 board visit
to **Fort Worth**, where Buffett met BNSF's [[Matt Rose]] — leading to the **BNSF acquisition** eleven
days later. "**BNSF will be a key asset for Berkshire and our country a century from now.**"

**Thanks.** Buffett reflects on teaching (the "**orangutan effect**"), urges students to take the job
they'd choose "if they had no need for money," and thanks Berkshire's "**til death do us part**" individual
shareholders. The meeting returns to Omaha (April 2022). (Closing pitch: a Jimmy Buffett pontoon boat made
by Forest River.)

## Key figures

- **Market value +29.6%** (S&P +28.7%); 1965–2021 compounded **20.1%**; 1964–2021 gain **3,641,613%**.
- **Four Giants:** insurance (float **$147B**), **Apple** (5.55%; $5.6B share of earnings, $785M
  dividends), **BNSF** (record **$6B**), **BHE** (record **$4B**, 91.1% owned). U.S. PP&E **$158B** (most
  of any U.S. company). Federal tax $3.3B (~$9M/day).
- **Equities** cost $104.6B / market **$350.7B**: Apple $161.2B, BofA $46.0B (12.8%), Amex $24.8B (19.9%),
  Coke $23.7B, Moody's $9.6B, BYD $7.7B, Chevron $4.5B; **Japanese trading houses** itemized (~5.5% each).
  Kraft Heinz 26.6% ($13.1B); Pilot 38.6%. **$10B Occidental** preferred (→ $10.7B).
- **Cash $144B** ($120B T-bills; $30B floor). **Buybacks: 9% of shares over 2020–21 for $51.7B**; float/
  A-share +25% to $99,497.
- **[[Paul Andrews Jr|Paul Andrews]]/[[TTI]] tribute** (died 2021): $500 start → $7.7B; the TTI→BNSF chain.
