# 2013 Shareholder Letter

## Summary

Net worth grew **$34.2 billion** (after $1.8B of "meaningless" charges from buying in the Marmon and
Iscar minorities); per-share book value rose **18.2%** to **$134,973** (49 years, **19.7%
compounded**) — but **underperformed a +32.4% S&P** by 14.2 points (the 10th shortfall in 49 years,
"all but one… when the S&P gain exceeded 15%"). The **120%-of-book buyback** authorized in 2012 went
unused (the price never fell that low) — "if it does, we will be aggressive."

**Two large acquisitions (~$18B).** **All of [[NV Energy]]** ($5.6B, via [[MidAmerican Energy]];
serves 88% of Nevada) and **a major interest in [[H.J. Heinz]]** ($8B of preferred at a 9% coupon
that "should increase… to 12% or so," plus half the common for $4.25B), partnered with **[[Jorge Paulo Lemann|3G Capital]]** (Bernardo Hees CEO, Alex Behring chairman). The Heinz "**partnership
template**" may be reused: Berkshire is the financing partner and "**never intends to sell a
share**." Heinz closed in June; $1.3B of one-time charges held back 2013 earnings.

**The "Powerhouse Five."** [[Burlington Northern Santa Fe|BNSF]], [[ISCAR]], [[Lubrizol]], [[Marmon Group]] and [[MidAmerican Energy]] earned a record **$10.8 billion pre-tax** (+$758M); the $10.4B
nine-year earnings gain came "with only minor dilution." **$3.5B** was spent on "the surest sort of
bolt-on" — buying the **remaining minorities of [[Marmon Group]] (→100%)** and **[[ISCAR]]** (the
Wertheimer family exercised its 20% put) — adding $300M pre-tax and $800M of cash, while a
"nonsensical" accounting rule forced a **$1.8B write-down** that *widened* intrinsic-vs-book by the
same amount. Twenty-five smaller bolt-ons were contracted for **$3.1B**; smaller non-insurance units
earned $4.7B pre-tax. Capex hit a record **$11 billion** (89% U.S.).

**Insurance.** An **eleventh consecutive underwriting profit** ($3.089B); float grew $41B → **$77.2
billion** over eleven years (cumulative UW profit **$22B**) — "all of this began with our 1967
purchase of [[National Indemnity Company|National Indemnity]] for $8.6 million." **[[GEICO]]** became
the **#2** auto insurer (passing Allstate; #7 in 1996); Tony Nicely (52 years); its economic goodwill
"approaching **$20 billion**" (carried at $1.4B). **[[Ajit Jain]]** (float **$37B**) launched
**[[Berkshire Hathaway Specialty Insurance]]** (BHSI) in June — a leap into **commercial insurance**
under Peter Eastwood, "instantly accepted" thanks to Berkshire's unmatched financial strength.
**[[General Re]]** (Tad Montross) "is now a gem." The Lloyd's/[[Equitas]] deal restated ("the world's
best mattress").

**The "two small investments" essay.** Buffett illustrates investing fundamentals with his 1986
**Nebraska farm** ($280,000 from the FDIC; ~10% yield; now worth 5×) and his 1993 **NYU-adjacent
retail property** (RTC sale; distributions now >35%/yr of cost). The lessons: you needn't be an
expert, only stay inside your **[[Circle of Competence]]** and judge an asset by its *future
productivity*, not its price quote; "**games are won by players who focus on the playing field — not
by those whose eyes are glued to the scoreboard**"; macro forecasting "is a waste of time"; and
**"a climate of fear is your friend; a euphoric world is your enemy."** Stocks' minute-to-minute
quotes are an *advantage* exploited only by those who treat a "mouthy neighbor's" wild prices as
opportunities (the **[[Mr. Market]]** farm-neighbor parable).

**The Big Four.** Berkshire's ownership of [[American Express]] (14.2%), [[Coca-Cola]] (9.1%),
[[IBM]] (6.3%) and [[Wells Fargo]] (9.2%) all rose — "each one-tenth of a percent… raises
Berkshire's share of their annual earnings by **$50 million**." 2013 attributable earnings $4.4B
(only $1.4B reported as dividends); unrealized gains **$39B**. "It's better to have a partial interest
in the **Hope diamond** than to own all of a rhinestone." **[[Todd Combs]] and [[Ted Weschler]]** each
now run **>$7 billion** and again beat both the S&P and Buffett ("by a lot," says Charlie).

**Regulated, capital-intensive ([[Burlington Northern Santa Fe|BNSF]] + [[MidAmerican Energy]]).**
BNSF interest coverage 9:1; ~15% of all U.S. intercity ton-miles; it spent a record **$4B** in 2013
(2× depreciation; "like Noah… we know it's our job to plan ahead"). Matt Rose **and Carl Ice** now
lead BNSF; **[[Greg Abel]]** leads MidAmerican, whose utilities span **11 states** (NV Energy added),
hold **7% of U.S. wind**, retain "**more dollars of earnings… than any other American electric
utility**," and ranked **#1 in customer satisfaction** (95.3% "very satisfied"); the renewables
portfolio will reach **$15 billion**. MidAmerican earnings to Berkshire **$1,470M**; BNSF net
**$3,793M** (revenues $22,014M). Employment hit a record **330,745** (incl. Heinz; HQ "still fits…
on one floor").

## Key figures

- **Book value/share:** $134,973, **+18.2%** (net worth +$34.2B, after $1.8B of minority-buy-in
  charges); 49 years, **19.7%**. Per-share investments $129,253 (+13.6%); non-insurance pre-tax EPS
  $9,116 (+12.8%). Capex record **$11B**.
- **Acquisitions (~$18B):** **NV Energy** ($5.6B) + **Heinz** ($8B preferred + $4.25B common, with 3G).
  **$3.5B** to take Marmon to 100% and Iscar to 100%. 25 bolt-ons for $3.1B.
- **Insurance:** **11th straight underwriting profit** ($3,089M: BH Reins $1,294M, Gen Re $283M,
  GEICO $1,127M, Other Primary $385M); **float $77,240M** (11-yr UW profit $22B). GEICO now **#2**;
  Ajit float $37B; **BHSI** launched.
- **Powerhouse five** pre-tax **$10.8B**. **BNSF:** revenues $22,014M, net $3,793M (coverage 9:1).
  **MidAmerican (89.8%):** earnings to Berkshire $1,470M; 11 states; renewables → $15B.
- **Big Four** ownership: Amex 14.2%, Coca-Cola 9.1%, IBM 6.3%, Wells Fargo 9.2%; attributable
  earnings $4.4B; unrealized gains **$39B**. **Combs & Weschler** each >$7B.
