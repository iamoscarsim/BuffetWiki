# 1999 Shareholder Letter

## Summary

Net worth grew just **$358 million**, lifting per-share book value a mere **0.5%** to
**$37,987** (35 years, 24.0% compounded) — "**the worst absolute performance of my
tenure and, compared to the S&P, the worst relative performance as well.**" Buffett takes
the blame: "My 'one subject' is **capital allocation**, and my grade for 1999 most
assuredly is a **D**." The damage came from Berkshire's **equity portfolio** — several
large investees ([[Coca-Cola]], [[Gillette]], [[American Express]] notwithstanding its
gains) "badly lagged the market … because they've had disappointing operating results."
The stock fell more than the business; Buffett expects intrinsic value to "**modestly
exceed**" the S&P over the next decade (no longer the "truly large superiorities" of the
past — size and high prices preclude them), and warns the S&P itself is a "virtual
certainty" to do "**far less well in the next decade or two than … since 1982**" (an
enclosed *Fortune* article). Two cash acquisitions advanced the operating side:
**[[Jordan's Furniture]]** and a major stake in **[[MidAmerican Energy]]** — "we bought
both for cash, issuing no Berkshire shares."

**Intrinsic value.** The two-column table: investments **$47,339/share** (little changed)
and operating earnings per share that "**fell apart**" to **−$458.55** — a *loss* — on a
huge underwriting loss at General Re. A standing wedge between intrinsic and book value is
the **~$500M/year goodwill amortization** charge, which shrinks book value while economic
goodwill usually grows. Book value is $57.8B, but intrinsic value "far exceeds" it — See's
and the Buffalo News alone "are now worth **fifteen to twenty times** the value at which
they are carried."

**A managerial story.** **[[Bill Child]]** of **[[R.C. Willey Home Furnishings|R.C. Willey]]** (a Mormon, whose stores never open Sunday) wanted to expand to Boise; skeptical
Buffett let him run his own business — and Child **personally built the ~$9M store**,
offering to sell it to Berkshire at cost only if it succeeded (and to eat the loss
himself, refusing even interest, if it failed). It succeeded; he turned it over and "**refused
to take a dime of interest**." "You can understand why the opportunity to partner with
people like Bill Child causes me to tap dance to work every morning."

**Insurance & float.** Float grew to **$25,298M** (GEICO $3.4B, General Re $15.2B, Other
Reinsurance $6.3B, Other Primary $0.4B), but 1999 brought a **$1.4 billion underwriting
loss** — a **5.8% cost of float** — almost all from an "aberrational" General Re result.
(Buffett "enthusiastically welcomed $400M of the loss" because it stems from business that
will deliver "exceptional float over the next decade.") **[[Ajit Jain]]** is showered with
praise — "**impossible to overstate Ajit's value**" — having "built from scratch an
outstanding reinsurance business … now hold[ing] $6.3 billion of float." At General Re,
incentive comp is now tied to **float growth and cost of float**; [[Ron Ferguson]] and his
team can still make it "the world's most profitable reinsurance company."

**GEICO.** "Exceptional progress." Market share rose **2.7% → 4.1%**; new policies
**1,648,095**, in-force **4,328,900**. Marketing reached **$242M** (vs. $33M in 1995) and
will hit **$300–350M** in 2000 ("we would happily commit $1 billion annually … if … the
last dollar … produce[d] new business at an attractive cost"). Profit-sharing was **28.4%
/ $113.3M**. Each GEICO household sends ~**$1,100/year**; in 1999 the customer base grew
**766,256** and generated **$590M of cash**. **[[Lorimer Davidson]]**, GEICO's former
chairman and Buffett's "friend, teacher and hero," **died in November** days after turning
97.

**Aviation services.** **[[FlightSafety International]]** (222 simulators, $215M capex —
very capital-intensive) and **[[Executive Jet]]/NetJets** (customers own >$2B of planes,
$4.2B on order; ~8% of all business jets; recurring revenue +46%) are "runaway leaders,"
still run by founders [[Al Ueltschi]] and [[Rich Santulli]]. EJA is expanding into Europe;
"**Buy a fraction, get a fleet.**" Charlie signed up — "No other endorsement could speak
more eloquently."

**Acquisitions.** Buffett mocks investment-banking "books" (the Superman riff; the 1985
[[Scott Fetzer]] deal where Munger said "I'll pay $2.5 million not to read it") — "our
carefully-crafted acquisition strategy is simply to **wait for the phone to ring**."
**[[Jordan's Furniture]]** (Barry and Eliot **[[Tatelman Brothers|Tatelman]]**; New
England's "shoppertainment" leader, family-run since 1927; highest sales per square foot
in the country; closed Nov 13) came via the Blumkin/Child/Wolff referral chain; the
Tatelmans gave employees **50¢/hour-ever-worked ($9M) from their own pockets**.
**[[MidAmerican Energy]]** (~$2B; Berkshire's first electric utility) came through director
**[[Walter Scott Jr]]**; structured around the **Public Utility Holding Company Act** to
take ~76% of equity but **just under 10% of the vote**, with CEO **[[David Sokol]]** and
Scott as controlling shareholder. "If we do [make additional utility commitments], the
amounts … could be large."

**Acquisition accounting.** Buffett wades into the **purchase-vs-pooling** fight: goodwill
amortization is "usually spurious" (economic goodwill, like land or See's, often *grows*
— "for 78 years" at See's), but **pooling is also a fiction** ("there is indisputably an
acquirer and an acquiree"). His proposal: record the purchase at fair value, carry the
goodwill **without amortization**, and write it down **only if impaired**. Tax structure
matters enormously — Jordan's was bought as **assets for cash** (tax-deductible goodwill
amortized over 15 years); a **stock-for-stock** deal is "the worst of all" (no basis
step-up). "We have never done an inefficiently-structured deal … to make our figures look
better."

**Portfolio & valuation.** Few changes; itemized over-$750M holdings led by Coca-Cola
($11.65B) and **Amex ($8.4B)**. Buffett restates **why Berkshire owns no tech stocks**:
"we have no insights into which participants in the tech field possess a truly **durable
competitive advantage**" — staying inside the **circle of competence**. He warns equities
are "wildly optimistic": with GDP ~3% real + 2% inflation → ~5% profit growth, future
equity returns will be "dramatically less than most investors … expect," and "the market
adjustment is apt to be severe." "Fools give you reasons, wise men never try."

**Share repurchases.** A buyback makes sense only when a company has **funds beyond its
needs** *and* its stock sells **below conservatively-calculated intrinsic value**, with
shareholders fully informed. Buffett condemns repurchases meant to "pump or support the
stock price" or to offset option exercises ("buy high, sell low") — "buying dollar bills
for $1.10 is not good business." He admits he has erred by *not* repurchasing at times;
with the A shares recently below **$45,000**, Berkshire considered buying but will wait
until shareholders read the report (and would buy only at/below the NYSE bid). "We will
never make purchases with the intention of stemming a decline."

**Misc.** Manufacturing/retail/service had "excellent results" except **[[Dexter Shoe]]**
— hammered as **93% of U.S. shoe purchases now come from abroad** (a domestic-manufacturing
problem, "not … managerial"). **[[See's Candies]]** set a record **24% operating margin**
($857M pre-tax since the $25M 1972 purchase) under **[[Chuck Huggins]]** ("Huggins' Law":
pre-tax profit in $M ≈ his age %). SDC reached **$17.2M** (cumulative 19-year $147M).

## Key figures

- **Book value/share:** $37,987, **+0.5%** (net worth +$358M) — the **worst year** of
  Buffett's tenure, absolute and relative. 35 years, 24.0% compounded.
- **Two-column table:** investments **$47,339/share** (little changed); pre-tax operating
  earnings ex-investments **−$458.55/share** (a loss, on Gen Re's underwriting hit).
- **Insurance:** float **$25,298M** (GEICO $3,444M / Gen Re $15,166M / Other Reins $6,285M /
  Other Primary $403M); **$1.4B underwriting loss**, cost of float **5.8%** (~$400M of it
  "welcomed"). Ajit's reinsurance holds **$6.3B** float.
- **GEICO:** market share **2.7% → 4.1%**; in-force **4,328,900**; marketing **$242M**
  (2000: $300–350M); profit-sharing **$113.3M / 28.4%**.
- **Operating earnings** $1,085M pre-tax / **$671M to Berkshire**; capital gains $1,365M /
  $886M; **total $2,450M / $1,557M**.
- **Look-through earnings:** **$1,926M** (reported $1,318M + $707M investee retained − $99M
  tax). See's record **24%** margin; $857M pre-tax since 1972.
- **Common stocks:** cost $8,203M / **market $37,008M**. Coca-Cola $11,650M, Amex $8,402M
  (11.3%), Gillette $3,954M, Freddie Mac $2,803M, Wells Fargo $2,391M (3.6%, post-Norwest),
  WaPo $960M.
- **Acquisitions (cash):** Jordan's Furniture (closed 11/13/99); MidAmerican Energy (~$2B,
  ~76% equity / <10% vote). **SDC:** $17.2M (cumulative $147M).
