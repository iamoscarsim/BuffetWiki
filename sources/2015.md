# 2015 Shareholder Letter

## Summary

Net worth grew **$15.4 billion**; per-share book value rose **6.4%** to **$155,501** (51 years,
**19.2% compounded**) — beating a roughly flat S&P (+1.4%). Buffett reiterates that with the shift to
owning whole businesses (whose "winners are never revalued upwards"), **intrinsic value now far
exceeds book** — hence the standing willingness to buy back stock at up to **120% of book**. Market-
value gain since 1964: **1,598,284%**.

**BNSF's turnaround — the year's most important development.** After 2014's service failures,
**[[Burlington Northern Santa Fe|BNSF]]** spent a record **$5.8 billion** on capex (≈3× depreciation;
"far and away the record for any American railroad") and "dramatically improved its service." It moves
~**17% of U.S. intercity freight** (45% more ton-miles than its closest competitor), and — while
rivals' volumes and earnings fell — lifted pre-tax income to a record **$6.8 billion** (+$606M; Matt
Rose and Carl Ice).

**The "Powerhouse Five" → Six.** BNSF, [[MidAmerican Energy|Berkshire Hathaway Energy]], [[Marmon Group]], [[Lubrizol]] and [[ISCAR|IMC]] earned **$13.1 billion** (+$650M; +$12.7B over twelve years
with minor dilution). Next year's newcomer: **[[Precision Castparts]]** ("PCC"), bought "a month ago
for more than **$32 billion of cash**" — the world's premier aerospace-components supplier (CEO **Mark
Donegan**, "the da Vinci of his craft"; 30,466 employees, 162 plants, 13 countries), brought to
Buffett's attention by **[[Todd Combs]]**. With PCC, Berkshire owns **10¼** would-be Fortune 500
companies ([[Kraft Heinz]] the ¼). Smaller non-insurance units earned **$5.7B**.

**Capex & renewables.** A record **$16 billion** of property/plant/equipment (86% U.S.). [[MidAmerican Energy|BHE]] has invested $16B in renewables — now **7% of U.S. wind, 6% of U.S. solar** (4,423 MW of
wind, 6× the runner-up utility) — and made major commitments tied to the **Paris Climate Conference**.

**Kraft Heinz.** The [[H.J. Heinz]] partnership with **[[Jorge Paulo Lemann|3G]]** "more than doubled
its size" by **merging with Kraft** ([[Kraft Foods|Kraft]]). Berkshire now owns **325.4 million shares
(~27%) of [[Kraft Heinz]]** (cost $9.8B; sales $27B) plus **$8B of preferred** ($720M/yr, to be
redeemed for **$8.32B in June 2016**). GAAP forced a **$6.8B write-up** on the merger. Buffett
contrasts the two philosophies: 3G's "extraordinarily successful" method is to **buy and ruthlessly
cut costs** (boosting productivity); Berkshire's is to buy already-lean businesses run by efficient
managers and leave them alone ("**If you want to guarantee yourself a lifetime of misery, marry
someone with the intent of changing their behavior**" — Munger). Berkshire will keep partnering with
3G (and others, as at [[Berkadia Commercial Mortgage|Berkadia]]) — but **only in friendly deals**:
"At Berkshire, we go only where we are welcome."

**Insurance.** A **13th consecutive underwriting profit** (~$1.8B) atop float that grew $41B → **$88
billion** (13-year cumulative underwriting profit **$26.2 billion**) — "Berkshire's largest unrecorded
wealth lies in its insurance business… it can't be replicated." **[[National Indemnity Company]]** is
"the **largest property-casualty company in the world**, as measured by net worth." Underwriting (2015):
[[Ajit Jain|BH Reinsurance]] $421M (float **$44,108M**; Mike Goldberg "achieved sainthood" by handing
Ajit the reinsurance keys in 1986), [[General Re]] $132M ("now a gem"), [[GEICO]] $460M (share **11.4%**
from 2.5% in 1995; Tony Nicely, 54 years; profit dented by rising accident frequency/severity), Other
Primary (the commercial group + **[[Berkshire Hathaway Specialty Insurance|BHSI]]**, already at **$1B
of premium** in under three years; Peter Eastwood "a home run"). A headwind named: **prolonged low
interest rates** will steadily depress earnings on float "for many years to come."

**Intrinsic value — a methodology change.** Per-share cash + investments rose 8.3% to **$159,794**
(Kraft Heinz at market); the second pillar — earnings from non-investment businesses — rose 2.1% to
**$12,304/share**, and **for the first time includes insurance underwriting income** ($1,118/share in
2015; 10-year average $1,434/share), reflecting Berkshire's shift away from volatile catastrophe
coverage toward stable "bread-and-butter" lines. Since 1970: per-share investments +18.9%/yr, earnings
+23.7%/yr.

**"Bet on America."** A sustained optimism essay: U.S. GDP per capita (~$56,000) is "six times the
amount in 1930"; even 2% real growth compounds to a **$19,000** per-capita gain in a generation; "the
babies being born in America today are the **luckiest crop in history**"; "for 240 years it's been a
terrible mistake to bet against America, and now is no time to start." **Big Four** ownership all rose
([[American Express]] 15.6%, [[Coca-Cola]] 9.3%, [[IBM]] 8.4%, [[Wells Fargo]] 9.8%); attributable
earnings $4.7B (dividends $1.8B). Bolt-ons: 29 for $634M. **[[Todd Combs]] and [[Ted Weschler]]** now
each manage ~**$9 billion**.

## Key figures

- **Book value/share:** $155,501, **+6.4%** (net worth +$15.4B); 51 years, **19.2%**. Market-value
  gain since 1964 **1,598,284%**. Per-share cash+investments **$159,794** (+8.3%); per-share business
  earnings (now incl. underwriting) **$12,304** (+2.1%).
- **Acquisition:** **Precision Castparts** (~**$32B cash**; closed early 2016 → "Powerhouse Six").
  **Kraft Heinz** formed (Berkshire ~27%, cost $9.8B, + $8B preferred; $6.8B GAAP write-up).
- **Powerhouse five** **$13.1B** pre-tax. **BNSF** record pre-tax **$6.8B** ($5.8B capex). Capex
  record **$16B**. **BHE**: 7% U.S. wind / 6% U.S. solar.
- **Insurance:** **13th straight underwriting profit** (~$1.8B; float **$87,722M**; 13-yr cumulative
  $26.2B). UW: BH Reins $421M, Gen Re $132M, GEICO $460M, Other Primary (incl. BHSI). GEICO share
  **11.4%**; NICO the world's largest P/C insurer by net worth.
- **Big Four** ownership up (Amex 15.6%, Coke 9.3%, IBM 8.4%, WFC 9.8%); attributable earnings $4.7B.
  **Combs & Weschler** each ~$9B.
