# 1985 Shareholder Letter

## Summary

Written early 1986. After a quiet 1984, "something big" popped up — repeatedly. Net
worth grew **$613.6M (+48.2%)** ("It is fitting that the visit of Halley's Comet
coincided with this percentage gain: neither will be seen again in my lifetime"),
lifting book value to $1,643.71 (23.2% compounded over 21 years). Buffett warns this
is unrepeatable: equities are no longer cheap (the market is "180 degrees" from a
decade ago), and **size** is "an iron law" dampening returns — earning 15% over the
next decade would need ~$5.7 billion of profit. He also flags that Berkshire's stock,
historically at a discount to [[Intrinsic Business Value]], now trades at a slight
*premium* — good for past owners, a caution for new ones — and reiterates that "in
the end, investor gains must equal business gains." He praises his individual-
shareholder base and mocks institutions and efficient-market academics (the Ben
Graham "oil discovered in hell" story).

The letter's set-pieces:

**Shutdown of the textile business** ([[Berkshire Hathaway Textiles]]): closed in
1985 after 21 years. Buffett delivers the definitive post-mortem — the
**Burlington Industries** comparison ($3 billion of capex over 21 years to end up
worth less in real terms), the lesson that "a good managerial record... is far more
a function of what business boat you get into than... how effectively you row," and
the brutal **auction postscript**: textile equipment that cost $13M (replaceable new
for $30–50M) fetched **$163,122 gross — net less than zero**; looms bought at $5,000
sold for $26 as scrap. "The economic goodwill attributable to two paper routes in
Buffalo — or a single See's candy store — considerably exceeds the proceeds we
received." Praises managers Ken Chace and Garry Morrison and the cooperative union.

**Executive compensation** (see [[Executive Compensation]]): a sustained critique of
ten-year fixed-price stock options, which reward managers merely for *retaining*
earnings (the savings-account-with-trustee analogy) and ignore the cost of capital —
"a managerial Rip Van Winkle... could not wish for a better incentive system." He
lays out Berkshire's alternative: reward unit results in each manager's own
"bailiwick," uncapped, non-hierarchical, unrelated to Berkshire's stock price.

**Three very good businesses** ([[Nebraska Furniture Mart]], [[See's Candies]],
[[Buffalo Evening News]]): aggregate pre-tax earnings rose from ~$8M (15 years ago)
to **$72M**, needing only ~$40M more capital — a vivid demonstration of
[[Economic Goodwill]] versus the "average American business" needing ~$5 of capital
per $1 of new earnings.

New deals and holdings: Berkshire bought **3 million shares of [[Capital Cities]]/ABC
at $172.50** (~$517.5M), financing its $3.5B ABC purchase and aligning with Tom
Murphy and Dan Burke under a long voting agreement — while Buffett wryly notes he had
*sold* the same stock at $43 in 1978–80 ("A little more time, please"). It acquired
**[[Scott Fetzer]]** (~$320M; 17 businesses including World Book and Kirby; CEO
[[Ralph Schey]]). It entered a **[[Fireman's Fund]]** 7% quota-share (following
[[Jack Byrne]], who left [[GEICO]]) and **sold [[General Foods]]** to Philip Morris
for a $338M gain. The **[[Washington Post]]** "triple dip" is celebrated — bought in
1973 at ¼ of value, lifted by business growth, buybacks, and a narrowing discount, to
$221M — a tribute to [[Katharine Graham]]. Insurance hardened (premium volume
tripled; [[Financial Strength]] now a real edge; National Indemnity's large-risk ad
drew $50M); the combined ratio improved to 111, though under-reserving persisted
("the real mistake is not the act, but the actor"; the "Except-For Insurance
Company"). And Buffett converts to **Cherry Coke** — foreshadowing 1988.

## Key figures
- Net worth +$613.6M (**+48.2%**); book value/share **$1,643.71** (21 yrs, 23.2%).
- Berkshire combined ratio improved 134 → **111**; premium volume tripled.
- Total common stocks: cost $275M, market **$1,198M**. GEICO (38%) market **$596M**;
  Washington Post market $205M; Cap Cities/ABC $109M (pre full purchase).
- General Foods sale: ~$338M pre-tax gain.
- Scott Fetzer: ~$320M for 17 businesses, ~$700M sales.
- WPPSS bonds: $194M (amortized cost), $62M unrealized gain, ~$30M tax-exempt income.
