# 1988 Shareholder Letter

## Summary

Written February 1989. Net worth grew **$569M (+20.0%)**, lifting book value to
**$2,974.52** (23.0% compounded over 24 years). Buffett opens with sobering candor on
**size**: four years ago a 15% decade return needed $3.9B of profit; now it needs
**$10.3B** — "a very big number." The market is less attractive, taxes higher,
acquisitions richer, and the three permanent holdings (about half of net worth) face
less favorable industry conditions. The offset is the quality of the businesses: "the
**[[Sainted Seven]]**… the Saints came marching in," earning about **67% on average
equity** with no leverage.

A long **Accounting Changes** essay defends Berkshire's reporting philosophy: GAAP is
"a beginning rather than an end" to informing owners, and good disclosure should let a
reader answer three questions — *how much is it worth, can it meet its obligations, how
good a job are the managers doing?* New rules forcing full consolidation (of
[[Mutual Savings and Loan]] and Scott Fetzer Financial) push Berkshire to publish
**four-sector** supplementary statements. He warns about GAAP abuse — earnings
smoothing, the "big bath," and outright fraud: "It has been far safer to steal large
sums with a pen than small sums with a gun." A 1990 deferred-tax rule change will cut
reported net worth by ~$71M (re-striking the deferred-tax liability on unrealized gains
at 34%).

The marquee event is **[[Borsheim's]]** — an 80% interest bought in early 1989 in the
Omaha jewelry store run by the **Friedman family** (headed by [[Ike Friedman]]),
another branch of Mrs. B's family (her sister Rebecca Friedman). Bought, like NFM, on a
one-page contract with no audit — "If you don't know jewelry, know the jeweler." It
shares the Blumkin formula: single huge-inventory store, rapid turnover, shrewd buying,
rock-bottom expenses, "sell cheap and tell the truth."

Operating updates: **NFM** — Mrs. B (**95**, 51 years a boss) "and her cart roll on";
a new Clearance Center; rival Dillard's declined to sell furniture in Omaha ("We don't
want to compete with them… they are about the best there is"); NFM is the Omaha
World-Herald's largest ROP advertiser. **Buffalo News** — [[Stan Lipsey]] "made me look
foolish," *improving* margins despite Buffett's forecast; news hole 49.5%. **See's** —
record 25.1M lbs; **90% of profit earned in December** ($29M of $32.5M pre-tax).
**Fechheimer** — a "fairly good-sized acquisition" Buffett and Munger "okayed… without
even looking at it." **Scott Fetzer** — Campbell Hausfeld (air compressors) doubled
earnings since 1986; World Book reached the Soviet Union.

**Insurance**: combined ratio **104**; favorable reserve development for a second year;
premium volume falling (the [[Fireman's Fund]] quota-share expires Aug 1989), but the
**float-to-premium ratio** will run ~3× the industry, so profits should beat peers. The
team is strengthening — "Mike Goldberg, with special help from **[[Ajit Jain]]**, Dinos
Iordanou, and the National Indemnity team" (Jain's first mention). Buffett dissects the
industry's poor economics and unpopularity — auto insurers are vilified for sub-cost
price hikes while breakfast-cereal makers (Kellogg, General Mills at 5× book) raise
prices freely — and flags **Proposition 103** in California as a serious threat to
[[GEICO]] (now **44%-owned**), which has uniquely large [[Economic Goodwill]] at risk.

**Marketable securities**: two big new long-term commitments — **[[Coca-Cola]]**
(14,172,500 shares, cost $592.5M) and **[[Freddie Mac]]** preferred (held via Mutual
Savings, the legal maximum) — held under the doctrine that "our favorite holding period
is **forever**" (Peter Lynch's "cutting the flowers and watering the weeds"; Mae West's
"too much of a good thing can be wonderful" for [[Concentration]]). The three permanent
holdings are unchanged. A pointed essay on **CEO accountability** argues it is "far
easier for an inadequate CEO to keep his job than… an inadequate subordinate" — no
performance standard, a congenial board, "the boss shoots the arrow… then paints the
bullseye." [[WPPSS Bonds]] now rated AA– ($247M cost / $352M market); [[Salomon]]
preferred now valued just over cost.

The **[[Arbitrage]]** essay is a centerpiece: a record **$78M pre-tax on ~$147M**, the
four questions for any deal, and two stories — the 1954 Rockwood cocoa-bean tender
(Jay Pritzker; Buffett at 24 at Graham-Newman) and the Arcata/redwood saga (a 15%
base return plus a windfall on the disputed timber claim). The big RJR Nabisco position
($281.8M) yielded $64M as KKR won. He pairs it with an attack on **Efficient Market
Theory**: a 63-year (1926–88) arbitrage record across Graham-Newman, Buffett
Partnership and Berkshire averaging **>20%** vs. the market's ~10% — "$1,000 would have
grown to $405,000… A 20% rate of return… would have produced $97 million." EMT's
disservice to students "has been an extraordinary service to us."

Also: Berkshire **listed on the NYSE** (Nov 29, 1988; specialist Henderson Brothers),
with the unusual goals of trading *near* intrinsic value and attracting low-turnover,
long-term owners; S&P raised Berkshire to **AAA** (one of only 16 such U.S. industrials/
insurers) amid bondholder "event risk"; and a warm eulogy for **[[David Dodd]]**,
Buffett's Columbia teacher and Ben Graham's *Security Analysis* co-author, who died at 93.

## Key figures
- Net worth +$569M (**+20.0%**); book value/share **$2,974.52** (24 yrs, 23.0%).
- Operating earnings to Berkshire **$313.4M**; total earnings $399.3M.
- [[Sainted Seven]] (plus small units) earned ~**67% on average equity**, unleveraged.
- Berkshire combined ratio (ex-structured settlements/financial reins.) **104**.
- Holdings >$100M: Cap Cities/ABC **$1,086.8M**, [[Coca-Cola]] **$632.4M** (cost $592.5M),
  [[Freddie Mac]] pfd **$121.2M** (cost $71.7M), GEICO **$849.4M**, WaPo **$364.1M**.
- Arbitrage: **$78M pre-tax** on ~$147M average funds; RJR position $281.8M (→$64M gain).
- 63-year arbitrage record averaged **>20%** vs. ~10% market.
- WPPSS bonds: cost **$247M** / market **$352M**, now rated AA–. Medium-term tax-exempts ~$800M.
- S&P credit rating raised to **AAA**.
