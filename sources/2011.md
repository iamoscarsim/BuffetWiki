# 2011 Shareholder Letter

## Summary

A modest year: per-share book value rose **4.6%** to **$99,860** (47 years, **19.8% compounded**).
The letter opens on **succession** — "the primary job of a Board… is to see that the right people are
running the business" — reporting that **[[Todd Combs]]** joined as an investment manager at the
start of 2011 and **[[Ted Weschler]]** would arrive just after year-end (each handling a few billion,
but able to run the whole portfolio later); the CEO successor is "an individual… whose managerial and
human qualities they admire" (unnamed; with two backups). Buffett notes >98% of his net worth is in
Berkshire stock, all bound for philanthropy.

**Lubrizol & the "fabulous five."** On September 16 Berkshire bought **[[Lubrizol]]**, a worldwide
specialty-chemicals maker, run by **[[James Hambrick]]** (who lifted pre-tax profit from $147M to
$1,085M since 2004); three bolt-ons ($493M) were already agreed. Each of Berkshire's **five largest
non-insurance businesses — BNSF, ISCAR, Lubrizol, Marmon, MidAmerican — set record operating
earnings**, together >$9B pre-tax (vs. $393M from the only one owned seven years earlier,
MidAmerican); 2012 should top $10B. Capex hit a record **$8.2B** (95% in the U.S.).

**Two big marketable-security moves.** A **$5 billion 6% [[Bank of America]] preferred** with
warrants to buy **700 million shares at $7.14** (until Sept 2, 2021), and **63.9 million shares of
[[IBM]]** for **$10.9 billion** (5.5%). Counting IBM, the **"Big Four"** are now Amex (13.0%),
Coca-Cola (8.8%), IBM (5.5%) and Wells Fargo (7.6%) — viewed as "**partnership interests in
wonderful businesses**." Their look-through math: $862M of dividends but $3.3B of attributable
earnings ($2.4B unreported, fueling future value).

**The bad news.** The **[[Energy Future Holdings]]** bonds (~$2B, a bet tied to natural-gas prices
that "tanked") were "a **big mistake**… a major unforced error" — written down $1B (2010) + $390M
(2011), carried at $878M. Three crisis securities ([[Swiss Re]], [[Goldman Sachs]], [[General Electric]]) were **called away for $12.8 billion**, ending ~$1.2B of pre-tax income (Lubrizol
offset most). And on housing: "**I was dead wrong**" about a recovery — the five housing-related
companies ([[Clayton Homes]], Acme Brick, Shaw, Johns Manville, MiTek) earned just $513M (vs. $1.8B
in 2006), employing 43,315 (down from 58,769). But "household formations now exceed housing starts…
**America's best days lie ahead**."

**Intrinsic value & the buyback doctrine.** Per-share investments $98,366 (+4%); per-share
non-insurance pre-tax earnings $6,990 (+18%); eight subsidiaries would each make the Fortune 500. In
September Berkshire **announced a buyback at up to 110% of book value** (bought just $67M before the
price ran away). The doctrine (see [[Evolving Views — Buybacks]]): repurchase only when (1) ample
funds remain and (2) the stock sells "at a material discount to… intrinsic business value,
conservatively calculated" — "**what is smart at one price is dumb at another**" (Jamie Dimon
praised); never below a **$20 billion cash floor** ("financial strength that is unquestionable takes
precedence over all else"). The linked **net-buyer lesson** via IBM: a long-term holder of a
share-repurchasing company should *wish the stock to languish* so buybacks retire more shares — "low
prices became my friend" after reading Chapter 8 of Graham's *The Intelligent Investor*.

**Insurance.** A **ninth consecutive underwriting profit** (~$17B over nine years), but a slim
**$248M** in a heavy-catastrophe year (Japan/NZ earthquakes, Thai floods): [[Ajit Jain|BH Reinsurance]] **−$714M**, [[General Re]] $144M, [[GEICO]] $576M, Other Primary $242M. **Float hit a
record $70,571M** (from $41B nine years earlier) — but is "unlikely to grow much" from here. A key
intrinsic-value point: float is fully *deducted* as a liability, yet costless, long-enduring float's
true value is far lower, and **$15.5B of insurance goodwill** is carried far below its real worth —
"a huge reason" intrinsic value exceeds book. [[Ajit Jain]]'s book holds **$34B of float**; a $250B
industry mega-cat "would likely [leave] Berkshire… a moderate profit" while rivals face insolvency —
"Charlie would gladly trade me for a second Ajit." [[General Re]] (Tad Montross) and the **four
underwriting disciplines** — "in the first few years… a major headache. Now it's a treasure."
**[[GEICO]]** (Tony Nicely, 50 years of service) lifted share **2.0% → 9.3%** under his 18-year
tenure (premium $15.4B vs. the $3.3B a static share would imply). **Princeton Insurance** (med-mal,
NJ) was a year-end bolt-on under [[Medical Protective]]'s Tim Kenesey (~$600M of float).

**Regulated, capital-intensive ([[Burlington Northern Santa Fe|BNSF]] + [[MidAmerican Energy]]).**
BNSF's 2011 interest coverage was 9.5×; it moves ~37% of rail ton-miles (~15% of *all* U.S.
intercity freight) over 23,000 miles of track, 13,000 bridges, 6,900 locomotives, 78,600 cars
(despite Missouri-River flooding); capex $1.8B above depreciation. "**Take care of your customer, and
the regulator… will take care of you.**" MidAmerican (Berkshire **89.8%**; now run by **[[Greg Abel]]**
after [[David Sokol]]'s 2011 departure) serves 2.5M electric customers and 8% of U.S. gas, with both
pipelines ranked #1/#2; it will have **3,316 MW of wind by end-2012** ($6B) plus two **solar**
projects (~$3B: California 100%, Arizona 49%). BNSF + MidAmerican carry **$20B of goodwill**, far
below intrinsic value.

**Manufacturing, Service & Retailing.** Revenues **$72,406M**; pre-tax $5,037M (incl. Lubrizol from
Sept 16). Non-housing units roared back ($4,387M ex-Lubrizol). Records at **[[CTB]]**, **[[TTI]]**
(sales $2.1B), **[[ISCAR]]** (Tungaloy set a record despite tsunami damage), **[[McLane]]** ($370M),
**[[Marmon Group]]** (Frank Ptak), **[[See's Candies]]** ($83M pre-tax, $1.65B cumulative on a
$25M cost — "buy commodities, sell brands"), and **[[Nebraska Furniture Mart]]** (which bought a
433-acre Dallas tract for a future record store). NetJets earned **$227M** under new CEO Jordan
Hansell and is entering China. A Marmon accounting oddity: the 2011 step-up to 80% forced a
"meaningless" **$614M write-off**, *widening* the intrinsic-vs-book gap. Buffett reaffirms **Rule 11**
— Berkshire keeps poor performers "through thick and thin" absent a permanent cash drain or labor
strife.

**Finance.** Leasing (XTRA, CORT) recovered ($13M → $155M pre-tax over three years); [[Clayton Homes]] stayed profitable through a manufactured-housing "depression" (sales 51,606, vs. 146,744 in
2005) on "old-fashioned loan policies." Common stocks: cost $48,209M / market **$76,991M**; few
changes besides IBM, Bank of America and a $1B add to [[Wells Fargo]].

## Key figures

- **Book value/share:** $99,860, **+4.6%** (47 years, **19.8% compounded**). Per-share investments
  $98,366; non-insurance pre-tax EPS $6,990.
- **Insurance:** **9th straight underwriting profit** (a slim **$248M**: BH Reins −$714M, Gen Re
  $144M, GEICO $576M, Other Primary $242M); **record float $70,571M**; insurance goodwill $15.5B.
  GEICO share **9.3%**; Ajit float $34B.
- **Acquisition:** **Lubrizol** (~$9B, closed Sept 16; James Hambrick). The "fabulous five" >$9B
  pre-tax. **Bank of America** $5B preferred + 700M warrants; **IBM** $10.9B (5.5%).
- **Mistake:** **Energy Future Holdings** bonds ($2B; written to $878M; "major unforced error").
- **BNSF:** revenues $19,548M, net $2,972M; interest coverage 9.5×. **MidAmerican (89.8%):** earnings
  to Berkshire $1,204M; wind 3,316 MW / $6B; +$3B in solar. Combined goodwill $20B.
- **Buybacks:** announced at up to **110% of book value** ($67M bought); **$20B cash floor**.
- **Common stocks** (cost $48,209M / market **$76,991M**): Coca-Cola $13,994M, **IBM $11,751M (new)**,
  Wells Fargo $11,024M, Amex $7,151M, P&G $4,829M, Kraft $2,953M, Munich Re $2,464M, Wal-Mart $2,333M,
  US Bancorp $2,112M, ConocoPhillips $2,121M, J&J $2,060M. (Plus the Bank of America preferred/warrants.)
