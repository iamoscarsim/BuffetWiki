# 1987 Shareholder Letter

## Summary

Written February 1988, months after the October 1987 crash. Net worth grew **$464M
(+19.5%)**, lifting book value to **$2,477.47** (23.1% compounded over 23 years).
Buffett stresses that business value, not book, is what counts — and at Berkshire the
two have tracked closely, the premium widening because "we own some remarkable
businesses and they are run by even more remarkable managers."

The letter's signature contribution is the **[[Sainted Seven]]** — Berkshire's seven
largest non-financial units ([[Buffalo Evening News]], [[Fechheimer Brothers]],
[[Kirby]], [[Nebraska Furniture Mart]], [[Scott Fetzer]] Manufacturing Group,
[[See's Candies]], [[World Book]]). Together they earned **$180M pre-tax on ~$175M of
equity with trivial debt** — about a **57% after-tax return on equity**, a figure
that (per a *Fortune* study Buffett cites) almost no large company approaches. The
same *Fortune* study found only **25 of 1,000** big companies averaged ROE over 20%
across 1977–86 with no year below 15%; 24 of the 25 beat the S&P. Most used little
leverage and sold "rather mundane" products "in much the same manner as they did ten
years ago" — confirming that "making the most of an already strong business franchise…
is what usually produces exceptional economics." Berkshire's managers "produced
extraordinary results by doing rather ordinary things — but doing them exceptionally
well." Buffett and Munger's contribution is "applause… informed applause."

Operating updates: **NFM** — Mrs. B at **94**, "gathering speed," prompting Buffett to
"scrap our mandatory retirement-at-100 policy"; sales $142.6M (+8%); the "A Competitor"
letter begging NFM to raise prices. **Buffalo News** — seven fresh editions every 24
hours (the obituary page redone seven times a day), a steady ~50% news hole, and
Buffett's rejection of **flexible operating budgets** (he won't cut the news hole or
See's quality when profits dip, nor add bloat when they boom — "occasionally a man
must rise above principle," re the jet). **See's** — a record ~25M lbs; same-store
poundage flat (an improvement); ~85% of profit earned in December; the customer revolt
when 14 pieces were culled. **Scott Fetzer** (19 businesses under [[Ralph Schey]]) —
pre-tax +10% while capital employed fell; World Book's most-revised edition since 1962.

**Insurance**: combined ratio **105** (vs. 103 in 1986), but profits improved on far
more **float**, and the float-to-premium ratio will keep rising. Buffett names
Berkshire's two escapes from commodity economics — **financial strength** ("hard for
an empty sack to stand upright") and **total indifference to volume** ("price-based-on-
exposure, not-on-competition"; willing to write 5× or ⅕ as much). He skewers the
**auditor's opinion letter** for certifying loss-reserve figures it cannot verify
("for them, long-tail insurance is heaven" for charlatans). The Tax Reform Act of 1986
(and a 1987 follow-on cutting the dividends-received deduction to 70%) keeps hurting.

The centerpiece essay is **[[Mr. Market]]** — Buffett's fullest telling of Ben
Graham's allegory: the manic-depressive partner who quotes a price daily and "doesn't
mind being ignored," whose "pocketbook, not his wisdom" is useful. "In the short run
the market is a voting machine but in the long run it is a weighing machine." "If
you've been in the game 30 minutes and you don't know who the patsy is, you're the
patsy." He applies it to the 1987 crash and **portfolio insurance** ($60–90B on a
"hair trigger"), arguing volatility helps the true investor. He details the three
**[[Permanent Ownership|permanent holdings]]** ([[Capital Cities]]/ABC $1,035M, [[GEICO]]
$756.9M, [[Washington Post]] $323.1M) — worth >$2B yet contributing only $11M of
reported earnings vs. >$100M of [[Look-Through Earnings|look-through earnings]], the
"accounting schizophrenia." And he confesses it took him "20 years to recognize how
important it was to buy good businesses": "we try to buy… an outstanding business at a
sensible price, not a mediocre business at a bargain price" (see
[[Good Business at a Fair Price]]). A long passage argues most **CEOs are poor at
capital allocation** — having risen via marketing or production, they face it like "a
highly-talented musician… named Chairman of the Federal Reserve" (see
[[Capital Allocation]]).

Other holdings: ~$900M of medium-term tax-exempts; a renewed **aversion to long-term
bonds** (the trade deficit, Blanche DuBois's "kindness of strangers," inflation as the
debtor's "H-bomb"); added [[WPPSS Bonds]] ($240M cost / $316M market); **arbitrage**
(historically ~25% pre-tax; the Allegis position; see [[Arbitrage]]); Texaco
post-bankruptcy bonds; and the year's biggest move — a **$700M [[Salomon]] Inc 9%
convertible preferred**, backing CEO [[John Gutfreund]]. **Financing**: $250M of 2018
debentures, with Buffett's debt creed — seek "acceptable long-term results under
extraordinarily adverse conditions, rather than optimal results under a normal range"
— and "finance in anticipation of need": "if you want to shoot rare, fast-moving
elephants, you should always carry a loaded gun" (see [[Financial Strength]]). The
*déjà vu* close: [[Harry Bottle]], Munger's fixer from the 1962 Dempster Mill days,
recalled as CEO of K&W Products and lifting its profits 300%.

## Key figures
- Net worth +$464M (**+19.5%**); book value/share **$2,477.47** (23 yrs, 23.1%).
- Operating earnings to Berkshire **$214.7M** (vs. $131.5M); total earnings $234.6M.
- **[[Sainted Seven]]**: ~$180M pre-tax on ~$175M equity → ~**57% after-tax ROE**.
- Berkshire combined ratio (ex-structured settlements/financial reins.) **105** (vs. 103).
- Permanent holdings market value: Cap Cities/ABC **$1,035M**, GEICO **$756.9M**,
  WaPo **$323.1M** — >$2B total, but only **$11M** reported earnings vs. >$100M look-through.
- [[Salomon]] preferred: **$700M**, 9%, convertible at $38 after 3 yrs.
- WPPSS bonds: amortized cost **$240M** / market **$316M**, $34M annual tax-exempt income.
- Medium-term tax-exempts ~$900M; $250M of 2018 debentures issued (~10% cost).
- WaPo bought 1973 at **$5.63**/sh (1987 EPS $10.30); GEICO avg **$6.67**/sh (EPS $9.01).
