# 2016 Shareholder Letter

## Summary

Net worth grew **$27.5 billion**; per-share book value rose **10.7%** to **$172,108** (52 years,
**19% compounded**). Buffett opens by formalizing what the [[Intrinsic Business Value|book-vs-intrinsic]]
gap has become: because GAAP requires writing "losers" down while "winners are never revalued upwards,"
the large and growing **unrecorded gains** at Berkshire's best businesses make intrinsic value "far
exceed" book — "truly huge" in the property/casualty operation. Over 52 years the **market-price gain
(1,972,595%)** has accordingly outrun the book-value gain (884,319%) as price gravitates to value
([[1965]]–2016 compounded: book **19.0%**, market **20.8%**, S&P **9.7%**).

**"What We Hope to Accomplish."** Buffett restates the mission as growing **normalized earning power per
share** every year (bumpy or not), noting Berkshire ranked **first among American companies in dollars of
earnings retained** in both 2015 and 2016. The refrain: "**Every decade or so, dark clouds will fill the
economic skies, and they will briefly rain gold. When downpours of that sort occur, it's imperative that
we rush outdoors carrying washtubs, not teaspoons.**"

**The strategic-history recap.** Buffett retells the pivot from stock-picker to business-owner through
four "key happenings": the **[[Dexter Shoe]]** error (the 25,203 shares given for it now worth **>$6
billion**); the 1996 cash purchase of all of **[[GEICO]]** (turning a stock into "the centerpiece" of the
P/C business); the 1998 stock-funded **[[General Re]]** deal — "a terrible mistake on my part to issue
272,200 shares… an act that increased our outstanding shares by a whopping **21.8%**"; and the 2000 cash
purchase of **[[MidAmerican Energy]]** that "firmly launched us on our present course." The closing line:
"**Today, I would rather prep for a colonoscopy than issue Berkshire shares.**" A table of after-tax
earnings since 1999 (operations vs. capital gains) documents the "two-pronged" capital-allocation edge.

**"America's economic dynamism."** A sustained optimism essay: from a standing start 240 years ago the
country has amassed **$90 trillion** of wealth ("**miraculous**"); the 20th-century Dow rose 66 → 11,497,
and by yearend 2016 to 19,763; "**Babies born in America today are the luckiest crop in history.**" The
investor's takeaway: in the inevitable panics, "**widespread fear is your friend… personal fear is your
enemy**," and those who avoid high costs and "simply sit for an extended period with a collection of
large, conservatively-financed American businesses will almost certainly do well."

**The share-repurchase essay.** Buffett distills [[Evolving Views — Buybacks|buyback]] logic to a
principle: repurchases help continuing shareholders **only if made below intrinsic value** ("a simple
analogy" of three partners), so it is "puzzling" that announcements almost never name a ceiling price.
"**What is smart at one price is stupid at another.**" He recaps Berkshire's own policy (authorized at
**≤120% of book**, a level he deems a meaningful discount to intrinsic value) and notes repurchasing has
"proved hard to do" precisely because his candor signals value well above 120% of book. He rebuts the
notion that buybacks are "un-American."

**Insurance.** A **14th consecutive year of underwriting profit** (pre-tax gain over the period totaling
**$28 billion**); float grew $87.7B → **$91.6 billion** (the table: $39M in 1970 → $91.6B in 2016). Buffett
notes Berkshire "recently wrote a huge policy that increased float to more than **$100 billion**" — the
early-2017 retroactive reinsurance cover (the **[[AIG retroactive reinsurance|$10.2B AIG deal]]**). The
**float-as-revolving-fund** argument is sharpened ($27B paid to 6M+ claimants in 2016, continuously
replaced), and the **$15.5 billion insurance "goodwill"** asset is shown to vastly understate the float's
true worth (nearly all of it was on the books in 2000 when float was $28B; float has since grown $64B
with "no… reflection in book value"). Underwriting by unit (2016): **[[Ajit Jain|BH Reinsurance]]** $822M
(float $45,081M), **[[General Re]]** $190M (float $17,699M) — **[[Tad Montross]] retired after 39 years**,
succeeded by **[[Kara Raiguel]]**; the **four disciplines** of sound underwriting restated — **[[GEICO]]**
$462M (share now **~12%**, up from 2.5% in 1995; **[[Tony Nicely]]** completed 55 years; employment
8,575 → 36,085; growth accelerated in H2 as rivals retrenched), and **Other Primary** $657M (incl.
**[[Berkshire Hathaway Specialty Insurance|BHSI]]**, volume +40% to $1.3B). Total UW profit **$2,131M**.

**Regulated, capital-intensive businesses.** **[[Burlington Northern Santa Fe|BNSF]]** and **90%-owned
[[MidAmerican Energy|Berkshire Hathaway Energy]]** together produced **33% of after-tax operating
earnings**. BNSF had "a disappointing year for railroads" (revenues $19,829M, net **$3,569M**, down from
$4,248M) yet interest coverage exceeded 6:1; revenue per ton-mile 3¢ vs. rivals' 4–5¢; "one of our better
purchases." BHE net **$2,542M** ($2,287M to Berkshire); its Iowa rate of **7.1¢/KWH** undercuts rival
Alliant's 9.9¢ (base rates frozen "until 2029 at the earliest"), and Iowa wind already equals **55% of
retail MWh sold**, headed to **89% by 2020** ("Iowa is the **Saudi Arabia** of America"). BHE + BNSF
invested **$8.9B** in plant and equipment. **HomeServices** (38 realty firms, 29,000 agents, 28 states)
participated in 244,000 "sides" worth $86B and bought Houlihan Lawrence.

**Manufacturing, Service & Retailing.** Revenues **$120.1 billion**; pre-tax **$8,462M**; net $5,631M; 44
businesses report to HQ (Marmon alone has 175 units; **[[Van Tuyl Automotive|Berkshire Hathaway Automotive]]** 83 dealerships in nine states). The group employed ~$24B of net tangible assets and earned
**24% after-tax** on it — though "a few" units "produce very poor returns" (capital-allocation "serious
blunders"). **[[Duracell]]** and **[[Precision Castparts]]** (both closed in 2016) will add a first full
year in 2017. Two accounting essays follow: GAAP **amortization** of intangibles is largely not a real cost
($15.4B left to amortize; $1.5B charged in 2016, only ~20% "real"), while GAAP **depreciation** *understates*
true cost in the railroad ($2.1B charged at BNSF, far below what is needed "simply to hold our own"). A
third skewers "**adjusted earnings**" — managements that wave away "restructuring" and "stock-based
compensation" ("To say 'stock-based compensation' is not an expense is even more cavalier"); the closing
1960s parable: "What is two plus two?" — "**What number do you have in mind?**"

**Finance & Financial Products.** **[[Clayton Homes]]** became **America's largest home builder** (42,075
units, ~5% of all new U.S. homes) and pushed into site-built housing; pre-tax $744M (foreclosed on 8,304
mortgages, 2.5%; lost $150M on foreclosures while extending loss-mitigation aid). **[[Marmon Group]]**'s
railcar business slowed (fleet utilization 97% → 91%).

**Investments.** The marketable portfolio (cost $65.8B / market **$122.0B**) shows two landmark new
positions: **[[Apple]]** (61.2M shares, 1.1%, cost $6,747M) — Berkshire's first major investment in a
technology hardware company — and a basket of **airlines** (**[[Delta Air Lines]]** 7.5%, **[[Southwest Airlines]]** 7.0%, **[[United Continental Holdings|United Continental]]** 8.4%, and **[[American Airlines]]**
in "others"), a striking reversal of Buffett's career-long airline aversion ([[USAir]]). Also new:
**[[Charter Communications]]** (2.5%). The Big Four marketable holdings: **[[American Express]]** 16.8%
($11.2B), **[[Coca-Cola]]** 9.3% ($16.6B), **[[IBM]]** 8.5% ($13.5B), **[[Wells Fargo]]** 10.0% ($27.6B).
**[[Kraft Heinz]]** (325.4M shares, equity-method, $9.8B cost / $28.4B market) is excluded from the table.
**[[Bank of America]]**'s $5B preferred carries a warrant for 700M shares (then $10.5B in the money;
exercisable before Sept 2, 2021). **[[Todd Combs]] and [[Ted Weschler]]** each now run **>$10 billion**.
Buffett also clarifies that Economic Principle 11 ("forever") covers *controlled businesses*, not
marketable securities — adding a sentence affirming any stock is available for sale.

**"The Bet."** The famous decade-long wager (begun 2008) against **[[Ted Seides]]**'s Protégé Partners:
through nine years a low-cost **[[Index Funds and Fees|Vanguard S&P index fund]]** gained **85.4%** (7.1%
compounded) vs. the five funds-of-funds' average of just **2.2%** compounded — with an estimated **~60% of
all gains diverted to two layers of fees** ("**Fees never sleep**"). Buffett lays out the arithmetic of
active vs. passive ("Whichever group has the lower costs will win"), estimates the "financial elites"
wasted **>$100 billion** over the decade chasing hyper-helpers, and crowns **[[Jack Bogle]]** the
"hands-down choice" for a statue honoring American investors. Bill Ruane's line: "In investment
management, the progression is from the **innovators to the imitators to the swarming incompetents**."

## Key figures

- **Book value/share:** **$172,108, +10.7%** (net worth **+$27.5B**); 52 years, **19% compounded**.
  Overall gain 1964–2016: book **884,319%**, market **1,972,595%**, S&P **12,717%**. 2016: BV +10.7%,
  market +23.4%, S&P +12.0%. After-tax earnings: **operations $17.57B**, capital gains $6.50B.
- **Insurance:** **14th straight underwriting profit** (**$2,131M**; 14-yr pre-tax total **$28B**); float
  **$91,577M** (+$3.9B; soon >$100B via the AIG deal). UW: BH Reins $822M (float $45.1B), Gen Re $190M
  (float $17.7B; **Montross→Raiguel**), GEICO $462M (share **~12%**), Other Primary $657M (BHSI $1.3B).
  Insurance goodwill carried **$15.5B**.
- **Regulated/capital-intensive (33% of after-tax operating earnings):** **BNSF** revenues $19,829M, net
  **$3,569M** (down year); **BHE (90%)** net **$2,542M** ($2,287M to Berkshire); BHE+BNSF capex **$8.9B**.
- **Mfg/Svc/Retail:** revenues **$120.1B**, pre-tax **$8,462M**, net $5,631M; **24% after-tax** on ~$24B
  net tangible assets. **Clayton** America's largest home builder (42,075 units); finance group $2,130M.
- **Equities** (cost $65.8B / mkt **$122.0B**): new **Apple** (1.1%, cost $6.7B), **airlines** (Delta,
  Southwest, United Continental, American), **Charter** (2.5%). Big Four: Amex 16.8%, Coke 9.3%, IBM 8.5%,
  WFC 10.0%. **Kraft Heinz** equity-method ($9.8B cost / $28.4B mkt). **Combs & Weschler** each **>$10B**.
- **The Bet (9 yrs):** index +85.4% (7.1% CAGR) vs. funds-of-funds avg **2.2%** CAGR; ~60% of gains to fees.
