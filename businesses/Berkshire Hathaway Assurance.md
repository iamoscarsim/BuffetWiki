# Berkshire Hathaway Assurance

Berkshire's **municipal (tax-exempt) bond insurer**, abbreviated **BHAC**, "activated" early in 2008
to guarantee bonds issued by states, cities and local entities — both at issuance (primary) and for
bonds already held by investors (secondary) ([[2008]]). Though Buffett "helps set policy," the
operation is run by **[[Ajit Jain]]** and his small Stamford crew ("Charlie and I decided it was
high time for them to start doing a full day's work").

BHAC stepped into a vacuum: by year-end 2007 the established **monoline** insurers had "all fallen
into big trouble" after drifting from low-risk munis into residential-mortgage guarantees ("I was
Snow White, but I drifted" — Mae West). Berkshire offered to reinsure $822 billion of three
monolines' tax-exempt books at a 1.5% rate; the monolines "summarily rejected" it (which proved "very
good news," as Buffett had "severely underpriced our offer"). BHAC then wrote **$15.6B of secondary
insurance at a 3.3% average rate** — 77% of it on bonds *already* insured by the monolines, so
Berkshire is "second-to-pay" (in one case "fourth to pay," collecting triple the original premium to
sit behind three other insurers) — plus **$3.7B of primary** business for $96M.

Crucially, Buffett is **skeptical the line will prove profitable**: the historically tiny default
record reflects a world of *uninsured* munis. A universe fully covered by a deep-pocketed insurer
would behave differently — when revenue shortfalls bite, a city council will choose "pain to a
far-away bond insurer" over tax hikes on local citizens (the 1975 New York City case), and losses
would be "highly correlated among issuers." Insuring tax-exempts thus "has the look today of a
dangerous business — one with similarities… to the insuring of natural catastrophes," where loss-free
years precede a wipe-out. A few tax-exempt guarantees were also written as **[[Derivatives|derivatives]]**
(forcing mark-to-market accounting; a −$631M mark in 2008) rather than as BHAC accrual insurance.

## Evolution
- [2008] Activated as Berkshire's tax-exempt bond insurer (run by [[Ajit Jain]]). Wrote $15.6B
  secondary (3.3%; mostly "second-to-pay") + $3.7B primary, after the monolines rejected a $822B
  reinsurance offer. Buffett flags it as possibly unprofitable — a correlated, cat-like risk.
