# McDonald's

McDonald's is the global fast-food chain that briefly became one of Berkshire's larger
common-stock positions, first disclosed in the **[[1996]]** letter: **30,156,600 shares,
cost $1,265.3M, market $1,368.4M** (~4.3% of the company). Berkshire's look-through
share of McDonald's retained earnings that year was **$38M**.

The position sits a little uneasily beside Buffett's "**[[The Inevitables]]**" framing of
[[Coca-Cola]] and [[Gillette]]: a strong consumer brand with worldwide reach, but in a
business with more competition and change than the soft-drink or razor franchises. It was
a relatively short-lived holding by Berkshire standards — Buffett later (in the 1998
letter) judged the failure to hold it among his mistakes — but in 1996 it ranked among
the eight itemized over-$500M positions.

## Evolution
- [1996] First disclosed: 30,156,600 shares (cost $1,265.3M / market $1,368.4M, ~4.3%);
  look-through share of earnings $38M.
- [1997] Look-through table dropped McDonald's (the position was being reduced); not separately
  itemized.
- [1998] **Sold — "a very big mistake."** In the "confession (ugh)" portion of the Investments
  section, Buffett admits his 1998 portfolio actions actually *decreased* the year's gain, "in
  particular, my decision to sell McDonald's." It is one of the rare instances of his selling a
  major consumer-brand holding, and one he promptly regretted — "you would have been better off …
  if I had regularly snuck off to the movies during market hours" (see [[Mistakes Ledger]]).
