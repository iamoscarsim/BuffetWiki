# MidAmerican Energy

MidAmerican Energy is the Iowa-based electric utility in which Berkshire agreed to invest
~**$2 billion** in 1999 — its **first move into the regulated electric-utility industry**,
and the seed of what became Berkshire Hathaway Energy, one of the company's largest
operating divisions. The deal came through director **[[Walter Scott Jr]]** (chairman of
Level 3 Communications and a substantial MidAmerican holder), who asked Buffett at a
September conference whether Berkshire might invest; Buffett liked CEO **[[David Sokol]]**
("talented and entrepreneurial") and "the idea of being in partnership with Walter."

Because utility acquisitions are tightly constrained — notably by the **Public Utility
Holding Company Act of 1935** — the transaction was deliberately structured to **avoid
Berkshire gaining voting control**: an **11% fixed-income security** plus common and
exchangeable preferred, giving Berkshire "just under **10% of the voting power** … but
about **76% of the equity interest**." Walter Scott and family added cash to ~$280M and
remained the **controlling shareholder** ("I can't think of a better person to hold that
post"). Buffett flagged the appetite for more: "it's possible that we will make additional
commitments in the field … the amounts involved could be large." *(They were — Berkshire
later bought control outright and rolled up multiple utilities and pipelines under
Sokol's, and then Greg Abel's, leadership.)*

## Evolution
- [1999] ~$2B agreed (Berkshire's first electric utility); structured around PUHCA to take
  ~76% of equity but <10% of the vote; CEO [[David Sokol]]; sourced and controlled by
  director [[Walter Scott Jr]]. Buffett signals large future utility commitments.
- [2000] **Closed** (the 76% stake). Because of the regulatory limits on Berkshire's voting
  privileges, MidAmerican is carried via a **"one-line" consolidation** — its earnings and equity
  appear on one line; "if we instead fully consolidated … our revenues in 2000 would have been **$5
  billion greater** than we reported, though net income would remain the same." First-year
  contribution: **$197M pre-tax / $109M** to Berkshire. The toehold that would grow, under [[David Sokol]], into Berkshire's sprawling regulated-utility and pipeline business.
- [2001] A good year, with reported earnings set to rise in 2002 as goodwill amortization ends
  under new GAAP. MidAmerican **swapped some U.K. properties to add Yorkshire Electric** (2.1M
  customers), bringing it to **3.6M U.K. customers** as the **#2 U.K. electric utility**, alongside
  its Iowa base and generation in California and the Philippines. The letter also reveals a
  surprise housed inside the utility: **[[HomeServices of America]]**, the **#2 U.S. residential
  real-estate brokerage** (Ron Peltier; ~106,000 transactions, ~$20B of property). 76%-owned.
- [2002] **Into pipelines; Berkshire's equity to 80.2%.** MEHC now has **$18 billion of assets** and
  "delivers our largest stream of non-insurance earnings… could well grow to be huge." It acquired
  two gas pipelines — **Kern River** (Wyoming → S. California; a $1.2B doubling underway) and
  **[[Northern Natural Gas]]** (16,600 miles, from a failing Dynegy — the ex-Enron/InterNorth Omaha
  "homecoming") — taking MEHC to ~**8% of all U.S. gas transport**. Berkshire funded it by buying
  $1,273M more of MEHC's 11% junior debt (total $1,728M) and $402M of "common-equivalent" stock →
  **80.2% of the equity** (still <10% voting under PUHCA, so consolidated on "one line"). **[[David Sokol]]** (CEO) and **[[Greg Abel]]** ("his key associate") are "**huge assets… dealmakers, and…
  managers**"; "Berkshire stands ready to inject massive amounts of money into MEHC."
- [2003] Equity **80.5%** (fully diluted). The portfolio: U.K. distribution (**Yorkshire + Northern
  Electric**, 3.7M customers — the **3rd-largest U.K. distributor**), Iowa (689,000 electric
  customers), and the Kern River + **[[Northern Natural Gas]]** pipelines (**7.8% of U.S. gas**).
  Partners **[[David Sokol]]**, **[[Greg Abel]]**, and **[[Walter Scott Jr]]** (the controlling vote
  under PUHCA); $1.578B of junior debt owed to Berkshire at 11%. A free-enterprise "tidbit": since the
  1990 U.K. denationalization, Northern/Yorkshire cut headcount from **6,800 to 2,539** while serving
  the same customers and distributing more power — "profit-motivated managers… find efficiencies that
  government never will." EBIT $1,076M.
- [2004] A **$579M write-off of a California zinc-recovery project** (extracting zinc from
  geothermal brine) dominated results — the source of Buffett's "**stay with simple propositions /
  mono-linked chains**" lesson (see [[Circle of Competence]]). Offsetting it, a wry **$72.2M gain**
  on an Enron receivable bundled with the Northern Natural purchase — Omaha's "**war reparations**"
  for Ken Lay's 1986 move of the company to Houston. **[[HomeServices of America]]** did **$59.8B**
  of transactions (18,000+ brokers, 18 states). No big acquisitions, so MidAmerican paid down $100M
  of its junior debt to Berkshire (to $1.478B).
- [2005] **PUHCA repealed (Aug 8, 2005)** — Berkshire converted its MidAmerican preferred to common,
  taking **83.4% of the votes** (economic interest 80.5%) and **consolidating** the company at last.
  The four-party "partnership" (Berkshire, **[[Walter Scott Jr]]**, **[[David Sokol]]**, **[[Greg Abel]]**) is unchanged: "we will make major moves only when we are unanimous." The marquee event:
  agreeing to buy **[[PacifiCorp]]** (~1.6M customers, six Western states; ~$5.1B), with Buffett
  signalling more utility deals to come. HomeServices did **$64B** of transactions (19,200 agents).
- [2006] **PacifiCorp closed (March 21)** — Pacific Power + Rocky Mountain Power, ~1.7M customers in
  six Western states — taking MidAmerican to **$1,718M** EBIT and Berkshire's fully-diluted interest
  to **86.6%**. With the U.K., Iowa, Western utilities and the Kern River + Northern Natural
  pipelines (~8% of U.S. gas), MidAmerican is now a sprawling regulated-utility group. **[[HomeServices of America]]** volume *fell* 9% to $58B (profits −50%) as the housing slowdown and "teaser-rate"
  lending reckoning began.
- [2007] **87.4%** owned; a full year of the Western utilities (PacifiCorp earned $692M) lifted
  earnings to Berkshire to **$1,114M**. Buffett tells the **$35.05/share** story (the 1999 purchase
  where he "caved" for an extra nickel — "I'm glad I wilted"; MidAmerican earned ~$15/share in 2007).
  HomeServices stayed slow (18,800 agents). The regulated-utility model is held up as Berkshire's
  prime "**good**" (put-up-more-to-earn-more) business (see [[The Great the Good and the Gruesome]]).
- [2008] **Record earnings ($1,704M to Berkshire) and a wind-powered crisis windfall.** Sokol and
  Abel's group "achieved results unmatched elsewhere in the utility industry." MidAmerican is now
  **#1 among regulated utilities in wind capacity** (Iowa #1 of all states; electric prices held flat
  since 1999, promised flat through 2013; $1.8B spent on wind in 2008 alone — more than the $1.1B
  Iowa + Western pre-tax earnings, since "in our utility business, we spend all we earn, and then
  some"). The Kern River and Northern Natural pipelines rose to **#1 and #3** in customer
  satisfaction (from 9th and 39th at purchase). The standout: MidAmerican's attempted rescue of
  **[[Constellation Energy]]** (hours from bankruptcy) broke up at a profit, adding **$1,092M** (a
  $175M breakup fee + a $917M investment gain). Berkshire aims to be the regulators' **"buyer of
  choice."** [[HomeServices of America]] lost $45M in "a terrible year for home sales." No dividend
  paid to Berkshire since 2000 — all earnings reinvested.
- [2009] **89.5% owned**; earnings to Berkshire **$1,071M** (recession-pressured: U.K. and Iowa
  utilities down, Western up). Over three years the Iowa + Western utilities earned $2.5B while
  spending **$3B on wind**. [[HomeServices of America]] returned to a modest profit ($43M) and bought
  a Chicago firm. The conceptual milestone: with **[[Burlington Northern Santa Fe|BNSF]]** joining
  Berkshire, MidAmerican's regulated-utility logic is generalized into a **"social compact"**
  framework (essential service + far-sighted heavy investment in exchange for fair regulated returns),
  and BNSF will be reported alongside MidAmerican in the new capital-intensive sector — Buffett's
  formal embrace of businesses that "regularly require large capital expenditures."
- [2010] **Paired with [[Burlington Northern Santa Fe|BNSF]] in the new "regulated, capital-intensive"
  sector.** Berkshire's interest **89.8%**; earnings to Berkshire **$1,131M**. MidAmerican serves 2.4M
  electric customers (largest in Iowa, Wyoming and Utah) and moves 8% of U.S. gas; both pipelines now
  rank at the top (Kern River #1, Northern Natural #2, from 43/43 at purchase). Iowa rates are flat
  since 1999 while the state's other major utility raised them >70%. The wind program reaches **2,909
  MW by end-2011** ($5.4B invested/committed — "more than any other regulated electric utility"),
  possible only because MidAmerican "retains all of its earnings." The "social compact" with regulators
  is the organizing idea (Sokol and Abel run it; [[Matt Rose]] runs BNSF).
- [2011] **Now run by [[Greg Abel]] alone** after [[David Sokol]]'s 2011 departure; one of the
  "fabulous five" record earners (earnings to Berkshire **$1,204M**). Serves 2.5M electric customers
  (largest in Iowa, Utah, Wyoming) and 8% of U.S. gas; both pipelines #1/#2; U.S. utilities ranked #2
  of 60 for customer satisfaction. Wind reaches **3,316 MW by end-2012** ($6B), "far more than any
  other regulated electric utility," plus **two new solar projects** (~$3B; California 100% / Arizona
  49%) — "many more wind and solar projects will almost certainly follow." Ben Franklin, updated:
  "**Take care of your customer, and the regulator… will take care of you.**" (BNSF + MidAmerican
  carry $20B of goodwill, far below intrinsic value.)
- [2012] One of the "powerhouse five"; earnings to Berkshire **$1,323M**. Has **6% of U.S. wind** and,
  with three projects building, will own ~**14% of U.S. solar**; the renewables portfolio will cost
  **$13B**. **[[HomeServices of America]]** (incongruously housed here since 2000) bought the
  Prudential/Real Living franchises and began the **Berkshire Hathaway HomeServices** rebrand.
- [2013] **NV Energy acquired ($5.6B) — utilities now span 11 states.** [[NV Energy]] (88% of Nevada)
  broadened MidAmerican's earnings base and cut its cost of debt; "**NV Energy will not be
  MidAmerican's last major acquisition.**" MidAmerican **retained more earnings than any other U.S.
  electric utility**, ranked **#1 in customer satisfaction** (95.3% "very satisfied"), holds **7% of
  U.S. wind**, and will spend up to **$15B** on renewables. Earnings to Berkshire **$1,470M**; led by
  **[[Greg Abel]]**. (Soon renamed **Berkshire Hathaway Energy**.)
- [[2016]] **Berkshire Hathaway Energy (90% owned)** — net earnings **$2,542M** ($2,287M to Berkshire),
  led by **[[Greg Abel]]**. Now reported as a distinct **"regulated, capital-intensive"** sector with
  **[[Burlington Northern Santa Fe|BNSF]]**; together they produced **33% of Berkshire's after-tax
  operating earnings** and invested **$8.9B** in plant and equipment. The "**social compact**" made
  concrete by low prices: BHE's Iowa retail rate is **7.1¢/KWH** vs. rival Alliant's 9.9¢ (and a national
  average of 10.3¢), with **base rates promised flat until 2029 at the earliest**. Wind leadership: in
  Iowa, MWh generated from wind already equal **55% of retail MWh sold**, headed to **89% by 2020** —
  "**Iowa is the Saudi Arabia of America.**" Segment detail: U.K. utilities $367M, Iowa $392M, Nevada
  $559M, PacifiCorp $1,105M, gas pipelines $413M, Canadian transmission $147M, renewables $157M,
  **[[HomeServices of America|HomeServices]]** $225M. HomeServices grew to **38 realty firms, 29,000+
  agents in 28 states**, participated in **244,000 "sides" ($86B volume)**, and bought four realtors
  including Houlihan Lawrence (Westchester) — "we expect to acquire many realtors and franchisees during
  the next decade."
- [[2018]] **A "towering redwood" (90.9% owned).** With **[[Burlington Northern Santa Fe|BNSF]]**, BHE is
  one of Berkshire's **"two towering redwoods"** in the [[The Five Groves|five-groves]] letter — together
  **$9.3B pre-tax (+6%)**. Its long-lived regulated assets carry large amounts of **non-guaranteed**
  long-term debt, the model Buffett endorses for capital-intensive, recession-resistant businesses
  (recall the "social compact"). HomeServices ([[HomeServices of America]]) continued its brokerage
  roll-up within BHE.
- [[2019]] **20 years under Berkshire (91% owned).** A milestone tour: Iowa residential rates (**8.8¢/kWh
  in 2000**) have risen **<1% a year**, with **no base-rate increases promised through 2028** — while
  another large Iowa utility now charges **61% more** (a gap widening to 70%). The engine is wind: BHE
  expected to reach **wind self-sufficiency in Iowa by 2021** (~25.2M MWh of owned/operated wind vs. ~24.6M
  MWh of customer need) — "no other investor-owned utility… will have achieved" it — drawing high-tech
  plants to the state. BHE (with **[[Walter Scott Jr]]** and **[[Greg Abel]]**) has **never paid Berkshire
  a dividend, retaining $28 billion** ("the more we can invest, the more we like it"), and stands "ready,
  willing and able" for utility projects "requiring investments of **$100 billion or more**."
- [[2020]] **A "Big Four" giant; the $18B Western grid.** BHE (91% owned) is the fourth of Berkshire's
  **"Big Four,"** earning $3.4B (from $122M at the 1999/2000 purchase — 21 years). With **[[Burlington Northern Santa Fe|BNSF]]**, the two "giants" earned **$8.3B** (vs. $4.2B in 2011) and anchor Berkshire's
  **$154B of U.S. property, plant and equipment**. BHE **pays no common dividend** ("a highly-unusual
  practice in the electric-utility industry"), reinvesting everything: the marquee project is an **$18
  billion rebuild and expansion of the Western transmission grid** (begun 2006, to finish by **2030**) to
  carry remote wind/solar power to population centers — a bet "based upon its trust in America's political,
  economic and judicial systems." "**BHE will be a leader in delivering ever-cleaner energy**," and is
  "searching for other projects of similar size."
- [[2021]] **The fourth "Giant" — record $4B.** BHE (91.1% owned) earned a **record $4 billion**, up
  **30-fold** from the $122M earned in 2000, the year Berkshire first invested — built by David Sokol and
  **[[Greg Abel]]** from "no wind or solar generation in 2000" into "a utility powerhouse… a leading force
  in wind, solar and transmission." Greg's signed report on renewables/transmission (in the report's
  appendix since 2007, "not… green-washing") accompanies the letter. Climate-conscious projects "soak up
  all of its earnings," and "more opportunities lie ahead."
- [[2023]] **"A costly mistake" — the regulatory/wildfire reckoning.** BHE was Berkshire's "even more
  severe earnings disappointment" (utilities/energy operating earnings fell to **$2,331M** from $3,904M).
  The century-old "social compact" — a fixed-but-satisfactory regulated return — "**has been broken in a
  few states**," raising "the specter of zero profitability or even bankruptcy" (the fate of California's
  **PG&E** and a threat at **Hawaiian Electric**). BHE booked a best-estimate for **forest-fire losses**
  (chiefly **PacifiCorp** in the West). Buffett's candid confession: "**I did not anticipate or even
  consider the adverse developments in regulatory returns and… I made a costly mistake in not doing so**"
  (see [[Mistakes Ledger]]). Some utilities may be forced toward the **public-power model** (as Nebraska
  chose in the 1930s). The Western transmission project (begun 2006) will eventually serve **10 states /
  30% of the continental U.S. acreage**.
- [[2024]] **Taken to 100%.** Late in 2024 Berkshire bought the remaining minority, raising its ownership
  of **[[MidAmerican Energy|BHE]]** from ~92% to **100%** for roughly **$3.9 billion** ($2.9B cash + the
  balance in Berkshire "B" shares) — buying out the Scott family and Greg Abel's residual stake. BHE
  earnings recovered to **$3,730M** (from $2,331M), though Buffett notes the railroad and utility "both…
  have much left to accomplish" after the 2023 regulatory/wildfire shock.
