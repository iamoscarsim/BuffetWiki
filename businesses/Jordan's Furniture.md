# Jordan's Furniture

Jordan's Furniture is the New England home-furnishings retailer Berkshire acquired in 1999
(closed **November 13**) — the **fourth** of Buffett's furniture-chain purchases sourced
through the [[Nebraska Furniture Mart|Blumkin]] referral chain (Blumkins → [[R.C. Willey Home Furnishings|R.C. Willey]] → [[Star Furniture]] → Jordan's). Run by brothers **Barry
and Eliot [[Tatelman Brothers|Tatelman]]**, the business had been in the family since
1927; under the brothers it became the **largest furniture retailer in both New Hampshire
and Massachusetts**.

Its distinction is **"shoppertainment"** — "they also present customers with a dazzling
entertainment experience," so "a family visiting a store can have a terrific time, while
concurrently viewing an extraordinary selection of merchandise." The results match the
showmanship: Jordan's has "**the highest sales per square foot of any major furniture
operation in the country**" (Buffett singles out the Natick store — "Bring money"). True
to the pattern of Berkshire's furniture sellers, when the Tatelmans sold they gave **every
employee at least 50¢ for every hour ever worked** — **$9 million from their own pockets**,
and "thrilled to write the checks." With Jordan's, Berkshire became the #1 furniture
seller in Massachusetts, New Hampshire, Texas, Nebraska, Utah and Idaho.

## Evolution
- [1999] Acquired (closed 11/13/99); New England's leading furniture retailer (largest in
  MA and NH); the "shoppertainment" model; highest sales per square foot in the country;
  Barry and Eliot Tatelman (family-run since 1927). Sourced via the Blumkin/Child/Wolff
  referral chain; folded into the Home Furnishings segment ($79M pre-tax with Jordan's).
