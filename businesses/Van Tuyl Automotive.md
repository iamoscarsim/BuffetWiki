# Van Tuyl Automotive

A group of **78 automobile dealerships** — the **fifth-largest auto group in the country** ($9 billion
of sales) — contracted for in October **2014** and Berkshire's entry into car retailing ("we are now
**car guys**") ([[2014]]). Renamed **Berkshire Hathaway Automotive**, it was built over 62 years by
**Larry Van Tuyl** and his father **Cecil** on a strategy of making "**owner-partners of all local
managers**" — a mutuality-of-interests culture that fit Berkshire's own. Larry had long decided that
"if he were ever to sell his company, its home should be Berkshire"; **Jeff Rachor** continues to run
operations.

Buffett saw a long runway: with ~17,000 U.S. dealerships and every transfer requiring manufacturer
approval, Berkshire aims "to perform in a manner that will cause manufacturers to welcome further
purchases" — building "a business that before long will be multiples the size of Van Tuyl's $9
billion of sales." With this deal, Berkshire owned **9½** would-be Fortune 500 companies (Heinz the ½).

## Evolution
- [2014] Acquired (78 dealerships, $9B sales, #5 U.S. auto group; Larry Van Tuyl / Jeff Rachor) →
  renamed Berkshire Hathaway Automotive; a platform for further dealership purchases.
