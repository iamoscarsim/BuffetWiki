# Shaw Industries

Shaw Industries is the **world's largest carpet manufacturer**, acquired by Berkshire in
2000 — and, "leaving aside our insurance operation, **by far our largest business**" (annual
sales ~**$4 billion**; Berkshire owns **87.3%**). It is run by CEO **Bob Shaw** and his
partner **Julian Saul**, each of whom "built a large, successful carpet business before
joining forces in 1998," and a key deal term was that both **continue owning at least 5%** —
"This leaves us associated with the best in the business."

The acquisition grew out of a *declined* deal. Shaw first came to Buffett mulling a merger
with a company that carried "huge **asbestos liabilities**," asking Berkshire to write an
**uncapped** policy covering all future asbestos costs. Buffett refused — "we would never
issue a policy that lacked a cap" — so that merger died, but his interest in Shaw was
sparked, and he and Munger bought the carpet business directly a few months later. The
episode foreshadows the asbestos theme that also runs through the year's [[Johns Manville]]
purchase and [[Ajit Jain]]'s retroactive-reinsurance deals. Buffett's quip: "Now, if people
walk all over us, we won't mind."

## Evolution
- [2000] Acquired 87.3% (world's largest carpet maker, ~$4B sales) — Berkshire's largest
  non-insurance business. CEOs Bob Shaw and Julian Saul each retained ≥5%. The deal arose
  after Berkshire declined to write an uncapped asbestos policy for a Shaw merger partner.
  The "carpet" leg of the 2000 brick/carpet/insulation/paint expansion.
- [2001] **Closed and taken to 100%.** Shaw "came through [its] first year with us in great
  fashion" (with [[Johns Manville]] and Benjamin Moore, the three earned ~$659M pre-tax;
  Shaw alone $292M pre-tax / $156M to Berkshire from its Jan 8 closing). Shortly after year-end
  Berkshire exchanged **4,740 A-share equivalents for the 12.7% minority**, taking Shaw to **100%**.
  "Shaw is our largest non-insurance operation and will play a big part in Berkshire's future."
- [2003] **Record year** ($436M pre-tax). In November Shaw acquired the carpet operations of **Dixie
  Group** (~$240M of added sales), pushing Shaw's volume toward **~$5 billion**. Bob Shaw "built this
  huge enterprise from a standing start"; another earnings record was expected in 2004.
- [2004] Absorbed a **>$300M fiber-cost hit** ("when you walk on carpet you are… stepping on
  processed oil") as oil prices spiked, narrowing margins with a price-increase lag — yet still
  earned **25.6% on tangible equity**. "A powerhouse… bright future" under Bob Shaw and Julian Saul.
- [2005] Continued raw-material/energy cost pressure (petrochemicals, natural gas) but "respectable
  results" on its franchise strength. Spent ~**$550M** on two **vertical-integration** bolt-ons to
  improve future margins. $485M pre-tax.
- [2006] **Founder Bob Shaw retires at 75**, recommending 31-year veteran **[[Vance Bell]]** as
  successor ("Bob, as usual, made the right call"). Housing weakness began to slow the carpet
  business, but Shaw "remains a powerhouse and a major contributor to Berkshire's earnings."
- [[2017]] **The U.S. Floors bolt-on.** Late in 2016 Shaw acquired **U.S. Floors (USF)**, "a rapidly
  growing distributor of **luxury vinyl tile**" (managers Piet Dossche and Philippe Erramuzpe), whose
  sales jumped **40%** in 2017 as it integrated with Shaw. CEO **[[Vance Bell]]** "originated, negotiated
  and completed" the deal, lifting Shaw's sales to **$5.7 billion** and employment to **22,000** — and
  pushing into the fast-growing LVT category beyond traditional carpet. "An important and durable source
  of earnings for Berkshire."
