# Fireman's Fund

Fireman's Fund Insurance Company (FFIC) entered the Berkshire story in [[1985]] via a
four-year **7% quota-share reinsurance contract** — Berkshire took 7% of all of
FFIC's in-force business (premiums, losses, and costs), generating float for
investment. The arrangement followed [[Jack Byrne]], who had left [[GEICO]] to become
FFIC's chairman and CEO ("Never one to let go of a meal ticket, we have followed Jack
Byrne to Fireman's Fund"). Buffett advised on general investment strategy but not
specific decisions or underwriting. The business was written through
[[National Indemnity Company]], with two-sevenths passed to **Wes-FIC** (Wesco-
Financial Insurance Company), a new [[Wesco Financial]] subsidiary. The contract was
a vehicle for deploying Berkshire's [[Financial Strength]] during the mid-1980s hard
market; it ran its term and was not extended.

## Evolution
- [1985] 7% quota-share of FFIC begun Sept 1, 1985 (4-year term); $92.7M of unearned
  premium transferred in. Followed Jack Byrne to FFIC; written via National Indemnity
  and Wes-FIC.
