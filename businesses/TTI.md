# TTI

A Fort Worth **distributor of electronic components** (passive components, connectors, etc.),
acquired by Berkshire in 2006 ([[2006]]). Owner **[[Paul Andrews Jr]]** had built TTI from
**$112,000 of sales to $1.3 billion** over 35 years. The deal was brought to Buffett by **John
Roach** (the ex-chairman of [[Justin Industries]], which Berkshire bought in 2000), and reflects
Buffett's appeal to founders worried about succession: Andrews, having seen "how disruptive the
death of a founder can be," rejected a "strategic" buyer (who would chase synergies and dismantle
the firm) and a private-equity firm (which "would very likely load the company with debt and then
flip it"). "That left Berkshire." Buffett and Andrews met the morning of November 15 "and made a
deal before lunch."

## Evolution
- [2006] Acquired (Paul Andrews Jr.; sourced by John Roach); a $1.3B electronic-components
  distributor; the founder-succession logic that makes Berkshire the buyer of choice.
- [[2021]] **The growth tallied, and its pivotal legacy.** On **[[Paul Andrews Jr|Paul Andrews]]'** death,
  Buffett records TTI's arc under Berkshire: employees **2,387 → 8,043**, earnings **+673%**, annual volume
  now **$7.7 billion** across **>1 million** items. TTI's outsized importance: hosting a 2009 Berkshire
  board visit in Fort Worth put Buffett in front of BNSF's [[Matt Rose]] — "**TTI was soon to lead
  Berkshire to its most important acquisition**" (the **[[Burlington Northern Santa Fe|BNSF]]** deal).
