# Guinness

Guinness PLC, the British drinks company, was a new position in 1991 and marks
**Berkshire's first significant investment in a company domiciled outside the United
States** ([[1991]]). At year-end Berkshire held 31,247,000 shares (cost $264.8M /
market $296.8M).

Buffett's rationale folds Guinness into his franchise thesis rather than treating it
as a foreign-currency bet: "Guinness… earns its money in much the same fashion as
[[Coca-Cola]] and [[Gillette]], U.S.-based companies that garner most of their
profits from international operations." In terms of *where* profits are earned —
continent by continent — "Coca-Cola and Guinness display strong similarities."
(Buffett could not resist adding that "you'll never get their drinks confused," and
that he "remains unmovably in the Cherry Coke camp.") The holding is an early sign of
Buffett's willingness to extend the [[Circle of Competence]] internationally when the
underlying economics resemble franchises he already understands.

## Evolution
- [1991] New position; Berkshire's first significant non-U.S. investment; framed as a
  global-franchise analogue to Coca-Cola.
- [1992] Added moderately, to **38,335,000 shares** (~2.0%; cost $333.0M / market $299.6M) —
  one of only four holdings changed during the year. First look-through contribution: $7M.
- [1993] 38,335,000 shares held; market slipped to **$270.8M** (below the $333.0M cost) —
  the lone underwater holding in the table; look-through share $8M. (Guinness quietly fades
  from later holdings tables.)
