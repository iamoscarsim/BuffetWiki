# Burlington Northern Santa Fe (BNSF)

One of North America's largest **railroads**, and the subject of Berkshire's largest-ever
acquisition. It first appears as a **new equity position** in [[2007]] (60.8M shares, **17.5%**,
cost $4,731M / market $5,063M) — Buffett accumulating a major stake in a capital-intensive but
newly-attractive business (rail's fuel-efficiency and rebuilt pricing power had restored its
economics). Berkshire kept buying through 2008–09.

In **2009** Berkshire agreed to buy the **~77.5% of BNSF it didn't already own for ~$26 billion**
(cash and stock; ~$34B total enterprise value including debt) — "an **all-in wager on the economic
future of the United States**," closed in **February 2010**. To partly fund it Berkshire issued
stock and **split its Class B shares 50-for-1** (so small BNSF holders could take stock tax-free).
BNSF became a wholly-owned pillar alongside MidAmerican in Berkshire's capital-intensive
"powerhouse," reliably reinvesting billions at regulated-but-fair returns ([[The Great the Good and the Gruesome|"good" business economics]]).

## Evolution
- [2007] New equity position (**17.5%**, ~$5B); a bet on rail's restored economics (fuel efficiency,
  pricing power).
- [2009] Berkshire agrees to buy the **rest of BNSF for ~$26B** — its **largest acquisition ever**,
  "an all-in wager on… the United States"; funded partly by stock + a **50:1 Class B split**.
- [2010] **Deal closes (February)**; BNSF becomes a wholly-owned subsidiary and a core earnings/
  capital-deployment engine.
- [2011] First full year owned; record results as freight volumes recovered — a heavy reinvestor
  ("good," capital-hungry economics) but a durable franchise.
- [2012]–[2013] A heavy reinvestor and core earner (net $3,372M in 2012, $3,793M in 2013; interest
  coverage ~9:1); ~15% of all U.S. intercity ton-miles; spent a record **$4B** in 2013 ("like Noah… we
  plan ahead"). Now co-led by **Matt Rose and Carl Ice**.
- [2014] **Service failures** — "BNSF disappointed many of its customers." Buffett's lone "bad news"
  from the Powerhouse Five; the fix is a planned **$6B** of 2015 capex ("nearly 50% more than any other
  railroad has spent in a single year").
- [2015] **The turnaround — "the most important development at Berkshire."** A record **$5.8B** capex
  (≈3× depreciation) "dramatically improved its service"; while rival railroads' volumes and earnings
  fell, BNSF held volume and lifted pre-tax income to a record **$6.8B** (+$606M). It carries ~**17%**
  of U.S. intercity freight (45% more ton-miles than its closest competitor) — "the largest of our
  Powerhouse Five."
- [[2016]] **"A disappointing year for railroads."** Industry-wide volumes fell; BNSF revenues slipped
  $21,967M → **$19,829M** and net earnings $4,248M → **$3,569M** — yet interest coverage still exceeded
  **6:1** (Buffett's definition: EBIT/interest, *not* the "seriously flawed" EBITDA/interest). Grouped
  with **[[MidAmerican Energy|BHE]]** as the **"regulated, capital-intensive"** sector that produced
  **33% of Berkshire's after-tax operating earnings**; the two invested **$8.9B** in plant and equipment.
  Efficiency points: revenue per ton-mile **3¢** (vs. rivals' 4–5¢); a single gallon of diesel moves a
  ton of freight ~500 miles — **4× more fuel-efficient than trucks**. Buffett uses BNSF to illustrate
  that GAAP **depreciation understates true cost** in railroading: the $2.1B charged "simply to hold our
  own" is far below what must actually be spent. "Charlie and I love our railroad, which was **one of our
  better purchases**."
- [[2018]] **A "towering redwood."** In the [[The Five Groves|five-groves]] letter, BNSF and **[[MidAmerican Energy|BHE]]** are the **"two towering redwoods"** of the non-insurance grove, together earning **$9.3
  billion pre-tax (+6%** over 2017) — recovering from 2016's off year. Their heavy, **non-guaranteed** debt
  is held up as the appropriate use of leverage at asset-heavy, recession-resistant businesses.
- [[2020]] **A "Big Four" jewel, and a CEO handoff.** BNSF is named one of Berkshire's **"Big Four"** (a
  "toss-up" with [[Apple]] for 2nd/3rd most valuable). Despite a **7% COVID volume decline**, **[[Carl Ice]]
  and [[Katie Farmer]]** *raised* the profit margin by **2.9 points**; Ice retired at year-end and **Farmer
  became CEO** ("your railroad is in good hands"). The scale, in numbers: ~**15% of all U.S. non-local
  ton-miles**; **$41B invested in fixed assets since 2010** ($20B above depreciation); 23,000 miles across
  28 states; **$41.8B of dividends paid to Berkshire** in total (paying out only what remains after keeping
  a ~$2B cash balance, so its debt needs no Berkshire guarantee). With **[[MidAmerican Energy|BHE]]**, the
  two "giants" earned **$8.3B** (vs. $4.2B in 2011) — proof that **asset-heavy businesses can still be
  good investments**; together they anchor Berkshire's **$154B of U.S. property, plant and equipment** (the
  most of any U.S. company).
- [[2021]] **"The number one artery of American commerce" — record $6B.** As the third of Berkshire's
  **Four Giants**, BNSF posted **record earnings of $6 billion** (an "old-fashioned" figure, after
  interest, taxes, depreciation, amortization and all compensation — Buffett's jab: "bull markets breed
  bloviated bull"). It ran **143 million train-miles** carrying **535 million tons** of cargo, "far
  exceed[ing] those of any other American carrier"; shifting that freight to trucks would make "America's
  carbon emissions soar."
- [[2023]] **An earnings disappointment — and a capital lesson.** Buffett admits he "erred… in my
  expectations": BNSF earnings fell to **$5,087M** (from $5,946M) as revenues dropped and
  **Washington-mandated wage increases** ran "far beyond the country's inflation goals," and its **profit
  margins slipped relative to all five** peer railroads since the 2010 purchase. The capital reality laid
  bare: BNSF (23,759 miles of main track, 99 tunnels, 13,495 bridges) is carried at **$70B** but "would
  cost at least **$500 billion to replicate**"; it has spent **$22B over depreciation** in 14 years, so its
  dividends to Berkshire "regularly fall considerably short of… reported earnings." Yet "**rail is
  essential to America's economic future**… A century from now, BNSF will continue to be a major asset of
  the country and of Berkshire."
