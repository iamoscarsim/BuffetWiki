# Lubrizol

A worldwide producer of **additives and specialty chemicals** (lubricant additives above all),
acquired by Berkshire on **September 16, 2011** for roughly **$9 billion** ([[2011]]). Run by CEO
**[[James Hambrick]]** since 2004, Lubrizol had compiled "an outstanding record," lifting pre-tax
profit from **$147 million to $1,085 million**. Buffett liked both the disciplined operator and the
business's runway for **bolt-on acquisitions** in specialty chemicals — three were agreed almost
immediately ($493M). Lubrizol joined Berkshire's **"fabulous five"** largest non-insurance earners
(with [[Burlington Northern Santa Fe|BNSF]], [[ISCAR]], [[Marmon Group]] and [[MidAmerican Energy]]).

The purchase had a notable backstory Buffett did *not* dwell on in the letter: it precipitated the
2011 departure of **[[David Sokol]]**, who had bought Lubrizol shares before pitching the deal to
Buffett — a corporate-governance episode that ended Sokol's Berkshire career.

## Evolution
- [2011] Acquired (~$9B, closed Sept 16); James Hambrick CEO; pre-tax profit $147M → $1,085M since
  2004; three bolt-ons ($493M) agreed; one of the "fabulous five." Its purchase helped offset the
  income lost when the Swiss Re/Goldman/GE preferreds were redeemed.
- [2012]–[2013] Continued bolt-on growth in specialty chemicals under Hambrick; a steady earner.
- [[2019]] **A French plant fire — insured by Berkshire.** On September 26, 2019 a fire that began at a
  small neighboring operation spread to a large French Lubrizol plant, causing significant property damage
  and business interruption — both "mitigated by substantial insurance recoveries." Buffett's punchline
  ("the rest of the story"): one of Lubrizol's largest insurers was itself a Berkshire company. One of the
  next-five non-insurance earners (Clayton/IMC/Lubrizol/Marmon/PCC: $4.8B combined in 2019).
