# Johns Manville

Johns Manville is the nation's **leading producer of commercial and industrial
insulation** (with major positions in roofing systems and engineered products) that
Berkshire agreed to buy in **late December 2000 for about $1.8 billion**, all cash. Its
"incredible odyssey" was shaped by a long history as an **asbestos** manufacturer: the
health problems among those exposed led JM to declare **bankruptcy in 1982**, after which
the bankruptcy court established a **trust for victims** whose major asset was a
controlling interest in JM. The trust, sensibly wanting to diversify, agreed in June 2000
to sell JM to an **LBO buyer** — but that group "was unable to obtain financing," and the
deal collapsed on Friday, December 8.

The following Monday, Buffett and Munger called trust chairman Bob Felise and made "an
**all-cash offer with no financing contingencies**"; the trustees voted tentatively to
accept the next day and a contract was signed a week later — a textbook example of
Berkshire's edge once the junk-bond market dried up and leveraged buyers couldn't fund
their bids. Sales exceed **$2 billion** with "good, if cyclical, returns." CEO **Jerry
Henry** had announced retirement, but Buffett and Munger "convinced him to stick around."
It is the "insulation" leg of the year's brick/carpet/insulation/paint expansion.
