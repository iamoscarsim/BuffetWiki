# Fechheimer Brothers

Fechheimer Bros. Company is a Cincinnati maker and distributor of uniforms — police,
fire, postal, military and other — that Berkshire bought in [[1986]], its first
acquisition since the landmark 1985 trio and a textbook case of the
[[Acquisition Criteria]] "want ad" working exactly as designed.

The deal came to Buffett unbidden. On January 15, 1986 he received a letter from
**Bob Heldman**, Fechheimer's Chairman and a Berkshire shareholder for many years,
who had "dutifully read Berkshire's annual reports" and recognized that his company
met the tests. Founded in **1842**, Fechheimer had been run by the Heldman family
since Warren Heldman joined in 1941; in 1981 it was sold to venture capitalists in a
leveraged buyout, with management keeping equity. By 1986 the LBO debt was largely
paid down, the VCs wanted out, and the Heldmans wanted a permanent home. Berkshire
quickly bought **about 84%** of the stock at a price based on a **$55 million
valuation** for the whole business (closing June 3, 1986), with the family retaining
their interest and continuing to run it — Bob and George Heldman (then mid-60s,
"spring chickens by our standards") plus three of the next generation (Gary, Roger,
Fred).

Buffett ranks Fechheimer with [[Nebraska Furniture Mart]] as the model owner-managed
acquisition: a superb economic record, talented family managers who love the work and
want to keep owning, and a seller who wants a buyer who will never re-sell. Its only
"drawback" is size — it used only ~2% of Berkshire's net worth — prompting Buffett to
raise the want-ad's minimum to $10M of after-tax earnings. Neither he nor Munger had
ever visited Cincinnati: "If our success were to depend upon insights we developed
through plant inspections, Berkshire would be in big trouble."

## Evolution
- [1986] Acquired ~84% at a $55M whole-business valuation (closed 6/3/86). Cincinnati
  uniform maker founded 1842, run by the Heldman family; came in via Bob Heldman
  answering the annual-report want ad. Pre-tax earnings $8.4M in its partial first year.
- [1987] Earnings "increased substantially" (pre-tax $13.3M) after a record 1986 — "the
  only magic is in the Heldmans" (Bob, George, Gary, Roger, Fred), who "know the business
  inside and out, and they have fun running it." One of the **[[Sainted Seven]]**.
- [1988] Pre-tax $14.2M. Made "a fairly good-sized acquisition" that Buffett and Munger
  "okayed… without even looking at it" — a measure of their confidence in the Heldmans
  ("very few managements anywhere — including… the top tier of the *Fortune 500* — in
  which we would exhibit similar confidence"). With internal growth, 1989 sales should
  rise significantly. Buffett notes uniform-making, like furniture retailing, has no
  "inherently attractive economics" — "only exceptional managements can deliver high
  returns," which the five Heldmans do.
- [1989] Profits dipped "somewhat" on the difficulty of integrating the 1988 acquisition
  ("ironed out in time"); return on invested capital "remains splendid." Buffett marvels
  at the Heldmans' command of detail ("when a prisoner enters San Quentin, Bob and George
  probably know his shirt size") and notes that four years in, he and Munger have still
  "never visited any of its plants or the home office in Cincinnati" — "much like the
  lonesome Maytag repairman: the Heldman managerial product is so good that a service call
  is never needed."
- [1990] **George Heldman (69) retires** — "I tried to talk him out of it but he had one
  irrefutable argument: with four other Heldmans — Bob, Fred, Gary and Roger — to carry on,
  he was leaving us with an abundance of managerial talent." Operating performance improved
  as the 1988-acquisition problems eased (though several unusual items kept reported
  earnings flat); the retail operation grew to **42 stores in 22 states**.
- [2000] A new CEO arrives: **[[Brad Kinstler]]**, previously head of Berkshire's Cypress
  workers'-comp insurer, was tapped to run Fechheimer — one of Berkshire's rare cross-business
  manager transfers. "Brad is hitting home runs in his new job, just as he always did at Cypress."
