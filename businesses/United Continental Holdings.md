# United Continental Holdings

Parent of United Airlines (formed in the 2010 United–Continental merger), one of the four major U.S.
carriers bought by Berkshire in [[2016]] as part of its airline re-entry ([[Evolving Views — Airlines]]).
Disclosed in the [[2016]] portfolio at **26.6M shares (8.4% of the company), cost $1,477M / market
$1,940M**. United was itself a product of the industry consolidation that underpinned Buffett's thesis
that the airlines had become a disciplined oligopoly rather than the "gruesome" commodity business of
[[USAir]] lore.

## Evolution
- [[2016]] New holding: 8.4% of United Continental, ~$1.5B cost. Part of the all-four-carriers basket bet.
- [[2020]] **Sold in full** during the COVID-19 travel collapse (announced May 2020), at a loss — gone
  from the year-end portfolio. See [[Evolving Views — Airlines]].
