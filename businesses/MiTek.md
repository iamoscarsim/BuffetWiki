# MiTek

The world's leading producer of **connector plates** — the toothed metal plates used to join
the wood members of roofing trusses — bought by Berkshire in 2001 ([[2001]]). The deal began
when CEO **Gene Toombs** mailed Buffett "an unprepossessing chunk of metal" with a letter
explaining that MiTek's U.K. parent wished to sell and that Berkshire "seemed to him the ideal
buyer." A one-minute phone call convinced Buffett that "he was our kind of manager and MiTek
our kind of business," and a cash deal followed quickly.

MiTek is the wiki's clearest example of Buffett's **owner-alignment** ideal in action.
Toombs's managerial crew wanted to invest alongside Berkshire, so **55 members of the MiTek
team bought 10% of the company**, each putting up a minimum of **$100,000 in cash** (many
borrowing to do so). Buffett contrasts this sharply with stock options: "As they would not be
if they had options, all of these managers are **true owners**. They face the downside of
decisions as well as the upside. They incur a cost of capital. And they can't 'reprice' their
stakes: What they paid is what they live with" — a jab at the option-repricing culture he
attacks elsewhere in the [[2001]] letter ([[Executive Compensation]], [[Corporate Governance]]).

## Evolution
- [2001] Acquired (cash); the "true owners" buy-in by 55 managers held up as the antithesis of
  option grants. MiTek folded into Berkshire's building-products group alongside [[Shaw Industries]], [[Johns Manville]], and Benjamin Moore.
- [2004] Despite a **100% steel-cost spike** (MiTek uses 665M lbs/yr), it "continues to be an
  outstanding performer"; CEO **Gene Toombs** had begun making "brilliant bolt-on acquisitions… on
  his way to creating a mini-Berkshire."
- [2006] **A "mini-conglomerate"** in the making (14 bolt-ons since 2001). The owner-alignment story
  paid off spectacularly: the **stock bought at $10,000/share in 2001 was valued at $71,699** five
  years later (sales tripled; debt to Berkshire repaid; $35M cash). Buffett joked it would be
  "embarrassing if MiTek's stock price soared beyond that of Berkshire 'A' shares."
