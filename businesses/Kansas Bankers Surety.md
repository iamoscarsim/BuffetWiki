# Kansas Bankers Surety

Kansas Bankers Surety (KBS) is a specialty insurer — its name describes its niche —
that Berkshire bought in **1996 for $75 million** and placed with its 80%-owned
subsidiary **[[Wesco Financial]]**, which had wanted to expand its insurance operations.
Despite the name, KBS does business in **22 states** and carries "an extraordinary
underwriting record," the work of manager **[[Don Towle]]**, who has first-hand
relationships with hundreds of bankers and "thinks of himself as running a company that
is 'his' — an attitude we treasure at Berkshire."

The acquisition is one of Buffett's favorite "**[[Acquisition Criteria|how we found it]]**" anecdotes. At a January 1996 birthday party he was seated next to Roy Dinsdale,
who mentioned coming from a KBS directors' meeting; Buffett shouted (over a loud band)
that Dinsdale should let him know if it were ever for sale. A letter arrived February 12;
on February 13 Buffett offered $75M, "and before long we had a deal." KBS joined
Berkshire's cluster of small, superbly-run primary insurers (National Indemnity's
traditional book, the homestate companies, workers' comp, Central States) whose combined
underwriting record Buffett calls "virtually unmatched in the industry."
