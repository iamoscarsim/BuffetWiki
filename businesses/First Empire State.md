# First Empire State

First Empire State Corp. is a Buffalo-based bank holding company (later M&T Bank) in
which Berkshire bought a **$40 million 9% convertible preferred** in 1991 ([[1991]]).
The issue is non-callable until 1996 and convertible at $78.91 per share.

Buffett conceded the purchase was, by Berkshire's standards, unusually small —
"normally I would think a purchase of this size too small for Berkshire" — but made
it because of his "enormous respect for **[[Bob Wilmers]]**, CEO of First Empire," and
his wish to "be his partner on any scale." The investment thus belongs to a small
family of commitments (alongside the larger [[Wells Fargo]] stake) driven primarily
by Buffett's judgment of a bank manager's quality.

## Evolution
- [1991] $40M 9% convertible preferred bought; sized below Berkshire's usual floor
  purely to back CEO [[Bob Wilmers]].
- [1995] The preferred (now valued at **$110M** on $40M cost — the second-best of the five
  convertible preferreds after Gillette) will be **called on March 31, 1996**, the earliest date
  allowable. Berkshire will **convert and keep the common**: "We are comfortable owning stock in
  well-run banks… Bob Wilmers… is an outstanding banker, and we love being associated with him."
- [1997] **Converted and held**, the common having "risen substantially, in line with the company's
  excellent performance": the **$40 million** committed in 1991 "had risen to **$236 million**" by
  year-end — grouped with [[Gillette]] as the two converted-preferred winners (vs. the once-troubled
  [[USAir]] and [[Salomon]], both now also resolved). [[Bob Wilmers]]'s bank continues to validate
  the manager-driven investment.
- [1999] Now appears in the look-through table under its modern name, **M&T Bank** (First Empire
  State Corp. having rebranded): Berkshire's **6.5%** stake contributed **$17M** of look-through
  earnings. Still a [[Bob Wilmers]]-driven holding, the smallest of the seven itemized investees.
