# HomeServices of America

Berkshire's **residential real-estate brokerage**, owned through **[[MidAmerican Energy]]** and
first highlighted in [[2001]] as a surprise inside the utility ("this may surprise you — we also
own the second-largest residential real estate brokerage business in the country"). Built by
**Ron Peltier** through acquisitions of locally-familiar brokerage brands, it was the market
leader in several large, mostly-Midwestern cities and handled about **106,000 transactions**
worth nearly **$20 billion** in 2001, with more deals (Atlanta, Southern California) added.

HomeServices grew steadily over the following two decades into one of the largest U.S.
residential brokerages by transaction volume — an acquisitive roll-up that compounded quietly
under the Berkshire/MidAmerican umbrella.

## Evolution
- [2001] First broken out; #2 U.S. residential broker, ~106,000 transactions; Ron Peltier
  building it by acquisition under MidAmerican.
- [2002] **Transactions doubled to $37B** (+100%) via three acquisitions, the largest **Prudential
  California Realty** (the LA/Orange/San Diego leader, $16B of closings). Operates under local
  brand names (CBS Omaha, Edina Realty Minneapolis, Iowa Realty Des Moines). "Though this business
  will always be cyclical, it's one we like" — and Berkshire keeps "an appetite for sensible
  acquisitions." Ron Peltier CEO.
- [2003] Transactions rose to **$48.6B** (+$11.7B), via 16 brokerage firms (each keeping its local
  identity) employing **16,343 brokers in 16 states** — "almost certain to grow substantially in the
  next decade as we continue to acquire leading localized operations."
- [2004] Transactions rose to **$59.8B** (+$11.2B) via 17 brokerage firms employing **18,000+
  brokers in 18 states** (six acquisitions during the year). Ron Peltier "building a brokerage
  powerhouse."
- [2005] **$64B** of transactions (+6.5%), 19,200 agents through 18 locally-branded firms. With the
  "white-hot" housing market cooling, Buffett expects "**additional acquisition possibilities**" and
  a far larger HomeServices a decade out.
- [2006] Volume **fell 9% to $58B** (profits **−50%**) as the housing boom ended (20,300 agents, 20
  firms). Buffett uses the slowdown to diagnose the coming subprime crisis — "teaser" rates and
  "optional" contracts ("a rolling loan gathers no loss"; the "Scarlett O'Hara" trap) — yet still
  intends to buy more brokerages ("a decade from now, HomeServices will almost certainly be much
  larger").
- [2009]–[2011] Survived the housing collapse, staying modestly profitable (a "terrible year for home
  sales") and adding brokerages opportunistically; ~16,000 agents under Ron Peltier.
- [2012] **The franchise pivot — and the Berkshire Hathaway HomeServices rebrand.** Agents (~16,000)
  participated in **$42 billion** of home sales (+33%). HomeServices bought **67% of the Prudential
  and Real Living franchise operations** (544 licensed brokerages, with an option to buy the rest
  within five years) and will **rebrand both its owned firms and its franchisees as "Berkshire
  Hathaway HomeServices."** Buffett notes it "incongruously" sits in the *Regulated, Capital-Intensive*
  section only because it arrived inside [[MidAmerican Energy]] in 2000. Ron Peltier "has done an
  outstanding job… during a depressed period"; earnings expected to rise as housing strengthens.
- [[2016]] **The #2 U.S. brokerage, still acquiring.** HomeServices now owned **38 realty firms with
  29,000+ agents in 28 states** (the **second-largest** brokerage, "far behind the leader, Realogy"),
  participating in **244,000 "sides" ($86B volume)**. It bought four realtors, including **Houlihan
  Lawrence** (Westchester County leader). Buffett: "we expect to acquire many realtors and franchisees
  during the next decade."
- [[2017]] **"Growth exploded."** Three big purchases — **Long and Foster** (the **#3** U.S. operator),
  **Houlihan Lawrence** (#12), and **Gloria Nilson** — added **12,300 agents** (to **40,950 total**),
  bringing HomeServices "close to leading the country in home sales" with **$127 billion of "sides"** (pro
  forma). Yet it is still "on track to do only about **3%** of the country's home-brokerage business in
  2018. That leaves 97% to go" — so Berkshire "will keep adding brokers in this most fundamental of
  businesses."
