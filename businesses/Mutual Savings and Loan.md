# Mutual Savings and Loan

Mutual Savings and Loan Association was a California thrift held within
[[Wesco Financial]] (and thus the [[Blue Chip Stamps]] complex). It appears in
Berkshire's Sources of Earnings tables from the late-1970s letters. In [[1980]]
Buffett singles out "the superb job done by Louie Vincenti and [[Charlie Munger]]
in repositioning Mutual Savings and Loan," which included selling its branch offices
(a ~$5.9M pre-tax gain) and shrinking the thrift's exposure as the savings-and-loan
industry headed into the rate-driven trouble of the early 1980s — an early instance
of Munger's defensive instincts steering Berkshire clear of a sector about to
implode.

## Evolution
- [1978–79] Contributes steady earnings within Wesco.
- [1980] Repositioned by Vincenti and Munger; branch offices sold; exposure reduced
  ahead of the S&L industry's troubles.
- [1988] Now **fully consolidated** into Berkshire's GAAP statements (previously
  "one-line"), prompting the new four-sector supplementary reporting. It holds
  Berkshire's **[[Freddie Mac]]** preferred (the legal maximum for a thrift), carried at
  cost rather than market. [[Charlie Munger]]'s Wesco letter gives what Buffett calls the
  best description he's seen of the unfolding savings-and-loan crisis.
