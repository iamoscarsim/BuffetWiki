# Disney

The Walt Disney Company became a large Berkshire holding in early 1996 when Berkshire
**exchanged its [[Capital Cities]]/ABC shares for cash plus Disney stock** in the
Disney/Cap Cities merger ([[1995]]). For its 20 million Cap Cities shares Berkshire elected
stock (each Cap Cities share converting to one Disney share plus $65, subject to allocation),
expecting "something over 20 million Disney shares," and bought additional Disney stock in
the market. Buffett: "happy to be once again a large owner of a business with both unique
assets and outstanding management."

The "once again" is a knowing nod to one of his earliest misadventures: he first studied
Disney in **1966** (market value under $90M against ~$21M of 1965 pre-tax earnings and net
cash — "a company selling at only five times rides," with the $17M Pirates of the Caribbean
about to open), and the Buffett Partnership bought at a split-adjusted **31¢** — only for
Buffett to sell out in 1967 at **48¢** (the stock later reaching $66). Buffett also reveals he
helped **broker the Disney/Cap Cities merger itself**: a chance Sun Valley encounter on July
14, 1995 with Disney CEO **Michael Eisner** and Cap Cities CEO **[[Tom Murphy]]** unstuck the
long-stalled cash-vs-stock impasse.

## Evolution
- [1995] Becomes a major holding via the early-1996 Disney/Cap Cities merger (>20M Disney
  shares for Berkshire's Cap Cities stake, plus market purchases). Buffett's 1960s Disney
  round-trip (31¢→48¢) recalled; he brokered the merger at Sun Valley.
- [1996] First appears as an **itemized holding**: **24,614,214 shares, cost $577.0M, market
  $1,716.8M** (~3.6% of the company), the result of the early-1996 Cap Cities exchange plus
  market purchases. Look-through share of Disney's earnings: **$50M**. Held quietly among the
  eight over-$500M positions.
- [1997] Berkshire **trimmed** the holding to **21,563,414 shares** (cost $381.2M / market
  **$2,134.8M**, ~3.2%) — one of "a few of the larger positions that we … modestly trimmed" in a
  year of ~5% net portfolio sales. Look-through share **$65M**.
- [1998] Shown at **51,202,242 shares** (cost $281.2M / market **$1,536M**) — but the **market
  value fell** from $2,134.8M despite the larger share count, as Disney stock declined. One of the
  major investees with "operating shortfalls" that pulled Berkshire's equity portfolio below the
  S&P. *(Berkshire would steadily exit Disney over the following years.)*
