# FINOVA Group

A troubled finance company whose 2000–2001 distress became one of Berkshire's larger
distressed-debt operations ([[2001]]). FINOVA had ~$11 billion of debt; beginning in late 2000
Berkshire bought **13% of it at about two-thirds of face**, expecting bankruptcy but judging
that an orderly liquidation would pay creditors well above cost.

As default loomed, Berkshire joined **[[Leucadia National]]** to present a **prepackaged
bankruptcy plan** (approved August 10, 2001): creditors received 70% of face in cash (plus
full interest) and a new 7½% note for the remaining 30%. To fund the cash distribution,
Leucadia and Berkshire formed a jointly-owned entity — **Berkadia** — which borrowed **$5.6
billion** (a syndication led by FleetBoston; see Chad Gifford) and re-lent it to FINOVA with a
priority claim. Berkshire **guaranteed 90%** of the Berkadia borrowing and earned 90% of the
~2-point spread between FINOVA's rate and Berkadia's cost. September 11th impaired FINOVA's
aircraft-heavy receivables — and triggered the market-close "out" that let Berkshire terminate
a related bond tender — but Buffett expected the transaction to "prove satisfactory."

## Evolution
- [2001] Berkadia ($5.6B, 90% Berkshire-guaranteed) funds FINOVA's prepackaged bankruptcy;
  Leucadia handles day-to-day operations. 9/11 dimmed but did not derail the prospects.
