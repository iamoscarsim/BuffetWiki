# Goldman Sachs

The Wall Street investment bank, and one of the three crisis-era fixed-income commitments Berkshire
made in 2008 "on terms that would be unavailable in normal markets" (alongside [[General Electric]]
and [[Wrigley]]). At the height of the September 2008 panic Berkshire bought **$5 billion of Goldman
Sachs perpetual preferred** paying a **10% dividend** (callable at a 10% premium), and received
**warrants** to buy $5 billion of common stock — a "substantial equity participation… as a bonus"
on top of an already-satisfactory current yield ([[2008]]). The deal was emblematic of Buffett's
crisis posture: "**pessimism is your friend, euphoria the enemy**," and of his insistence on
deploying Berkshire's fortress liquidity precisely when frightened sellers needed a credible,
deep-pocketed buyer (see [[Financial Strength]]).

The three 2008 commitments totaled **$14.5 billion**; to fund them Buffett sold portions of
[[Johnson & Johnson]], [[Procter & Gamble]] and [[ConocoPhillips]] he would otherwise have kept,
rather than draw down Berkshire's cash cushion ("we never want to count on the kindness of
strangers").

## Evolution
- [2008] $5B of 10% perpetual preferred + $5B of common warrants bought during the financial
