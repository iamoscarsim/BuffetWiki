# U.S. Liability

U.S. Liability is a medium-sized, highly-respected writer of unusual risks — **"excess and
surplus lines"** in insurance jargon — that Berkshire acquired in 2000 (with two sister
companies) in a **half-stock, half-cash** deal. The Berry family had owned it for **49
years**; **[[Ron Ferguson]]** of [[General Re]] put Buffett in touch with Bob Berry, and
"after Bob and I got in touch, we agreed by phone."

The operation has been run by **Tom Nerney**, who "has achieved a rare combination of
excellent growth and unusual profitability." Buffett's verdict on the franchise: "Few
property-casualty companies are outstanding businesses. We have far more than our share,
and U.S. Liability adds luster to the collection." (Nerney also coaches the Philadelphia
Belles, a young-teen girls' basketball team that went 62-4.) U.S. Liability joined
Berkshire's cluster of small, superbly-run primary insurers that collectively post strong
underwriting profits and generate float (see [[Insurance Float]], [[National Indemnity Company]]).
