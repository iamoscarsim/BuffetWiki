# Champion International

Champion International Corp., a paper and forest-products company, was the third of
the convertible preferreds Berkshire bought in [[1989]]. Berkshire purchased **$300
million** of Champion preferred with a **9.25% dividend**, mandatory redemption in ten
years, and conversion into common at **$38 per share**.

Like [[USAir]] and unlike [[Gillette]], Champion was a business whose economics Buffett
did not pretend to forecast — paper being a cyclical, commodity-leaning industry ("we're
agnostics, not atheists"). The preferred structure was again the hedge: a mediocre but
protected return if the industry struggled, with equity upside if it prospered. The bet
turned on CEO **Andy Sigler**, whom Buffett liked and trusted, and on Berkshire's
unrestricted converted-basis voting rights. Champion eventually repurchased the
preferred in the mid-1990s; the position produced a modest return — the "preferred-stock
yield during a period when the typical preferred stock would have held no appeal" that
Buffett warned was the disappointing-case outcome.

## Evolution
- [1989] $300M 9.25% convertible preferred (convert at $38; 10-yr redemption). CEO Andy
  Sigler. A paper-industry bet structured as a preferred because Buffett can't forecast
  the economics; one of the "four preferreds" with [[Salomon]], [[Gillette]], [[USAir]].
- [1990] Held; valued at year-end "about what we paid" — most of its worth residing in its
  fixed-income characteristics, not the conversion option (Buffett corrects press accounts
  that valued the preferreds purely on conversion value).
- [1995] **Exited.** Champion's right to **call the preferred at 115% of cost** "forced a move
  out of us last August that we would rather have delayed"; Berkshire converted just before the
  call and sold the common back to the company (which wanted to repurchase shares). The result: a
  **modest ~19% after-tax capital gain over six years** plus a good after-tax dividend yield —
  about the disappointing-but-acceptable outcome Buffett had warned a preferred could deliver.
  "I can't remember ever owning the common stock of a paper producer in my 54 years of investing."
  Cost $300M / value ~$388M (proceeds from the converted-common sale).
- [1997] Recalled in the convertible-preferred wrap-up as the one already exited "at the time of
  that earlier report" (1995), on which "we had realized a small profit" — leaving the four others
  ([[Gillette]], [[First Empire State]], [[USAir]], [[Salomon]]), all of which then "grown
  significantly in value."
