# Central States Indemnity

Central States Indemnity is an Omaha insurer that makes the monthly payments owed by
credit-card holders who become disabled or unemployed — a credit-card payment-
protection writer. Berkshire bought **82%** in December 1992, the parent company's only
acquisition of the year and one Buffett calls "a **prototype** of what we now look for"
([[1992]]). Annual premiums were about $90M and profits about $10M; the **[[Bill Kizer]]**
family (sons Bill, Dick, and John) retained 18% and continued to run the business.

Buffett draws an explicit parallel to Berkshire's very first acquisition 26 years
earlier: like [[Jack Ringwalt]] with [[National Indemnity Company]], Bill Kizer — a
friend of 35 years who built the business himself — "thought of me when he wished to
sell." Ringwalt's line ("if I don't sell the company, my executor will, and I'd rather
pick the home for it") frames the recurring Berkshire pitch to owner-builders. "Hollywood
has had good luck with sequels; I believe we, too, will."
