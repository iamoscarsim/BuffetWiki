# Moody's

The credit-rating agency, which entered Berkshire's equity portfolio as a new position
disclosed in [[2001]] (**24,000,000 shares**, cost $499M, market $957M). Berkshire received the
Moody's stake when **Dun & Bradstreet spun the rating business off** in 2000. Moody's is an
archetypal asset-light, high-return **franchise** ([[Commodity vs Franchise Economics]],
[[Economic Moat]]) — a duopolist (with S&P) whose ratings were effectively embedded in
regulation and bond-market practice, earning huge margins on little capital.

The position is notable in hindsight: Berkshire held it through the **2008 financial crisis**,
when the rating agencies' role in stamping AAA on mortgage securities drew heavy criticism, and
trimmed it over subsequent years.

## Evolution
- [2001] First disclosed as a >$500M holding (24M shares, market $957M), received via the
  Dun & Bradstreet spin-off; a high-margin ratings franchise.
- [2008] **Crosses 20% → equity accounting.** Buffett notes Berkshire's stake rose above 20% not
  through buying (it had "not since bought a share") but because **Moody's repurchased its own
  shares**, shrinking the count. The holding is thus moved off the market-value table to **"equity
  value"** (cost plus retained earnings since purchase, less deferred tax) — the same treatment
  applied to [[Burlington Northern Santa Fe|BNSF]] this year. (Berkshire later trimmed the position.)
