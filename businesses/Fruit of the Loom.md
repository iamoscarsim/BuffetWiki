# Fruit of the Loom

The underwear and apparel maker, acquired by Berkshire **out of bankruptcy** in 2001–2002
([[2001]]) — a deal layered with Buffett's own history. The company had entered bankruptcy "a
victim both of too much debt and poor management": after **John Holland** (responsible for
"Fruit's operations in its most bountiful years") retired in 1996, management loaded it with
debt and made disappointing acquisitions. Holland was rehired, cut employment from a bloated
40,000 to 23,000, and restored deliveries, costs and customer relations.

Berkshire entered through the **distressed debt**, buying Fruit's public and bank debt at
about 50% of face (earning ~15% currently on the senior debt, which would "probably end up
returning about 70%"), then made creditors a financing-condition-free bid for the company —
insisting on one "very unusual proviso": **John Holland had to stay as CEO**. "To us, John and
the brand are Fruit's key assets" ([[Margin of Safety]] — distressed-debt as a path to
control).

The personal echo: in 1955, working at **Graham-Newman**, the young Buffett held shares in
**Philadelphia and Reading Coal and Iron (P&R)**, which bought Union Underwear — then a
licensee of the Fruit of the Loom name — from Jack Goldfarb for $15M (much of it on
non-interest-bearing notes; "Those were the days; I get goosebumps"). P&R's *second* purchase,
Acme Boot, also resurfaced in 2001 when [[H. H. Brown]] bought Acme's remnants. Buffett was
helped in the Fruit deal by his former boss **Micky Newman**, then 81 — "What goes around truly
does come around."

## Evolution
- [2001] Bid for the company out of bankruptcy (via its debt), conditioned on John Holland
  staying as CEO; closed 2002. A homecoming to Buffett's Graham-Newman P&R/Union Underwear
  history of the 1950s.
- [2002] Acquisition **completed** (April 30, 2002); Fruit produces ~**33.3%** of U.S. men's & boys'
  underwear. John Holland, 70, stayed on as CEO — Buffett trying to make Holland's "next retirement
  coincident with mine."
- [2003] Berkshire's **largest apparel operation**, with "three major assets: a 148-year-old…
  brand, a low-cost manufacturing operation, and **John Holland**." Fruit reached **42.3%** of U.S.
  men's & boys' underwear sold by mass marketers (Wal-Mart/Target/K-Mart) and grew its women's &
  girls' share to **13.9%** (from 11.3%).
- [2004] Unit sales **+14%** (10M dozen), women's & girls' intimate apparel **+31%** — prompting
  Buffett's joke that since Munger "assures me… women are not wearing more underwear," Fruit's market
  share "must be growing rapidly." "Thanks to John Holland, Fruit is on the move."
- [2005] Men's & boys' underwear share among mass-marketers reached **48.7%** (from 44.2% at the
  2002 acquisition); women's & girls' intimates **24.7%** (from 13.7%). "A gain like that in a major
  category doesn't come easy. Thank John Holland."
- [2006] **Two big tuck-ins** under John Holland: **[[Russell Athletic|Russell Corp.]]** (~$1.2B;
  athletic apparel/uniforms) and **VF Corp.'s intimate-apparel** business — together adding ~**$2.2B
  of sales and 23,000 employees**. Fruit becomes Berkshire's vehicle for "leverag[ing] the
  managerial talent already with us."
