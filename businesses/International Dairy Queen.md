# International Dairy Queen

International Dairy Queen (IDQ) is the franchisor of the Dairy Queen system, agreed to in
1997 and **closed early in 1998**. At purchase it had **5,792 Dairy Queen stores in 23
countries** (nearly all run by franchisees), plus **409 Orange Julius** and **43
Karmelkorn** operations, with 190 combined "treat centers." After a "bumpy history," a
Minneapolis group led by **John Mooty** and Rudy Luther took control in 1970, rationalized
a tangle of franchise agreements and unwise financing, and built "a strong organization";
**Mike Sullivan** was CEO at the time of the sale.

The deal arose when Luther's 1997 death forced his estate to sell. Buffett structured it
"patterned on our [[FlightSafety International|FlightSafety]] acquisition" — a cash-or-stock
option tilted (slightly lower immediate value for stock) to **encourage holders to take
cash**, "the type of payment we by far prefer" (see [[Issuing Shares for Acquisitions]]);
even so, only **45%** elected cash. Buffett and Munger claimed "a modicum of product
expertise": Munger had patronized the Dairy Queens of Cass Lake and Bemidji, Minnesota
"for decades," and Buffett "a regular in Omaha" — "We have put our money where our mouth
is." The annual-meeting ritual later added a pilgrimage to the 114th Street Omaha Dairy
Queen.

## Evolution
- [1997] Acquired (announced 1997, closed early 1998); 5,792 DQ stores + Orange Julius +
