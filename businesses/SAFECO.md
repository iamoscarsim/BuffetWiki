# SAFECO

SAFECO Corporation was a property-and-casualty insurer Berkshire bought into as a
stock holding, introduced in the [[1978]] letter (953,750 shares; cost $23.9M,
market $26.5M). Buffett calls it "probably the best run large property and
casualty insurance company in the United States" — superb underwriting,
conservative loss reserving, sensible investing — "a much better insurance
operation than our own."

SAFECO carries two of Buffett's most important arguments. First, **buy the best
through the market, cheap**: he paid less than book value ("less than 100 cents on
the dollar") for the best company in the business, while mediocre companies fetched
far more than book in negotiated deals. Second, **[[Look-Through Earnings]]**: only
~18% of SAFECO's earnings reached Berkshire as dividends, but the retained 82% was
"just as real in terms of eventual benefit to us" — and at a company that reinvests
so well, may be worth more than 100 cents on the dollar. He sees no loss in passive
minority ownership of superb management: "if one controlled a company run as well
as SAFECO, the proper policy also would be to sit back and let management do its
job."

## Evolution
- [1978] New holding (953,750 shares). Used to argue for buying the best insurer
  below book through the market and for the value of investees' retained earnings.
- [1979] Held at cost $23.9M / market $35.5M; grouped with [[GEICO]] as one of "two
  truly outstanding management groups" Berkshire owns indirectly.
