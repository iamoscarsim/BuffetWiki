# McLane Company

A giant wholesale **distributor of groceries and non-food items** to convenience stores, drug
stores, wholesale clubs, mass merchandisers, quick-service restaurants and theaters, acquired by
Berkshire from **Wal-Mart** in 2003 ([[2003]]). With ~**$23 billion of sales** on "paper-thin"
**~1% pre-tax margins**, McLane "swell[s] Berkshire's sales figures far more than our income" — a
high-volume, low-margin, capital-efficient distributor.

The deal is a model of Berkshire's frictionless dealmaking: sourced by **[[Byron Trott]]** of
Goldman Sachs, it took a single two-hour meeting with Wal-Mart CFO Tom Schoewe, a handshake, **no
due diligence** ("We knew everything would be exactly as Wal-Mart said it would be — and it was"),
and money in Wal-Mart's hands **29 days** later. CEO **Grady Rosier** had already begun winning
accounts from retailers who had previously shunned McLane for being owned by their competitor
Wal-Mart.

## Evolution
- [2003] Acquired from Wal-Mart (sourced by Byron Trott); ~$23B sales, ~1% pre-tax margins; CEO
  Grady Rosier; handshake deal, no due diligence, $150M pre-tax from the May 23 closing.
- [2004] First full year; Rosier won former-competitor accounts, and McLane earned solid returns on
  its modest capital despite the thin margins — exactly the high-volume distribution business
  Buffett expected.
