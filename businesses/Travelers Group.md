# Travelers Group

Travelers Group entered Berkshire's portfolio in 1997 **not by purchase but by merger**:
**[[Salomon]] Inc merged into Travelers Group**, so Berkshire's long-troubled Salomon
stake (the $700M convertible preferred bought in 1987 and the common it later held)
converted into Travelers shares. At year-end 1997 Berkshire held **23,733,198 shares (cost
$604.4M, market $1,278.6M)** — a $750M+ position itemized for the first time.

The merger was the happy ending to the Salomon saga (the 1991 Treasury-auction scandal,
Buffett's interim chairmanship, the long rebuild). Buffett credited **Deryck Maughan** and
**Bob Denham** with "first, playing key roles in saving Salomon from extinction … and,
second, restoring the vitality of the company to a level that made it an attractive
acquisition for Travelers." Berkshire's final Salomon results "will be far better than I
anticipated two years ago." (Travelers, led by Sandy Weill, would soon merge with Citicorp
to form Citigroup.)
