# World Book

World Book is the encyclopedia publisher that is the largest of the 17 businesses
inside [[Scott Fetzer]], acquired with it after year-end 1985. It dominates the U.S.
direct-sales encyclopedia market, and Buffett treats it as a small franchise with
genuine pricing power and a missionary salesforce.

In the [[1986]] letter — its first detailed appearance — World Book's encyclopedia
unit sales rose **7% over 1985 and 45% over 1982** (a fourth consecutive year of
gains), with Childcraft also growing. Buffett admires the product on the merits:
"extraordinarily well-edited and priced at under 5 cents per page," with an unusual
editing discipline (44,000 words ranked by difficulty, so longer entries open in
simple language and escalate gradually). "Selling World Book is a calling" — over half
the active salespeople are current or former teachers, another 5% former librarians,
and they "correctly think of themselves as educators." Its direct-sales model, like
[[Kirby]]'s, faced the challenge of "the era of the working woman" but kept growing.

## Evolution
- [1985] Joined Berkshire inside [[Scott Fetzer]] (~40% of its sales; U.S. leader,
  outselling its four biggest rivals combined).
- [1986] Encyclopedia units +7% (and +45% over 1982); under-5-cents-per-page value;
  teacher/librarian salesforce; "selling World Book is a calling."
- [1987] U.S. unit sales up a **fifth consecutive year**; international sales and profits
  grew substantially. Launched its **most dramatically revised edition since 1962** (color
  photos 14,000 → 24,000; 6,000+ articles revised; 840 new contributors). One of the
  **[[Sainted Seven]]**.
- [1995] **"Berkshire's most difficult problem."** Pre-tax earnings fell sharply (to $8.8M from
  $24.7M) as the print encyclopedia faced "increasingly tough competition from **CD-ROM and
  on-line offerings**." Still profitable — "a claim that perhaps no other print encyclopedia can
  make" — but "sales and earnings trends have gone in the wrong direction." At year-end 1995 World
  Book **overhauled distribution**, stepped up electronic products, and "sharply reduced its
  overhead costs"; Buffett is "confident they will significantly improve our viability." One of
  three units Buffett flagged for declining earnings (with the Shoe Group and [[Buffalo Evening News]]) — "just tell me the bad news."
- [1996] **A rebound** off the 1995 trough: pre-tax earnings recovered to **$12.6M** (from
  $8.8M). World Book became "the **only direct-seller of encyclopedias** in the country"
  after **Encyclopedia Britannica exited** the field — yet *unit volume still fell*, and the
  company spent heavily on a new **CD-ROM product** (launched in early 1997 with **IBM**).
  Earnings "would have evaporated" but for the 1995 distribution overhaul and overhead cuts,
  which "dramatically reduc[ed] its fixed costs" — leaving the business "a long way toward
  assuring its long-term viability in both the print and electronic marketplaces."
