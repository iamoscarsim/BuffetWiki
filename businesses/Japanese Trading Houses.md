# Japanese Trading Houses

The five great Japanese **sōgō shōsha** (general trading companies) — **Itochu, Mitsubishi, Mitsui,
Sumitomo, and Marubeni** — in which Berkshire disclosed roughly **5% stakes each** in [[2020]] (announced
August 2020, on Buffett's 90th birthday). Only **Itochu** is itemized in the [[2020]] equity table (81.3M
shares, 5.1%, cost $1,862M / market $2,336M); the others sit in "Others." Acquired patiently over ~12
months on the open market, the positions were funded largely by **yen-denominated bonds** Berkshire had
issued — a natural currency hedge — making the stakes close to self-financing.

The trading houses are sprawling, diversified, conservatively-run conglomerates (energy, metals, food,
machinery, trading) with **modest valuations and decent dividends** — a structure that echoes Berkshire's
own. Buffett signaled these would be **very long-term holdings** ("passive… for a very long time"), with a
contractual pledge to keep stakes below 9.9%. The move was Berkshire's largest stride yet into investing
**across borders** (the ambition voiced in [[2018]]'s "American Tailwind" coda that "other countries… have
bright futures").

## Evolution
- [[2020]] ~5% stakes in each of the five Japanese trading houses (Itochu itemized; rest in "Others"),
  bought over ~12 months and largely funded with yen bonds — a long-term, cross-border bet on
  Berkshire-like diversified conglomerates.
- [[2021]] **Itemized in the portfolio.** Three of the five — **Itochu (5.6%), Mitsubishi (5.5%), Mitsui
  (5.7%)** — now appear among Berkshire's fifteen largest holdings (combined cost ~$5.8B, market ~$7.5B);
  the positions kept growing toward the eventual ~9.9% ceilings.
- [[2023]] **Raised to ~9% each, after a Tokyo visit.** Buffett and **[[Greg Abel]]** traveled to Tokyo to
  meet the managements, then increased all five stakes (Itochu, Marubeni, Mitsubishi, Mitsui, Sumitomo) to
  **~9% each**, with a pledge to stay below **9.9%**. Cost **¥1.6 trillion** / year-end value **¥2.9
  trillion** — a **+$8 billion (61%)** unrealized gain in dollars, **financed largely with ¥1.3 trillion
  of yen bonds** (a natural hedge; the weak yen produced a separate $1.9B GAAP gain). Buffett praises their
  shareholder-friendly conduct (share buybacks, restrained executive pay, ~⅓ payout) and flags possible
  **partnerships** "around the world." The purchases began **July 4, 2019**; building them "is like turning
  a battleship."
- [[2024]] **The 9.9% ceiling relaxed — "for many decades."** Aggregate cost **$13.8B** vs. year-end value
  **$23.5B**. As Berkshire approached its self-imposed <10% cap, the five companies "agreed to moderately
  relax the ceiling," so ownership "will likely… increase somewhat." Buffett expects "**Greg and his
  eventual successors will be holding this Japanese position for many decades**" and to find ways to
  "work productively with the five." The yen-debt funding has produced **$2.3B of cumulative after-tax FX
  gains** ($850M in 2024); 2025 expected dividends ~$812M vs. ~$135M of yen-interest cost.
