# Equitas

The runoff vehicle created in 1996 to hold and pay **all pre-1993 [[Lloyd's of London|Lloyd's]]
liabilities** — chiefly the asbestos, pollution and health claims that nearly destroyed Lloyd's and
ruined many of its 27,972 "names." In 2006 ([[2006]]) Berkshire wrote Equitas a **huge retroactive
reinsurance** policy, the largest of the many such deals that made it "the reinsurer of choice" for
long-tail liabilities.

The terms: Equitas paid Berkshire **$7.12 billion** of cash and securities (boosting [[Insurance Float|float]] ~$7B), and Berkshire agreed to **pay all future claims and expenses up to $13.9
billion** — $5.7B above Equitas's own estimate of its ultimate liabilities. That gave the names
"almost certainly sufficient" protection (Equitas could even return cash to them). CEO **Scott
Moser**: "Names wanted to sleep easy at night, and we think we've just bought them the **world's best
mattress**." For Berkshire the bet is the usual retroactive calculus — accept a near-certain
underwriting loss (the DCRA amortization) in exchange for decades of investable float — and "Ajit
and I think the odds are in our favor. And should we be wrong, Berkshire can handle it."

## Evolution
- [2006] Berkshire takes $7.12B to cover Equitas's pre-1993 Lloyd's claims up to $13.9B — the
  marquee retroactive-reinsurance deal; the post-Equitas annual DCRA charge rises to ~$450M.
