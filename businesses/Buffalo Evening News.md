# Buffalo Evening News

The Buffalo Evening News was a newspaper bought by [[Blue Chip Stamps]] in 1977.
It first appears in the Sources of Earnings table in the [[1978]] letter showing a
pre-tax loss of $2.9 million — the cost of a bruising early period that included a
celebrated antitrust fight after the News launched a Sunday edition and a
competitor sued. The paper eventually won its market, became the dominant daily in
Buffalo, and turned into a highly profitable franchise — Buffett's prime example of
the economics of a city's surviving newspaper. Its arc (heavy early losses → near-
monopoly profitability → later structural decline of newspapers) makes it a
recurring case study, especially for [[Evolving Views — Newspapers]].

## Evolution
- [1978] Pre-tax loss of $2.9M; early, money-losing phase under Blue Chip
  ownership amid the Sunday-edition antitrust battle.
- [1979–81] Continued losses ($4.6M pre-tax in 1979) during the circulation war with
  the rival Courier-Express.
- [1982] Turning point: Sunday circulation hit 367,000 — a **35% gain** over the
  Courier-Express's pre-News 272,000 — "no city... with a long history of seven-day
  newspaper publication" had grown Sunday penetration "at anything like this rate."
  Among the 14 largest U.S. dailies by weekday circulation. Managers Henry Urban,
  Stan Lipsey, Murray Light and others credited.
- [1983] **Profitable** — exceeded its 10% after-tax margin target (helped by a
  newsprint-cost fluke). Renamed the **Buffalo News** with a new morning edition.
  After the rival Courier-Express closed, the News became Buffalo's monopoly daily and
  ranked **#1 in weekday penetration among the 100 largest U.S. papers** (#3 Sunday).
  Buffett attributes success to a stable population, editorial integrity (editors
  Alfred Kirchhofer, Murray Light), and an unusually large 50% "news hole." Stan
  Lipsey became publisher on Henry Urban's retirement. The turnaround vindicates the
  Sunday-edition gamble.
- [1984] Very profitable; costs well-controlled by Stan Lipsey (a union "catch-up"
  raise will pressure 1985 margins). Buffett lays out the **economics of a dominant
  newspaper** — "among the very best in the business world" — and the stark point that
  even a "third-rate" paper prospers if dominant (see [[Evolving Views — Newspapers]]).
  News hole 50.9%, far above the typical 35–40%, a chosen quality standard. #1 in
  weekday penetration among the 100 largest U.S. papers.
- [1986] **Ten years owned.** Pre-tax earnings $34.7M; [[Stan Lipsey]]'s third straight
  year of lower man-hours and tight cost control lifted margins, even with below-
  industry ad-rate increases. Highest weekday penetration of any of the **top 50** U.S.
  papers; Sunday penetration **83%** (vs. the old Courier-Express's ~63% a decade
  earlier), selling ~100,000 more Sunday copies than the Courier-Express did despite a
  shrinking market-area population. Keeps a **50% news hole** (vs. ~40% at comparable
  papers) — a quality standard Buffett vows not to cut even though a newsprint price
  rise means margins "almost certainly have peaked." Credits editor Murray Light.
- [1987] National leader among dominant papers in both **penetration** and **news-hole
  %** — "it may not be coincidence that one newspaper leads in both." Seven fresh
  editions every 24 hours (the obituary page redone seven times a day). Buffett uses the
  News to reject **flexible operating budgets**: he won't cut the ~50% news hole (a $4M
  newsprint saving) or See's quality when profits dip, nor add bloat when money rolls in
  — "that kind of yo-yo approach is neither business-like nor humane" ("but what about
  the corporate jet? …occasionally a man must rise above principle"). [[Stan Lipsey]]'s
  management produced excellent profit growth on only moderate revenue growth; Buffett
  warns margins "will, without question, shrink in 1988" on skyrocketing newsprint costs.
  One of the **[[Sainted Seven]]**.
- [1988] [[Stan Lipsey]] "made me look foolish": after Buffett "unequivocally" forecast
  falling margins, Stan *improved* them "a tad" despite industry-norm newsprint and wage
  increases — "no one in the newspaper business has a better managerial record." News
  hole **49.5%** (vs. 49.8% in 1987), a commitment held "around 50%, whatever the level
  or trend of profit margins." Twelve years owned; editor Murray Light still credited.
- [1989] **Seventh consecutive profit record** (in a year profits slipped at many major
  papers); #1 in both daily and Sunday household penetration; news hole **50.1%**. Buffett
  ties the three together — a rich news hole boosts penetration, which makes the paper a
  uniquely valuable single "megaphone" to advertisers. Erie County's population kept
  falling (1.11M in 1970 → ~966K in 1988) even as Sunday circulation rose to 292,700.
  Credits editor Murray Light and judges [[Stan Lipsey]]'s management worth "at least
  **five extra percentage points** in profit margin" vs. an average manager (Lipsey had
  helped win a 1972 Pulitzer at the Sun Newspapers of Omaha).
- [1990] **The franchise cracks.** Earnings fell only 5% (far better than most metro
  papers), but Buffett warns media's "**pricing power**" has structurally weakened (more ad
  channels, slower ad growth), "**materially reduc[ing] the intrinsic value**" of the News —
  "though [it] remain[s] [a] fine business." 1991 earnings "will fall substantially"
  (higher newsprint and labor costs against soft revenue). News hole rose to **52.3%** —
  but only because *ad* pages fell; Buffett vows to hold "at least a 50% news hole," since
  "cutting product quality is not a proper response to adversity." See
  [[Evolving Views — Newspapers]].
- [1991] The predicted decline lands: pre-tax earnings fell to **$37.1M** (from $44.0M in
  1990), consistent with Buffett's 1990 warning that media earnings were "sure to decline." He
  formalizes the cause in the **franchise → business** essay (see [[Commodity vs Franchise Economics]]): media properties have lost "some — though far from all — of [their] franchise
  strength," and the News's **intrinsic value has declined materially**. Still, "under
  [[Stan Lipsey]]'s leadership, [it] has done far better than most newspapers," and Buffett
  reaffirms holding it under the no-sell rule for permanent operations.
- [1995] Pre-tax earnings slipped to **$46.8M** (from $54.2M), and the News is one of three units
  Buffett flags for **declining earnings**. Updating the 1991 thesis: newspapers have now "**lost
  still another notch**" in economic attractiveness, and "we expect the competitive strength of
  newspapers to gradually erode" — "though the industry should nevertheless remain a fine business
  for many years to come." Still doing "very well in comparison to other newspapers." See
  [[Evolving Views — Newspapers]].
- [2006] **"The days of lush profits… are over."** In the landmark newspaper-decline essay (see
  [[Evolving Views — Newspapers]]), Buffett finally calls the structural end: the internet/cable have
  broken the "Survival of the Fattest" monopoly economics. The News still has "**the highest
  [penetration] among… this country's large newspapers**" (Stan Lipsey; editor Margaret Sullivan) and
  the leading local website, but "faces unrelenting pressures that will cause profit margins to
  slide." Berkshire keeps it (economic principle 11; "Charlie and I love newspapers… a free and
  energetic press is a key ingredient… of a great democracy"), aiming to build a "sustainable
  business model."
- [2012] **Stan Lipsey retires; the paper anchors Berkshire's newspaper expansion.** As Berkshire
  bought 28 dailies, the Buffalo News (held its revenue loss to ~3%, near the top of large U.S. papers
  in home-market penetration) became a model for the **local-news + paywall** thesis (see [[Evolving Views — Newspapers]]). **[[Stan Lipsey]] retired** as publisher — "the News might now be extinct were
  it not for Stan." Editor Margaret Sullivan left for The New York Times.
