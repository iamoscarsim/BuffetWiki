# PetroChina

The Chinese state-controlled oil company, a new equity holding first itemized in [[2003]]
(**2,338,961,000 H-shares**, cost $488M, market $1,340M, ~1.3% of the company) — one of Berkshire's
rare large **foreign-stock** bets and one of its most celebrated round-trips. Buffett bought after
reading PetroChina's annual report and judging the whole company worth ~$100B while the market
priced it at ~$35B — a wide [[Margin of Safety]] in an understandable (oil) business at a low price.

The position was sold in 2007 as the share price tripled-plus and the implied valuation approached
Buffett's estimate of value; Berkshire realized a gain of roughly **$3.5 billion** on the ~$488M
cost. The sale also drew political attention because of PetroChina parent CNPC's operations in
Sudan (the Darfur divestment campaign), though Buffett framed the decision as **valuation-driven**.

## Evolution
- [2003] First itemized (~1.3%, cost $488M / market $1,340M); bought on a wide value gap (≈$35B
  market vs. ≈$100B estimated value) read straight from the annual report.
- [2007] **Sold** as the price tripled-plus toward intrinsic value — a ~$3.5B gain on a ~$488M cost;
  a textbook value round-trip (amid, but framed as independent of, the Sudan/Darfur controversy).
