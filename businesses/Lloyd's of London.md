# Lloyd's of London

Not an insurance company but a centuries-old **marketplace** where member-insurers ("syndicates,"
backed by passive investors called "**names**") transact, dating to **Edward Lloyd's** ~1688 London
coffee house. Buffett recounts its history in the [[2006]] letter as the backdrop to Berkshire's
**[[Equitas]]** retroactive-reinsurance deal. Names "joined expecting to pick up some extra change
without effort," accepting in theory "unlimited and everlasting liability… down to the last
cufflink" — a warning "three hundred years of retained cufflinks" had rendered "perfunctory."

Then **asbestos** and the environmental/product-liability "tidal wave" of the 1980s "began to
implode" Lloyd's: decades-old policies developed tens of billions in losses; "many names opted for
bankruptcy; some even chose suicide." The 1996 rescue pooled all pre-1993 liabilities into
**[[Equitas]]** — which Berkshire reinsured a decade later. The saga is Buffett's cautionary tale of
**long-tail liabilities** and the danger of promises whose true cost surfaces only decades on.
