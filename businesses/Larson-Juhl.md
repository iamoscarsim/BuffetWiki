# Larson-Juhl

The U.S. (and Canadian, and much-of-Europe) leader in **custom-made picture-frame** moulding,
serving about 18,000 framing shops, acquired by Berkshire in late 2001 ([[2001]], closing
early 2002). Owner **Craig Ponzio** — who had worked in the company's manufacturing plant while
in college, then bought it in 1981 and grew sales from **$3 million to $300 million** — called
Buffett on December 3rd. Buffett "had never heard of Larson-Juhl before," but a few minutes'
talk (Ponzio was "straightforward… cared about who bought it, and was realistic as to price")
convinced him; Ponzio and CEO Steve McKenzie flew to Omaha and reached agreement in ninety
minutes, with a signed contract ten days later — a textbook example of Berkshire's
**"we answer the phone"** sourcing ([[Acquisition Criteria]], [[Permanent Ownership]]).

## Evolution
- [2001] Acquired (closed early 2002); expected to make complementary bolt-on acquisitions.
