# R.C. Willey Home Furnishings

R.C. Willey is Utah's leading home-furnishings retailer, acquired by Berkshire in mid-1995
([[1995]]) — the second of the year's "ABWA" (acquisitions by walking around) deals, this
one sourced by **Irv Blumkin** of [[Nebraska Furniture Mart]], who had told CEO **[[Bill Child]]** how pleased the Blumkin family was with its Berkshire relationship. When Child
mentioned (for estate-tax and diversification reasons) that he and the other owners might
sell, "things could not have been simpler": Child sent figures, Buffett wrote back his idea
of value, "we quickly agreed on a number," and by mid-year the merger (a stock exchange) was
done.

Child took over from his father-in-law in 1954 when sales were ~$250,000 and, aided by his
brother **Sheldon**, built R.C. Willey to **$257M of sales** and **>50% of Utah's furniture
business** — Mae West's philosophy, "it's not what you've got, it's what you do with what
you've got." Like NFM it sells appliances, electronics, computers and carpets as well as
furniture, and the two are similar in volume — but where NFM runs from one Omaha complex, R.C.
Willey was opening its **sixth major store**. Buffett used the purchase to distinguish a
"have-to-be-smart-**every-day**" business (retail, where "to coast is to fail") from a
"have-to-be-smart-once" business — and to praise managers like Bill and Sheldon, who run
autonomously: "We want them to feel that the businesses they run are *theirs*."

## Evolution
- [1995] Acquired mid-year via stock exchange (Irv Blumkin's introduction); $257M sales,
  >50% of Utah furniture; CEO Bill Child (with brother Sheldon); sixth store opening. First
  partial-year home-furnishings earnings (from 6/29/95) part of $29.7M pre-tax segment.
- [1997] **Bill Child pays the referral forward.** Asked (as the Blumkins had been) for industry
  standouts, Child named the remaining two on the Blumkins' original list — one being **[[Star Furniture]]** of Houston, which Berkshire then bought. "We have been delighted with that
  association — Bill is the perfect partner." (R.C. Willey sits with NFM and Star in the Home
  Furnishings segment, which earned $56.8M pre-tax in 1997.)
- [1999] **The Boise expansion** — the year's signature managerial story. [[Bill Child]] (a Mormon)
  keeps R.C. Willey **closed on Sundays**, yet had grown sales to **$342M**. Skeptical of taking the
  no-Sunday model into Boise against seven-day rivals, Buffett let Child run his own business —
  whereupon Child **personally built the ~$9M store**, to be sold to Berkshire at cost only if it
  succeeded (and absorbed by him if it failed). It "immediately became a huge success"; Child turned
  it over (with appreciated extra land) and **refused even interest**. By Buffett's grand-opening
  speech, "it all had come back to me: Opening a store in Boise had been my idea."
- [2004] **Las Vegas vindicates the out-of-state model.** A standout year: **41.9% of sales** now
  came from out-of-state stores that didn't exist before 1999. Buffett confesses he'd thought the
  closed-on-Sundays expansion "were mistakes": the first Las Vegas store (2001) instantly became the
  chain's #1 unit, and when [[Bill Child]] and successor CEO **Scott Hymas** proposed a *second* Las
  Vegas store 20 minutes away, Buffett feared cannibalization — instead each does ~26% more volume
  than any other store in the chain. "The opinion of someone who is always wrong has its own special
  utility to decision-makers" (Reno next).
- [2005] Same-store sales **+9.9%**; the never-on-Sunday model "continues to overwhelm seven-day
  competitors." Boise +21% (off +10% in 2004); **Reno** opened in November above Boise's early pace;
  **Sacramento** next — "if this store succeeds… Californians will see many more R.C. Willey stores."
