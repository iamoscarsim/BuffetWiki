# Occidental Petroleum

U.S. oil-and-gas producer to which Berkshire committed **$10 billion in April 2019** — **$10B of
preferred stock paying 8%, plus warrants** to buy common — to help finance Occidental's contested
acquisition of **Anadarko Petroleum** (outbidding Chevron). The structure echoes Berkshire's crisis-era
"call Warren" financings ([[Goldman Sachs]], [[General Electric]], [[Bank of America]]): a fat current
yield plus equity upside, supplied because Berkshire's balance sheet could move fast and large. It
appears in the [[2019]] letter inside the "Others" line of the equity table.

The deal proved poorly timed — oil prices and Occidental's shares slumped, and the 2020 crash forced the
company to pay part of the preferred dividend in stock. *(Berkshire would later, in 2022, turn buyer of
Occidental's **common** stock in size, eventually amassing a ~25%+ stake — a separate, much larger
chapter.)*

## Evolution
- [[2019]] **$10B of 8% preferred + warrants** to fund Occidental's Anadarko bid; held in "Others."
- [[2020]] The preferred (still in "Others") was **marked down to ~$9B** as oil prices and Occidental's
  shares slumped in the COVID year; the company even paid part of its preferred dividend in stock. A
  poorly-timed deal — though Berkshire's 2020 energy tilt also added **[[Chevron]]** common.
- [[2021]] The **$10B preferred** (still in "Others") was carried at **$10.7B**; oil's recovery had
  restored its value ahead of Berkshire's large 2022 move into Occidental *common*.
- [[2022]] **From financier to major common-stock owner.** Beyond the 2019 preferred, Berkshire bought
  Occidental **common stock** aggressively in 2022, becoming **its largest shareholder** (one of the
  letter's "eight giants") and crossing the ~20% threshold that brings **equity-method accounting**.
  Berkshire also held warrants to buy more. Buffett praised CEO Vicki Hollub's stewardship — the start of
  a stake that would grow toward ~28% in following years (while Buffett stressed Berkshire would *not* seek
  control).
- [[2023]] **27.8% — but no control sought.** Berkshire increased its Occidental common stake to **27.8%**
  (plus warrants giving a >5-year option to buy more at a fixed price), naming it one of two holdings
  (with the Japanese trading houses) "we expect to maintain indefinitely" — yet "**Berkshire has no
  interest in purchasing or managing Occidental**." Buffett praises CEO **Vicki Hollub** ("she does know
  how to separate oil from rock") and devotes an essay to U.S. **energy independence** (production 8M BOEPD
  in 1975 → 5M by 2007 → **>13M now** after shale) and Occidental's leadership in **carbon capture** —
  "very much in our country's interest."
