# Helzberg's Diamond Shops

Helzberg's Diamond Shops is a Kansas City–based jewelry chain Berkshire acquired in 1995
via a **tax-free exchange of stock** — the famous "ABWA" (acquisitions by walking around)
deal ([[1995]]). In May 1994, a week after the Annual Meeting, **[[Barnett Helzberg]] Jr.**
(an owner of four Berkshire shares) stopped Buffett on a New York street corner (58th and
Fifth) and mentioned he had a business that might interest him. Founded by Barnett's
grandfather in 1915 from a single store, Helzberg's had grown to **134 stores in 23 states**,
with sales rising from $10M (1974) to $53M (1984) to **$282M (1994)** — "we weren't talking
lemonade stands."

The deal hinged on management: Barnett (then 60) had brought in CEO **[[Jeff Comment]]**
(formerly president of Wanamaker's) in 1988, "a homerun." Buffett: "We would not have bought
the business if Jeff had not been there to run it. Buying a retailer without good management
is like buying the Eiffel Tower without an elevator." The chain's edge is **per-store
productivity** — average sales of ~$2M, far above similarly-sized competitors — and Buffett
saw room to grow it "to several times its present size." Helzberg's operates **independently
of [[Borsheim's]]**, Berkshire's Omaha jewelry business, which is a different kind of
operation. (Barnett, "under no obligation," shared a meaningful part of his proceeds with his
associates — "when someone behaves that generously, you know you are going to be treated
right as a buyer.")

## Evolution
- [1995] Acquired via tax-free stock exchange (the ABWA street-corner introduction); 134
  stores, $282M sales; run by CEO Jeff Comment; ~$2M average store sales. First-year jewelry
  earnings (from 4/30/95) $33.9M pre-tax (with Borsheim's).
- [1996] **Berkshire's "only disappointment" of the year** in operating results: while
  [[Borsheim's]] did fine, Helzberg's "suffered a material decline in earnings." Its expense
  base had been geared to a sizable same-store-sales increase (as in prior years); when sales
  came in flat, margins fell. CEO [[Jeff Comment]] "is addressing the expense problem in a
  decisive manner," and earnings "should improve in 1997." (Combined jewelry pre-tax earnings
  $27.8M, down from $33.9M.)
- [1997] **Recovered.** "Profits improved at Helzberg's after a disappointing 1996" — CEO [[Jeff Comment]] "took decisive steps early in 1997 that enabled the company to gain real momentum by the
  crucial Christmas season," with sales still strong into early 1998. (Jewelry segment pre-tax rose
  to $31.6M.)
- [1999] **The turnaround completes.** Recalling that "a few years back, Helzberg's, our 200-store
  jewelry operation, needed to make operating adjustments to restore margins," Buffett reports that
  "under [[Jeff Comment]]'s leadership, the job was done and **profits have dramatically rebounded**"
  — and offers it as the template he expects the Shoe Group to follow. (Jewelry segment pre-tax
  $51M.)
