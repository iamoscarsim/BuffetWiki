# General Re Securities

The **derivatives dealer** that came with the 1998 **[[General Re]]** acquisition — a business
Buffett "didn't want, judging it to be dangerous," tried and failed to sell, and from 2002 began
**winding down**. Its troubles are the concrete grounding of the [[2002]] **[[Derivatives]]**
essay ("financial weapons of mass destruction").

Exiting proved slow and costly: at year-end 2002, after ten months of run-off, it still had
**14,384 contracts** outstanding with **672 counterparties** and **$6.5 billion of receivables**,
and it lost **$173M pre-tax** that year (partly "a belated acknowledgment of faulty, albeit
standard, accounting" used earlier). Buffett's refrain — "**Like Hell, [derivatives] are easy to
enter and almost impossible to exit**" — applied literally: the runoff produced cumulative
**pre-tax losses of roughly $400M+** over 2002–2005 before the book was substantially closed. The
episode is Berkshire's case study in why it wants to be a "fortress of [[Financial Strength]]" and
minimize counterparty links.

## Evolution
- [2002] Identified as the unwanted derivatives unit from Gen Re; wind-down begun; −$173M pre-tax;
  14,384 contracts / 672 counterparties / $6.5B receivables.
- [2003] Runoff continues at further cost; the contract count and receivables fall steadily — but
  slowly, illustrating the [[Derivatives]] "impossible to exit" warning.
- [2004] Down to **2,890 contracts** (from the 23,218 peak); −$44M for the year. "Like Hell,
  derivative trading is easy to enter but difficult to leave."
- [2005] **−$104M** loss reducing the book to **741 contracts** — cumulative exit losses now
  **−$404M**. One liquidated contract had a **100-year term** ("what 'need' could [it] fulfill except…
  a compensation-conscious trader['s]"). Buffett's blunt self-rebuke ("**Fault me for dithering** —
  Charlie calls it thumb-sucking") and the **"canary in the coal mine"** warning: Berkshire was a
  *favorable* case (minor player, benign market, no pressure), yet still bled — "imagine… firms with
  positions many multiples of ours attempting to liquidate in chaotic markets." (See [[Derivatives]],
  [[Mistakes Ledger]].)
- [2006] **The last word.** Down to **197 contracts**; cumulative pre-tax loss **−$409M** (only $5M
  in 2006). "Charlie says that if we had properly classified the $409 million on our 2001 balance
  sheet, it would have been labeled '**Good Until Reached For**.'" Buffett's epitaph for the
  business: "**All's well that ends.**"
