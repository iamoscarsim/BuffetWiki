# Illinois National Bank

The Illinois National Bank and Trust Company of Rockford was Berkshire's banking
subsidiary, purchased in **1969**. Founded in 1931 by [[Gene Abegg]] with
$250,000, it earned just $8,782 in its first full year; by [[1977]] it earned
**$3.6 million** — "more than achieved by many banks two or three times its size"
— at roughly three times the return on assets of most large banks, while paying
savers maximum rates and holding a low-risk, highly liquid asset position. No new
capital had been contributed since purchase; on the contrary, it had paid $20
million in dividends to Berkshire since 1969.

The bank is an early example of Buffett's preference for businesses that earn high
returns on capital and need little of it. Berkshire was later forced to divest the
bank under the Bank Holding Company Act, which barred bank ownership by
non-banking holding companies — an exit driven by regulation rather than by the
quality of the operation.

## Evolution
- [1977] Bought 1969. Earns ~3x peers' return on assets; $3.6M earnings; $20M
  dividends paid to Berkshire since purchase. Run by founder [[Gene Abegg]], 80,
  with Peter Jeffrey brought in as successor president.
- [1978] Earned ~2.1% of average assets (~3x major banks) at lower asset risk.
  Since 1968 consumer time deposits quadrupled, net income tripled. **Required to
  divest the bank by December 31, 1980** (Bank Holding Company Act); most likely a
  spin-off to Berkshire shareholders in the second half of 1980.
- [1979] Last year reported as a subsidiary; record **2.3% on average assets**
  (>3x average bank). Fed rules barring shared officers/directors push Berkshire
  toward *selling* 80–100% rather than spinning off; Buffett warns the proceeds
  can't replace the bank's earning power ("you simply can't buy high quality
  businesses at the sort of price/earnings multiple likely to prevail on our
  bank sale").
- [1980] **Disposition completed Dec 31, 1980.** Rather than a plain spin-off,
  Berkshire offered shareholders an exchange of Berkshire shares for shares of
  Rockford Bancorp (owner of 97.7% of the bank); ~63 of ~1,300 holders participated,
  and Buffett took the residual as "exchanger of last resort." Bank ends up owned by
  65 shareholders who each chose it affirmatively.
- [1985] **Sold** (Rockford liquidation completed). Per-share proceeds roughly
  matched Berkshire's per-share intrinsic value at the time — vindicating Buffett's
  "you cut the cake, you get first choice" fairness in the 1980 exchange.
