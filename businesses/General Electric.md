# General Electric

The U.S. industrial-and-finance conglomerate, and one of the three crisis-era fixed-income
commitments Berkshire made in 2008 "on terms that would be unavailable in normal markets" (alongside
[[Goldman Sachs]] and [[Wrigley]]). In October 2008 Berkshire bought **$3 billion of GE perpetual
preferred** paying a **10% dividend** (callable at a 10% premium), plus **warrants** to buy $3
billion of common — the same high-yield-plus-equity-kicker structure as the Goldman deal ([[2008]]).

These deals exemplified Berkshire as the **"buyer of choice"** in a credit market that had "turned
non-functional": only a balance sheet of Berkshire's strength could write such checks while every
leveraged competitor was retrenching (see [[Financial Strength]], [[Margin of Safety]]). The three
2008 commitments totaled **$14.5 billion**.
