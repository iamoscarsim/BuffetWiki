# Duracell

The battery maker, acquired from **[[Procter & Gamble]]** in a **tax-efficient stock exchange** —
Berkshire handed back its appreciated P&G shares in return for Duracell (recapitalized with cash),
agreed in **2014** and closed in **2015** ([[2014]]). It was the largest of the year's 31 bolt-ons and
was placed under **[[Marmon Group]]**'s jurisdiction. The structure exemplified Buffett's preference
for swapping low-basis marketable stock for an *operating business* (the same logic as the Phillips 66
and Graham Holdings exchanges), avoiding the capital-gains tax a cash sale of the P&G stake would have
triggered.

## Evolution
- [2014] Agreed (via a P&G stock-for-business exchange); to close in 2H 2015 and sit under Marmon.
- [2015] Acquisition completed — a wholly-owned Berkshire consumer-products business.
- [[2016]] **First (partial) year under Berkshire** — and a heavy one. Buffett notes Berkshire "spent
  significant sums getting Duracell in shape for the decades ahead" (transitional costs that "will not
  recur"); both Duracell and **[[Precision Castparts]]** will add a **first full year of earnings in
  2017**, helping the Manufacturing, Service & Retailing group's earnings grow.
