# H.J. Heinz

The global packaged-foods company (ketchup, condiments), and the deal that launched Berkshire's
recurring partnership with **[[Jorge Paulo Lemann]]**'s Brazilian **3G Capital**. Announced in
February 2013 (reported in the [[2012]] letter), Berkshire and 3G each bought **50% of a holding
company owning all of Heinz** ([[2012]]). Each side contributed about **$4 billion of common
equity**; Berkshire *additionally* invested **$8 billion in 9% preferred** carrying two
value-boosters — redemption at a significant premium, and **warrants** to buy 5% of the common for a
nominal sum — a ~**$12 billion** total commitment.

The structure became Berkshire's template: 3G supplies aggressive operational management (zero-based
budgeting, cost discipline) while Berkshire supplies capital and a permanent home. Buffett liked the
arrangement precisely because it paired a great consumer brand with managers "both talented and
shareholder-oriented" — though 3G's cost-cutting style differs markedly from Berkshire's hands-off
norm.

## Evolution
- [2012] Deal announced (Feb 2013): Berkshire + 3G each take 50%; Berkshire puts in ~$4B common + **$8B
  of 9% preferred** + warrants (~$12B total). Lemann a "long-time friend… extraordinary manager."
- [2013] Deal closed (June); Heinz run by 3G's Bernardo Hees; the partnership a success.
- [2015] **Merged with Kraft to form [[Kraft Heinz]]**; Berkshire and 3G become controlling holders
  of the combined food giant (see [[Kraft Heinz]]).
