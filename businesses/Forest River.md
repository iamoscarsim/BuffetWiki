# Forest River

A leading **recreational-vehicle manufacturer** ($1.6 billion of sales; 60 plants, 5,400 employees,
also expanding into boats), acquired by Berkshire on August 31, 2005 ([[2005]]). The deal is a
classic of Berkshire's frictionless sourcing: on June 21 Buffett received a **two-page fax** from
owner-manager **[[Pete Liegl]]** explaining "point by point" why Forest River met the acquisition
criteria; Buffett — who "had not before heard of the company" — asked for figures, got them the next
morning, made an offer that afternoon, and shook hands on June 28.

Liegl's history shaped the deal's terms: years earlier he had sold a smaller version of the business
to an **LBO operator** who "promptly began telling him how to run the place"; Liegl left, the
business went bankrupt, and he repurchased it. "You can be sure that I won't be telling Pete how to
manage his operation." Liegl later quipped (in a WSJ profile) that "**it was easier to sell my
business than to renew my driver's license**."

## Evolution
- [2005] Acquired (8/31) after a two-page fax and a one-week handshake; RV leader run by
  entrepreneur Pete Liegl, who keeps full operating autonomy.
- [[2024]] **19 years of "shooting the lights out."** On **[[Pete Liegl]]**'s death (November 2024, at 80,
  "still working"), Buffett recounts that "**no competitor came close to his performance**" over his 19
  years under Berkshire — and the legendary terms (Liegl set his own $100,000 salary plus an incremental-
  earnings bonus). Forest River is held up as proof that "a single winning decision can make a breathtaking
  difference over time."
