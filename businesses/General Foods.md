# General Foods

General Foods was a major Berkshire equity holding from 1979 through its 1985 sale —
one of the four big non-controlled positions whose [[Look-Through Earnings]] Buffett
repeatedly highlighted (alongside [[GEICO]], [[Washington Post]], and R.J. Reynolds).
Berkshire bought it in 1980 "at a price far below... its per-share business value."
Over the following years management (Jim Ferguson and Phil Smith) grew the company's
value, and General Foods made repeated bargain-priced buybacks, including the
**proportional redemptions** Berkshire participated in (taxed favorably as
dividends).

In late 1985 **Philip Morris** offered to buy General Foods at a price reflecting its
grown value, and Berkshire sold for a pre-tax gain of about **$338M** — the largest
component of that year's realized gains. Buffett used it to summarize his approach:
the four favorable factors were "a bargain purchase price, a business with fine
underlying economics, an able management concentrating on the interests of
shareholders, and a buyer willing to pay full business value" — but "we devote our
attention to attractive purchases, not to the possibility of attractive sales."

## Evolution
- [1979–84] Built into a top-four holding; bargain purchase (1980); buybacks and
  proportional redemptions.
- [1985] **Sold to Philip Morris** for a ~$338M pre-tax gain.
