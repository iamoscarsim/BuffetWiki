# Bank of America

The U.S. money-center bank. In **2011**, amid lingering crisis doubts about the bank, Berkshire bought
a **$5 billion 6% preferred stock** that came with **warrants to buy 700 million common shares at
$7.14** any time before **September 2, 2021** — a classic Buffett crisis-style deal pairing a solid
current yield with large equity upside ([[2011]]). The idea reportedly came to Buffett in the bath;
the structure echoes the 2008 [[Goldman Sachs]] and [[General Electric]] preferreds.

Buffett credited CEO **Brian Moynihan** with "excellent progress" cleaning up "huge mistakes… made by
prior management" while "nurturing a huge and attractive underlying business that will endure long
after today's problems are forgotten." The warrants — deeply out of the money at purchase — "will
likely be of great value before they expire," and Berkshire would later exercise them (2017),
converting into a top-tier common-stock position and one of its most profitable crisis-era bets.

## Evolution
- [2011] $5B of 6% preferred + warrants for 700M shares at $7.14 (exp. Sept 2021); a vote of
  confidence in Brian Moynihan's turnaround. The warrants were later exercised at a large gain.
- [[2016]] **The warrant deep in the money.** The $5B preferred still paid **$300M/year**, and the
  warrant (700M common shares for $5B, exercisable before **Sept 2, 2021**) was now worth a **$10.5B
  profit** at year-end. Buffett spells out the trigger: if BofA's common dividend (then 30¢) rises above
  **44¢** before 2021, Berkshire would make a **cashless exchange** of the preferred into common;
  otherwise it would likely exercise just before expiry. He also praises BofA's own aggressive **share
  repurchases** of underpriced stock — "when a company grows and outstanding shares shrink, good things
  happen for shareholders." *(Berkshire exercised in 2017, becoming one of BofA's largest holders.)*
- [[2017]] **Warrant exercised → a top-tier common-stock holding.** Berkshire exercised the 700M-share
  warrant (BofA having raised its common dividend above the 44¢ trigger), converting the preferred into
  **700,000,000 common shares (6.8% of BofA)** carried at **cost $5,007M / market $20,664M** — a ~$15B
  paper gain and now one of Berkshire's largest positions. The 2011 crisis preferred had done exactly what
  Buffett predicted: "the warrants… will likely be of great value before they expire."
- [[2018]] **Added to — a top-three holding.** Berkshire grew the position to **918,919,000 shares (9.5%
  of BofA), cost $11,650M / market $22,642M**, buying more common in the open market during 2018's ~$43B
  equity-buying year. One of the five largest holdings in the retained-earnings table: Berkshire's share
  of 2018 was **$551M of dividends** vs. **$2,096M of retained earnings**.
- [[2019]] **The #2 holding** (947.8M shares, 10.7%, cost $12,560M / market **$33,380M**) — second only to
  Apple. In the retained-earnings table, Berkshire's 2019 share was **$682M dividends vs. $2,167M
  retained**; BofA's continued buybacks keep lifting Berkshire's ownership.
- [[2020]] **Added to even as the rest of the bank book was sold.** Amid a year in which Berkshire
  **exited Wells Fargo, JPMorgan and Goldman Sachs**, BofA was the conspicuous exception — Berkshire
  *bought more*, lifting the stake to **1,032.9M shares (11.9%), cost $14,631M / market $31,306M**. It
  remained the #2 equity holding behind Apple, a sign of Buffett's continued confidence in Brian
  Moynihan's bank where he had soured on the others.
- [[2021]] **Approaching a $46B holding.** Berkshire's stake (1,032.9M shares, unchanged) rose to **12.8%
  of BofA** as the bank repurchased stock; market value **$45,952M** (cost $14,631M) — the #2 holding
  behind Apple. Brian Moynihan's bank remained Buffett's favored money-center name after the 2020 exits
  from Wells Fargo, JPMorgan and Goldman.
- [[2024]] **Trimmed.** Alongside the big Apple sale, Berkshire **reduced its Bank of America** stake
  during 2024 (selling steadily once the position again exceeded ~10% reporting thresholds), part of the
  marketable-equity drop from $354B to $272B. BofA remained one of the "dozen or so" large household-name
  holdings, but no longer the near-untouchable position of the prior decade.
