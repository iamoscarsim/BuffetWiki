# Justin Industries

Justin Industries, acquired by Berkshire in **July 2000 for ~$570 million in cash**, is two
businesses in one: the **leading maker of Western boots** (the **Justin, Tony Lama, Nocona,
and Chippewa** brands) and, through **Acme Brick**, "the premier producer of brick in Texas
and five neighboring states." The deal arrived via a fax from a stranger, **Mark Jones**,
who proposed Berkshire join a group to buy an unnamed company; Buffett declined to invest
*with* others but offered a commission, learned the target was Justin, and met chairman
John Roach and **John Justin** (who built the business and was its major shareholder).
Justin had been forced to retire for health reasons and **died in late February 2001** —
"a class act … as a citizen, businessman and human being" — having groomed two autonomous
managers: **Harrold Melton** at Acme and **Randy Watson** at Justin Boot.

**Acme** is the larger unit: **>1 billion bricks a year from 22 plants (~11.7% of national
output)**, with regional dominance so complete that **75% of Texans name Acme** when asked
for a brand of brick (vs. 16% for the runner-up) — brand equity built on product quality
and "many decades of extraordinary community service." Buffett relishes the optics:
Berkshire, "whose top management has long been mired in the 19th century, is now one of the
very few authentic **'clicks-and-bricks'** businesses around" (GEICO online + Acme). "You
can bet this move … is making them sweat in Silicon Valley."
