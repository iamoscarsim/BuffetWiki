# AIG retroactive reinsurance

The **largest reinsurance policy in history**, written in **January 2017**: through **[[National Indemnity Company|National Indemnity]]**, Berkshire agreed to reinsure **up to $20 billion** of long-tail commercial
losses already incurred by **AIG**, in exchange for a single **$10.2 billion premium** — "a world's
record and one we won't come close to repeating" ([[2017]]). Foreshadowed in the [[2016]] letter as the
"huge policy" that pushed Berkshire's [[Insurance Float|float]] **above $100 billion**, it is the archetype
of the **retroactive reinsurance** Berkshire alone can write: only its "unparalleled financial strength"
lets other insurers transfer enormous, decades-long legacy liabilities to a counterparty certain to be
able to pay (see [[Loss Reserving]], [[Ajit Jain]]).

The economics follow the familiar [[Insurance Float|retroactive-reinsurance]] pattern: a large up-front
premium produces a big slug of float Berkshire invests for years (even decades) as the assumed losses pay
out slowly, against a **"deferred charge"** asset that amortizes into underwriting losses over the long
life of the claims. The boost to 2017 premium volume was one-time, so Buffett warned premium volume would
fall in 2018.

## Evolution
- [[2016]] Flagged as the post-year-end "huge policy" that lifted float over **$100 billion**.
- [[2017]] Booked: **up to $20B of AIG long-tail losses** reinsured for a record **$10.2B premium** — the
  largest such policy ever; premium volume reached $60.6B and float **$114.5B**.
