# Apple

The maker of the iPhone, Mac, and an enormous services ecosystem — and, beginning in [[2016]],
Berkshire's first major investment in a **technology hardware company**. The position first appeared in
the [[2016]] portfolio at **61,242,652 shares (1.1% of Apple), cost $6,747M / market $7,093M**, and would
be built rapidly thereafter into Berkshire's largest single equity holding. Its arrival marks the final
softening of the [[Circle of Competence|technology aversion]] that had kept Berkshire out of tech for
decades — Buffett came to view Apple not as a gadget company but as a **consumer-products franchise** with
an [[Economic Moat|ecosystem moat]] and ferociously loyal customers, the kind of "[[The Inevitables|toll bridge]]" dominance he had long prized in [[Coca-Cola]].

The initial stake was sourced by one of the new investment lieutenants (**[[Todd Combs]]** or **[[Ted Weschler]]**, who by 2016 each independently managed **>$10 billion** and whose decisions Buffett "usually
learns about… by looking at monthly trade sheets"); Buffett soon embraced the idea and made Apple his own
signature post-2016 purchase. It follows the cautious, hold-it-as-a-stock tech precedent set by **[[IBM]]**
([[2011]]) — but where IBM disappointed, Apple would not.

## Evolution
- [[2016]] First disclosed: **1.1% of Apple**, cost $6.7B. Listed among the fifteen largest common-stock
  holdings; an early, still-modest position relative to what it became. Buffett's clearest break yet from
  "[[Circle of Competence|why we own no tech]]," treating Apple as a consumer franchise rather than a
  hardware bet. Compare the wary handling of [[IBM]] five years earlier.
- [[2017]] **Built up roughly fourfold into a major holding.** Berkshire ended 2017 with **166,713,209
  shares (3.3% of Apple), cost $20,961M / market $28,213M** — already one of its three largest equity
  positions. Buffett (with Combs/Weschler having opened the door) committed to Apple at scale, treating it
  as the consumer-products franchise its ecosystem and customer loyalty make it. *(IBM, by contrast, was
  being wound down — Apple decisively replaced it as Berkshire's marquee technology investment.)*
- [[2018]] **Berkshire's largest holding.** The stake reached **255,300,329 shares (5.4% of Apple), cost
  $36,044M / market $40,271M** — the single biggest position in the equity grove. In the retained-earnings
  table of the five largest holdings, Berkshire's share of Apple's 2018 results was **$745M of dividends**
  against **$2,502M of retained earnings** — Buffett "rejoic[ing]" that Apple uses much of those retained
  earnings to **repurchase its own (underpriced) shares**, steadily lifting Berkshire's ownership without
  Berkshire spending a dime. The clearest sign yet that Apple had become a core, franchise-grade holding.
- [[2019]] **By far the largest holding — $73.7B.** As the stock surged, the stake (250.9M shares, 5.7%,
  cost $35,287M) reached a market value of **$73,667M** — nearly double 2018's $40B and well over twice
  the next holding ([[Bank of America]]). In the retained-earnings table, Berkshire's 2019 share of Apple:
  **$773M dividends vs. $2,519M retained**. Apple alone now accounted for roughly **30% of Berkshire's
  $248B equity portfolio**.
- [[2020]] **A "Family Jewel" — and the textbook buyback illustration.** Apple is named one of Berkshire's
  **"Big Four"** (a "toss-up" with [[Burlington Northern Santa Fe|BNSF]] for 2nd/3rd most valuable). Buffett
  recaps the full position: bought late-2016 to mid-2018 for a **5.2% stake costing $36B**; ~$775M/yr of
  dividends; in **2020 he sold a small slice for an $11B gain** — yet, thanks to **Apple's own buybacks**,
  Berkshire **still owned 5.4%** (a "costless" increase). Year-end value **$120.4 billion** (cost now
  $31.1B after the trim). Combined with Berkshire's own repurchases, "you now indirectly own a full **10%
  more of Apple**… than you did in July 2018" — "the math of repurchases grinds away slowly, but can be
  powerful." (Mae West: "Too much of a good thing can be… wonderful.") See [[Evolving Views — Buybacks]].
- [[2021]] **The "runner-up Giant" — $161B.** Apple is named the second of Berkshire's **Four Giants**;
  ownership ticked up to **5.55%** (from 5.39%) "**with no Berkshire funds**" spent — Apple's own buybacks
  did it (each 0.1% of Apple ≈ $100M of earnings). GAAP counted only the **$785M of dividends**, but
  Berkshire's "share" of Apple's earnings was a "**staggering $5.6 billion**." Year-end market value
  **$161.2 billion** (cost still $31.1B). Buffett praises CEO **Tim Cook**, "Apple's brilliant CEO."
- [[2024]] **Sharply reduced.** Berkshire's marketable-equity portfolio fell from **$354B to $272B** over
  2024, driven chiefly by large **sales of Apple** (the position was cut by roughly two-thirds during the
  year). Buffett didn't single out Apple by name in the letter, but framed the trims as ordinary portfolio
  management and stressed that "the great majority of your money remains in equities" — Apple stayed among
  the "dozen or so very large and highly profitable businesses with household names" Berkshire owns. *(The
  sale lifted Berkshire's cash/T-bill pile to a record and prompted much commentary about valuation.)*
