# Kraft Foods

The packaged-foods giant, a Berkshire equity position established in [[2007]] (124.4M shares, ~8.1%,
cost $4,152M / market $4,059M) — a large branded-foods franchise. The holding became notable for a
public clash: in **2010** Buffett **criticized Kraft's acquisition of Cadbury** (paid for partly with
"undervalued" Kraft stock) and its sale of the frozen-pizza business as **"a dumb deal,"** voting
against the stock issuance — a rare instance of Berkshire publicly opposing the management of one of
its holdings. Berkshire later received **Mondelez/Kraft** shares in the 2012 split and ultimately
became a major owner of **Kraft Heinz** via the 2013 Heinz deal and 2015 merger (see [[Mistakes Ledger]] for the later Kraft Heinz writedown).

## Evolution
- [2007] New position (~8.1%, ~$4B) — a branded-packaged-foods holding.
- [2009] Buffett publicly opposes Kraft's **Cadbury** acquisition as "a dumb deal" — issuing
  undervalued stock and selling the pizza business cheaply.
- [2011] Position held as Kraft prepares to split into Mondelez and the grocery business; sets up
  Berkshire's later, far larger Kraft Heinz involvement.
- [2015] **Merged with [[H.J. Heinz]] to form [[Kraft Heinz]]** — the Berkshire/[[Jorge Paulo Lemann|3G]]
  partnership "more than doubled its size." Berkshire emerged with ~27% of the combined company (cost
  $9.8B) plus $8B of preferred; ~$27B of sales (see [[Kraft Heinz]]). Buffett's earlier "dumb deal"
  Cadbury-era skepticism of Kraft's management gives way to the 3G operating model running the merged
  brands.
