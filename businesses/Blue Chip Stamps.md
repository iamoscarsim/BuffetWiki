# Blue Chip Stamps

Blue Chip Stamps ran a trading-stamp business (retailer loyalty stamps redeemed
for merchandise) whose real importance to Berkshire was as a holding vehicle. Its
float — money held between selling stamps and redeeming them — funded the
acquisition of excellent businesses, most famously [[See's Candies]] (99% owned)
and [[Wesco Financial]] (80% owned, run by Louis Vincenti).

In the [[1977]] letter Berkshire owned approximately **36.5%** of Blue Chip and
was still increasing its stake; Buffett's proportional share of Blue Chip's
earnings flowed into Berkshire's reported operating earnings. Blue Chip earned
~$12.9 million from operations in 1977 plus $4.1 million of realized securities
gains. Over the following years Berkshire absorbed Blue Chip entirely, and its
key subsidiaries (See's, Wesco) became direct Berkshire holdings.

## Evolution
- [1977] Berkshire owned ~36.5% and increasing. Holds See's (99%) and Wesco (80%).
  Acts as an early float-and-acquisition vehicle parallel to the insurance group.
- [1978] After the [[Diversified Retailing Company]] merger, Berkshire's stake rose
  to ~58%, triggering full consolidation of Blue Chip's businesses (See's, Wesco,
  Buffalo Evening News, Mutual Savings) into Berkshire's statements.
- [1983] **Fully merged into Berkshire** (mid-year; <0.1% of shares dissented).
  Berkshire's net Wesco interest rose to 80%. The merger used purchase accounting,
  creating the See's/Buffalo News Goodwill discussed in the [[Economic Goodwill]]
  appendix; net dilution to original 1965 owners was negligible.
