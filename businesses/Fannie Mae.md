# Fannie Mae

The Federal National Mortgage Association ("Fannie Mae") appears in the letters not
as a holding but as the subject of one of Buffett's most painful confessed **errors
of omission** ([[1991]]). The episode is the "Mistake Du Jour" of the 1991 letter and
sits in the [[Mistakes Ledger]].

In early 1988 Buffett decided to buy 30 million shares (split-adjusted) of Fannie Mae
— a $350–400M position. Berkshire had owned the stock years earlier and understood
the business; Buffett judged that CEO David Maxwell had "dealt superbly" with inherited
problems and built "a financial powerhouse — with the best yet to come," and even
visited Maxwell to confirm he'd be comfortable with a large Berkshire position. After
buying about 7 million shares, the price climbed; "in frustration, I stopped buying"
— and then, "in an even sillier move," sold the 7 million shares already owned out of
"distaste for holding small positions." Buffett's estimate of the gain Berkshire
*didn't* make because of this thumb-sucking, as of year-end 1991: **about $1.4
billion**. "I wish I could give you a halfway rational explanation… But there isn't
one." He notes he avoided repeating the stop-buying error during the [[Coca-Cola]]
purchase program.

## Evolution
- [1991] Disclosed as a ~$1.4B error of omission: bought ~7M of an intended 30M
  shares in 1988, then sold; David Maxwell admired as CEO.
- [2000] Fannie Mae briefly returns — and exits again. The letter notes Berkshire "**sold nearly all
  of our Freddie Mac and Fannie Mae shares**" in 2000, implying it had rebuilt a GSE position in the
  interim. The exit (with [[Freddie Mac]]) preceded the two agencies' 2008 conservatorship by eight
  years — this time a *timely* sale rather than a thumb-suck.
