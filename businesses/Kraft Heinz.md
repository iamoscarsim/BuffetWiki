# Kraft Heinz

The packaged-foods giant formed in **2015** by **merging [[H.J. Heinz]] with [[Kraft Foods|Kraft]]**,
controlled jointly by Berkshire and **[[Jorge Paulo Lemann|3G Capital]]** ([[2015]]). The deal "more
than doubled" the Heinz partnership: Berkshire, which had owned ~53% of Heinz (cost $4.25B), emerged
with **325.4 million shares (~27%) of Kraft Heinz** (cost **$9.8 billion**) — a company with ~**$27
billion** of annual sales (Heinz ketchup/mustard + Oscar Mayer + Kraft cheeses). GAAP required a
**$6.8 billion write-up** on the merger, leaving the stake carried far above cost but below market
("an outcome only an accountant could love"). Berkshire also held **$8 billion of Kraft Heinz
preferred** ($720M/yr), redeemed for **$8.32 billion in June 2016**.

The position embodies the **3G model** (zero-based budgeting, aggressive cost-cutting to lift
productivity) — distinct from Berkshire's hands-off [[Decentralization|decentralization]], but a
partnership Buffett prized: "Jorge Paulo and his associates could not be better partners." The stake
later disappointed as Kraft Heinz's brands struggled and it took a large 2019 writedown — a rare
soft spot among Berkshire's big holdings.

## Evolution
- [2015] Heinz merges with Kraft → Kraft Heinz; Berkshire holds ~27% ($9.8B cost) + $8B preferred; a
  $6.8B GAAP write-up; the 3G cost-cutting model contrasted with Berkshire's hands-off style.
- [[2016]] **Equity-method holding; the $8B preferred redeemed.** Because Berkshire is part of the
  control group, the **325,442,152 shares** are carried at the **equity-method GAAP figure of $15.3B**
  (cost $9.8B; **year-end market $28.4B**) and excluded from the marketable-securities table. The **$8B
  preferred was redeemed in June 2016**. Buffett (a Kraft Heinz director) uses the company as his
  exemplar of *honest* restructuring disclosure: when "a total rebasing of a business" occurs (as in the
  Kraft–Heinz merger), the "huge one-time costs of rationalizing the combined operations" *should* be
  explained to owners for several years — which "is precisely what the CEO of Kraft Heinz has done" — the
  opposite of managements that perennially tell owners to "don't count this" (see [[Owner Orientation and Candor]]). On dividends/tax: Berkshire's parent owes only ~7¢ per $1 of dividends from this >20%-owned,
  parent-held stake.
- [[2017]] Still equity-method (the 325.4M shares carried at a **GAAP figure of $17.6B**; market $25.3B;
  cost $9.8B) and excluded from the marketable-securities table. A quiet year in the letter before the
  brand troubles and the large 2019 writedown.
- [[2018]] **The trouble surfaces.** Kraft Heinz took a **large write-off of intangible assets**;
  Berkshire's **26.7% share contributed a ~$3.0 billion non-cash impairment** to its 2018 GAAP results
  (a major component of why GAAP net income was only $4.0B). The carrying value fell to a **GAAP figure of
  $13.8B** (market $14B; cost $9.8B) — the stake's market value now barely above its cost, an early sign
  of the brand-value erosion that the [[2019]] letter would confront more squarely.
- [[2019]] **Market value falls below cost.** Carried at the equity-method **GAAP figure of $13.8B**
  (Berkshire's share of Kraft Heinz's audited net worth), but the **market value of the shares had fallen
  to just $10.5B** — now *below* the $9.8B cost basis and far below the carrying value. Kraft Heinz's
  packaged-brand struggles (and an SEC inquiry/restatement) had turned a once-celebrated 3G partnership
  into a conspicuous laggard among Berkshire's large holdings.
- [[2020]] Still equity-method and still underwater: carried at a **GAAP figure of $13.3B** (Berkshire's
  share of audited net worth), but **market value only $11.3B**. Excluded from the equity table; a quiet,
  unresolved disappointment among the otherwise-celebrated holdings.
- [[2021]] Still equity-method at **26.6%**, carried at **$13.1 billion** — a quiet, large holding noted
  beside (not within) the marketable-securities table.
