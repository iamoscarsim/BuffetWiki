# ISCAR

The Israeli maker of **small, consumable metal-cutting tools** (used with large machine tools),
acquired by Berkshire in 2006 ([[2006]]) — **Berkshire's first acquisition of a business based
outside the United States**, and a milestone in Buffett's long-stated wish to "extend Berkshire's
appeal beyond U.S. borders."

The deal began with a **1¼-page letter** (October 25, 2005) from chairman **[[Eitan Wertheimer]]**,
of whom Buffett "then knew nothing": "I am writing to introduce you to ISCAR… Our conclusion is that
Berkshire Hathaway would be the ideal home for ISCAR." Its "quality… and the character of its
management leap[t] off the page." After meetings in Omaha and a Buffett/Munger visit to Israel ("we
have never been more impressed with any operation"), Berkshire bought **80% for $4 billion** (the
Wertheimer family keeping **20%** as "our valued partner"); CEO **Jacob Harpaz** and CFO Danny
Goldman stayed on.

Buffett's framing of the economics: "It's a business **without magic** except for that imparted by
the people who run it" — ISCAR's engineers constantly develop tools that make customers' expensive
machines more productive, so "**ISCAR makes money because it enables its customers to make more
money**." Berkshire later bought the **remaining 20% in 2013** (~$2.05B, valuing ISCAR/IMC at ~$10B).

## Evolution
- [2006] 80% acquired for $4B — Berkshire's first non-U.S. acquisition; sourced by Eitan Wertheimer's
  letter; cutting tools in 61 countries; family retains 20%.
- [2013] Berkshire buys the **remaining 20%** of the IMC/ISCAR group (~$2.05B, implying ~$10B value)
  — full ownership of a business that had performed superbly since 2006.
