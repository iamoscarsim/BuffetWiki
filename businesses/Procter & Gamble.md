# Procter & Gamble

The consumer-products giant, which entered Berkshire's portfolio in 2005 ([[2005]]) when **[[Gillette]]
merged into P&G** — converting Berkshire's long-held 9.7% Gillette stake into **100 million P&G
shares** (market $5,788M). GAAP forced Berkshire to book a **$5.0 billion pre-tax capital gain** on
the merger, which Buffett dismissed as "**meaningless** from an economic standpoint… ignore it" — no
tax was incurred and he intended to keep the P&G shares (just as he hadn't meant to sell Gillette).

The position is the continuation of one of Buffett's classic franchise holdings, now inside a larger
branded-goods company. Berkshire later **trimmed and exited** much of the P&G stake over the following
years (partly swapping P&G shares for P&G's **Duracell** battery business in a 2014 cash-rich split-off
— a tax-efficient unwind).

## Evolution
- [2005] Acquired via the Gillette→P&G merger (100M shares; a "meaningless" $5.0B GAAP gain); a
  continuation of the Gillette franchise bet.
- [2007]–[2008] Held among Berkshire's larger consumer-brand positions; later gradually reduced.
