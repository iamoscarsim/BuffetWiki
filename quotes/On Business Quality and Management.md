# On Business Quality and Management

> "One of the lessons your management has learned — and, unfortunately, sometimes
> re-learned — is the importance of being in businesses where tailwinds prevail
> rather than headwinds." — [[1977]]

The tailwinds-vs-headwinds lesson, drawn from contrasting insurance with
[[Berkshire Hathaway Textiles]].

> "It is comforting to be in a business where some mistakes can be made and yet a
> quite satisfactory overall performance can be achieved." — [[1977]]

On the forgiving economics of a good business (insurance) versus textiles, "where
even very good management probably can average only modest results."

> "We can obtain a better management result through non-control than control."
> — [[1977]]

Buffett's unorthodox case for passive minority ownership of superbly managed
companies like [[Capital Cities]].

> "If each of us hires people who are smaller than we are, we shall become a company
> of dwarfs. But, if each of us hires people who are bigger than we are, we shall
> become a company of giants." — David Ogilvy, quoted in [[1986]]

Buffett's hiring creed; see [[Decentralization]].

> "[Working with people who cause your stomach to churn] seems much like marrying for
> money — probably a bad idea under any circumstances, but absolute madness if you are
> already rich." — [[1986]]

On working only with managers he likes and admires.

> "The difference between GEICO's costs and those of its competitors is a kind of moat
> that protects a valuable and much-sought-after business castle." — [[1986]]

The origin of the [[Economic Moat]] metaphor, on [[GEICO]] and [[Bill Snyder]].

> "Time is the friend of the wonderful business, the enemy of the mediocre." — [[1989]]

The compounding case for quality over cheapness ([[Good Business at a Fair Price]]).

> "In a difficult business, no sooner is one problem solved than another surfaces —
> never is there just one cockroach in the kitchen." — [[1989]]

Why "cigar-butt" bargains disappoint ([[Mistakes Ledger]]).

> "Our most surprising discovery: the overwhelming importance in business of an unseen
> force that we might call 'the institutional imperative.'" — [[1989]]

See [[Institutional Imperative]].

> "We've never succeeded in making a good deal with a bad person." — [[1989]]

On only partnering with people he likes, trusts, and admires.
