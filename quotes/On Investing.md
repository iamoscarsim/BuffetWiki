# On Investing

> "We select our marketable equity securities in much the same way we would
> evaluate a business for acquisition in its entirety." — [[1977]]

The foundation of [[Stocks as Fractional Ownership]].

> "If their business experience continues to satisfy us, we welcome lower market
> prices of stocks we own as an opportunity to acquire even more of a good thing
> at a better price." — [[1977]]

Falling prices on a sound business are an opportunity — a precursor to [[Mr. Market]].

> "Even a totally dormant savings account will produce steadily rising interest
> earnings each year because of compounding." — [[1977]]

Why rising EPS is a vanity metric and [[Return on Equity]] is the real test.

> "We simply attempt to be fearful when others are greedy and to be greedy only when
> others are fearful." — [[1986]]

Buffett's signature temperament, stated amid Wall Street euphoria: the "two super-
contagious diseases, fear and greed, will forever occur," and the goal is to exploit
the mood, not catch it. Relates to [[Mr. Market]].

> "I would rather be vaguely right than precisely wrong." — [[1986]]

Quoting Keynes, on why [[Owner Earnings]] (with its necessarily estimated
maintenance-capex term) beats the "deceptively precise figures provided by GAAP."

> "Though dentists correctly claim that if you ignore your teeth they'll go away, the
> same is not true for [maintenance capital expenditures]." — [[1986]]

The [[Owner Earnings]] attack on the Wall Street "cash flow" fallacy.

> "Mr. Market is there to serve you, not to guide you. It is his pocketbook, not his
> wisdom, that you will find useful." — [[1987]]

The heart of the [[Mr. Market]] allegory.

> "If you've been in the game 30 minutes and you don't know who the patsy is, you're
> the patsy." — [[1987]]

On knowing whether you can value a business better than the market ([[Mr. Market]]).

> "In the short run, the market is a voting machine but in the long run it is a weighing
> machine." — Ben Graham, quoted in [[1987]]

Why business results, not price quotes, eventually decide investment success.

> "We try to buy… an outstanding business at a sensible price, not a mediocre business
> at a bargain price… making silk purses out of silk is the best that we can do; with
> sow's ears, we fail." — [[1987]]

The mature statement of [[Good Business at a Fair Price]].

> "If you want to shoot rare, fast-moving elephants, you should always carry a loaded
> gun." — [[1987]]

On financing ahead of need and keeping firepower ready ([[Financial Strength]]).

> "When we own portions of outstanding businesses with outstanding managements, our
> favorite holding period is forever." — [[1988]]

The most-quoted statement of [[Permanent Ownership]].

> "Too much of a good thing can be wonderful." — Mae West, quoted in [[1988]]

On [[Concentration]] — buying meaningful amounts of the few great businesses.

> "It has been far safer to steal large sums with a pen than small sums with a gun."
> — [[1988]]

On accounting-based fraud and the limits of GAAP ([[Owner Orientation and Candor]]).

> "In any sort of a contest — financial, mental, or physical — it's an enormous
> advantage to have opponents who have been taught that it's useless to even try."
> — [[1988]]

On the gift of [[Efficient Market Theory]] to disciplined investors.

> "[The deferred tax liability] resembles an interest-free loan from the U.S. Treasury
> that comes due only at our election." — [[1989]]

The tax case for buy-and-hold ([[Permanent Ownership]]).

> "We're agnostics, not atheists." — [[1989]]

On industries Berkshire can't forecast (airlines, paper) — hence the convertible
[[Capital Allocation|preferred]] structure for [[USAir]] and [[Champion International]].

> "Only in the summer of 1988 did my brain finally establish contact with my eyes."
> — [[1989]]

On waiting 52 years to buy [[Coca-Cola]] despite knowing the product since boyhood.

> "It's optimism that is the enemy of the rational buyer." — [[1990]]

The net-buyer logic — welcome the low prices that pessimism produces ([[Mr. Market]]).

> "We always have preferred a lumpy 15% return to a smooth 12%." — [[1990]]

On accepting volatility for higher returns ([[Super-Catastrophe Reinsurance]]).

> "Confronted with a challenge to distill the secret of sound investment into three
> words, we venture the motto, Margin of Safety." — Ben Graham, quoted in [[1990]]

The three words Buffett still endorses; see [[Margin of Safety]].

> "In a business selling a commodity-type product, it's impossible to be a lot smarter
> than your dumbest competitor." — [[1990]]

The lesson of the [[USAir]] "unforced error" and commodity economics.
