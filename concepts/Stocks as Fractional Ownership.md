# Stocks as Fractional Ownership

The bedrock of Buffett's method: a share of stock is a fractional ownership
interest in a real business, not a chip to be traded on price moves. In the
[[1977]] letter he states it directly — "We select our marketable equity
securities in much the same way we would evaluate a business for acquisition in
its entirety" — and applies the same four [[Acquisition Criteria]] to both.

Several corollaries follow and recur throughout the letters:

- **Welcome lower prices.** "If their business experience continues to satisfy
  us, we welcome lower market prices of stocks we own as an opportunity to acquire
  even more of a good thing at a better price." Falling quotes on a sound business
  are an opportunity, not a loss — the seed of the later [[Mr. Market]] parable.
- **Ignore short-term price behavior.** "We ordinarily make no attempt to buy
  equities for anticipated favorable stock price behavior in the short term." The
  scorecard is business results over years, not prices on any given day.
- **Non-control can beat control.** With outstanding managers in place (see
  [[Capital Cities]]), Buffett argues "we can obtain a better management result
  through non-control than control."

## Evolution
- [1977] Fully articulated: stocks evaluated as businesses; lower prices welcomed;
  short-term price action ignored; non-control ownership embraced when management
  is excellent.
- [1978] Reinforced via [[SAFECO]]: bought the best P&C insurer below book through
  the market while mediocre companies fetched premiums in negotiated deals.
  Passive ownership of excellent management is no real loss ("the proper policy
  also would be to sit back and let management do its job"). Pairs with
  [[Concentration]] and [[Look-Through Earnings]], both stated this year.
