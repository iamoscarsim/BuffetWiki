# Efficient Market Theory

Buffett's standing rebuttal to **Efficient Market Theory (EMT)** — the academic
doctrine, "almost holy scripture in academic circles during the 1970s," that all public
information is already reflected in stock prices, so analysis is useless and a
dart-thrower will do as well as the best analyst. His objection, stated fully in the
[[1988]] letter, is precise: observers "correctly" noted the market is *frequently*
efficient and then "incorrectly" concluded it is *always* efficient — "the difference
between these propositions is night and day."

His evidence is the **63-year [[Arbitrage|arbitrage]] record** (1926–1988) across
Graham-Newman, Buffett Partnership, and Berkshire — disciplined, publicly-announced,
hindsight-free deals — averaging **over 20% a year** against the market's ~10%. The
compounding makes the point unmistakable: $1,000 at 10% becomes $405,000 over 63 years;
at 20% it becomes **$97 million**. He also cites (in the [[1984]]-era "Superinvestors of
Graham-and-Doddsville" spirit) the cluster of value investors who beat the market for
decades. Yet "no one, to my knowledge, has ever said he was wrong, no matter how many
thousands of students he has sent forth misinstructed."

For Buffett the practical consequence is gleeful: EMT is "an extraordinary service to
us," because "in any sort of a contest… it's an enormous advantage to have opponents
who have been taught that it's useless to even try" — "Grahamites should probably endow
chairs to ensure the perpetual teaching of EMT." But he adds the honest caveat: the
market *is* reasonably efficient much of the time, so simply "committing to a specific
investment category or style" (arbitrage included) guarantees nothing; superior returns
come "only by carefully evaluating facts and continuously exercising discipline." The
page connects to [[Mr. Market]] (the emotional source of inefficiency) and
[[Stocks as Fractional Ownership]].

## Evolution
- [1988] Full critique: market frequently ≠ always efficient; the 63-year >20%
  arbitrage record ($1,000 → $97M at 20%) as discordant evidence EMT ignores; the
  caveat that style alone guarantees nothing.
