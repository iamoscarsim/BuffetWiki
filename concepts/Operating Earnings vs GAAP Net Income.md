# Operating Earnings vs GAAP Net Income

Buffett's insistence that shareholders judge Berkshire by its **operating earnings** — the profits of its
businesses plus dividends and interest — and **ignore the GAAP "bottom line,"** which accounting rules
make wildly misleading. The theme becomes a fixture of the letters from [[2017]] on, when a new rule made
the gap acute.

**The 2017 warning.** Buffett devotes the letter's second page to a "new accounting rule" effective
**2018**: GAAP now requires the **net change in unrealized gains and losses on stocks** to be run through
**net income** each period. With Berkshire holding **$170 billion** of marketable stocks whose value can
"easily swing by $10 billion or more within a quarterly reporting period," this "will produce some truly
wild and capricious swings in our GAAP bottom-line… For analytical purposes, Berkshire's 'bottom-line'
will be useless." It compounds an older distortion — **realized** gains, which Berkshire books when it is
"intelligent" to sell, not to manage earnings, so a great portfolio year can coincide with small realized
gains, or vice versa. Buffett fears the rule will let headlines "unnecessarily frighten or encourage"
investors "for whom accounting is a foreign language," and continues releasing results late Friday / early
Saturday so analysts can interpret them first.

The principle is the accounting-honesty flipside of his critique of *other* companies' "[[Owner Orientation and Candor|adjusted earnings]]": Berkshire counts all real costs as expenses, yet must now
*add* unreal volatility to its headline number. The remedy he presses on owners is the same one he applies
to investees ([[Look-Through Earnings]], [[Intrinsic Business Value]]): value a business by its durable
earning power, not the period's mark-to-market noise — "**in the short run, the market is a voting machine;
in the long run… a weighing machine**" (Graham).

## Evolution
- [[2017]] The new GAAP rule announced (effective 2018); Buffett warns GAAP net income will become
  "useless" and begs focus on **operating earnings**.
- [[2018]] The rule bites: huge headline swings in reported net income from mark-to-market stock moves,
  exactly as warned.
- [[2019]] **The rule swings the other way — a "crazy 1,900% increase."** Rising markets pushed net
  unrealized gains **up $53.7B**, lifting GAAP net income to **$81.4B** — vs. the prior year's $4B (a
  down-market figure). Buffett notes the accounting profession's pre-2018 stance (unrealized gains never
  in earnings; losses only if "other than temporary") was a "monumental shift." "In what we might call the
  real world… the intrinsic value of the stocks we own grew steadily and substantially." The plea repeats:
  "**focus on operating earnings… ignore both quarterly and annual gains or losses from investments.**"
- [[2020]] **A four-part GAAP figure — including a real loss.** GAAP net earnings of **$42.5B** decompose
  into **operating $21.9B** (down 9% in the COVID year), **+$4.9B realized gains**, **+$26.7B unrealized**
  (the mark-to-market noise), and a genuinely meaningful **−$11B write-down** (mostly [[Precision Castparts]]).
  "**Operating earnings are what count most, even during periods when they are not the largest item in our
  GAAP total.**" The capital-gains components "fluctuate capriciously from year to year," but Buffett
  reaffirms the unrecorded **retained earnings** of investees are "building value — lots of value."
- [[2022]] **"100% misleading."** Record **operating earnings of $30.8B** sat beside a wildly swinging GAAP
  line: quarterly GAAP of **+5.5, −43.8, −2.7, +18.2** vs. steady operating earnings of 7.0, 9.3, 7.8, 6.7.
  "**The GAAP earnings are 100% misleading when viewed quarterly or even annually**," their gyrations
  "regularly and mindlessly headlined by media." A new warning: even the *operating* figure "can easily be
  manipulated" — "'Bold imaginative accounting'… has become one of the shames of capitalism."
- [[2023]] **"Worse-than-useless."** GAAP "net earnings" of **$90B (2021), −$23B (2022), $96B (2023)**
  ("What in the world is going on?") are contrasted with Berkshire's preferred **operating earnings of
  $27.6B / $30.9B / $37.4B** (a record). The mandated figure is "worse-than-useless" because it sweeps in
  unrealized gains that "can exceed $5 billion a day" — yet it is duly "sanctified" by FASB, SEC and the
  auditors and "transmitted throughout the world." Buffett: "Galileo's experience… should have taught us
  not to mess with mandates from on high. But… we can be stubborn." Graham's voting-machine/weighing-
  machine maxim recurs.
- [[2024]] **Record operating earnings $47.4B.** "We regularly — endlessly, some readers may groan —
  emphasize this measure" over GAAP, which sweeps in capital gains "whether realized or unrealized."
  Scorecard: insurance-underwriting **$9,020M**, insurance-investment income **$13,670M**, BNSF $5,031M,
  BHE $3,730M, other controlled $13,072M, non-controlled $1,519M, other $1,395M. "EBITDA, a flawed favorite
  of Wall Street, is not for us."
