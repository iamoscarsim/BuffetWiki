# The Five Groves

Buffett's framework, introduced in the [[2018]] **"Focus on the Forest — Forget the Trees"** essay, for
appraising Berkshire's [[Intrinsic Business Value|intrinsic value]] without getting lost in its hundreds
of individual "trees." The forest, he says, contains **five "groves," each appraisable as a whole**:

1. **The non-insurance controlled businesses** — "the most valuable grove," owned ≥80% (usually 100%).
   These earned **$16.8 billion after-tax** in 2018, "after *all* income taxes, interest, managerial
   compensation… restructuring, depreciation, amortization and home-office overhead" — a "far cry" from
   Wall Street's "adjusted EBITDA." The two "**towering redwoods**" are **[[Burlington Northern Santa Fe|BNSF]]** and **[[MidAmerican Energy|BHE]]** (90.9% owned), together earning $9.3B pre-tax.
2. **The equity portfolio** — ~5–10% stakes in great public businesses, worth ~**$173 billion** at
   year-end 2018; its true worth lies less in the $3.8B of dividends than in investees' far larger
   **retained earnings** ([[Look-Through Earnings]]).
3. **The jointly-controlled companies** — a quartet: 26.7% of **[[Kraft Heinz]]**, 50% of
   **[[Berkadia Commercial Mortgage|Berkadia]]** and Electric Transmission Texas, 38.6% of **[[Pilot Flying J]]** (~$1.3B of Berkshire's share of after-tax earnings).
4. **Cash and Treasuries** — $112B in T-bills/cash plus $20B of other fixed income, anchored by a pledged
   **$20 billion cash floor** ([[Financial Strength]]).
5. **Insurance and float** — the "less obvious" grove whose **[[Insurance Float|float]]** funds much of
   the first four at below-zero cost (Berkshire's "fifth grove… a source of financing… cost-free — or
   maybe even better").

**Why it matters: the whole exceeds the sum of the parts.** Buffett's key claim is that assembling the
five groves into one entity is itself value-creating — it lets Berkshire "**seamlessly and objectively
allocate major amounts of capital, eliminate enterprise risk, avoid insularity, fund assets at
exceptionally low cost, occasionally take advantage of tax efficiencies, and minimize overhead**." To
approximate intrinsic value, sum the four "asset-laden" groves, subtract deferred tax on unrealized
securities gains, and credit the float (the fifth grove) as cost-free funding. He pointedly refuses to
deduct hypothetical taxes on selling wholly-owned businesses — "it would be foolish for us to sell any of
our wonderful companies." The groves framework is the mature successor to the older two-column
investments-plus-earnings valuation aid, fitted to a Berkshire now dominated by operating businesses.

## Evolution
- [[2018]] Framework introduced (replacing book value as the headline metric): the five groves and the
  "whole > sum of the parts" claim; appraise by summing the four asset groves, net of deferred tax,
  funded by the fifth (float).
