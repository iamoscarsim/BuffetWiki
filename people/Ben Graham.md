# Ben Graham

Benjamin Graham — Buffett's teacher at Columbia, his employer at Graham-Newman Corp.,
and the author of *Security Analysis* (with [[David Dodd]]) and *The Intelligent
Investor* — is the intellectual root of Buffett's entire method. Though Berkshire's
philosophy evolved (under [[Charlie Munger]]'s influence) from Graham-style "cigar-butt"
bargains toward paying fair prices for wonderful businesses, the foundations remain
pure Graham: stocks as fractional pieces of businesses, a margin of safety, and
temperament over cleverness.

Graham recurs throughout the letters as the source of Buffett's touchstones. The
**[[Mr. Market]]** allegory ([[1987]]) is explicitly "Ben's"; so is "In the short run
the market is a voting machine but in the long run it is a weighing machine."
[[1985]]'s "oil discovered in hell" story mocking efficient markets is Graham's. The
**[[Arbitrage]]** principles Buffett applied for decades ([[1988]]) were learned at
Graham-Newman, where Buffett worked at 24. And "Investment is most intelligent when it
is most businesslike," cited in the [[WPPSS Bonds]] discussion, is Graham's. Buffett
repeatedly calls him "my friend and teacher" and credits Berkshire's prosperity to "the
fruit of [the] intellectual tree" he and Dodd planted.

## Evolution
- [1977–88] Cited throughout as teacher and source: stocks-as-businesses,
  [[Mr. Market]] (1987), arbitrage principles learned at Graham-Newman (1988),
  "voting machine / weighing machine," and the margin-of-safety habit of mind.
- [2000] **A 50th-anniversary tribute.** "It was exactly 50 years ago that I entered Ben Graham's
  class at Columbia." Buffett recalls that during the prior decade he had "loved … analyzing, buying
  and selling stocks" but his "results were no better than average"; from 1951 his performance
  improved — "The only new ingredient was Ben's ideas. Quite simply, **a few hours spent at the feet
  of the master proved far more valuable … than … ten years of supposedly original thinking.**" "In
  addition to being a great teacher, Ben was a wonderful friend. My debt to him is incalculable."
