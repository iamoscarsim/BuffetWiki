# Todd Combs

Investment manager hired in **2010** as the first of the new generation meant to run Berkshire's
portfolio when Buffett, Munger and **[[Lou Simpson]]** (who retired that year) are gone ([[2010]]).
Buffett had searched four years for someone whose record showed not just returns but *how* they were
earned and a real "sensitivity to risk (which in no way should be measured by **beta**)" — above all
"the ability to anticipate the effects of economic scenarios not previously observed" (see [[Risk and Volatility]]). When commentators called Combs "little-known," Buffett shrugged: "Our goal was to find
a **2-year-old Secretariat, not a 10-year-old Seabiscuit**" (the same obscurity that once attached to
Lou in 1979, Ajit in 1985, "or, for that matter, Charlie in 1959").

Combs began managing **$1–3 billion**, paid a salary plus a performance-contingent payment versus the
S&P, with **deferrals and carryforwards** so that "see-saw performance" can't earn undeserved payouts
— a deliberate rebuke to hedge-fund "heads-I-win" economics ("investors who put money with such
managers should be labeled **patsies, not partners**"). Berkshire's "only style box is **smart**."

## Evolution
- [2010] Hired as a new investment manager (initially $1–3B); the "Secretariat not Seabiscuit" pick;
  risk ≠ beta; anti-hedge-fund compensation with deferrals.
- [2011]–[2013] Allocation steadily increased; later joined by **Ted Weschler**; the two become
  Berkshire's designated post-Buffett portfolio managers.
- [[2024]] **Operating manager, too: the GEICO turnaround.** Beyond investing, Combs has served as
  **CEO of [[GEICO]]** since 2020. The letter credits him: "**In five years, Todd Combs has reshaped GEICO
  in a major way, increasing efficiency and bringing underwriting practices up to date**" — "a long-held
  gem that needed major repolishing"; the 2024 improvement was "spectacular," a major driver of the year's
  record insurance earnings.
