# Ajit Jain

Ajit Jain is the insurance executive who became, in Buffett's repeated later
judgment, one of the most valuable people at Berkshire — the architect of its
super-catastrophe and large-risk reinsurance operations and a manager whose
underwriting judgment Buffett came to rank with his own. Recruited by
[[Mike Goldberg]], he joined Berkshire's insurance group in 1986.

His **first mention** in the letters comes in [[1988]], almost in passing: Berkshire is
well positioned on float because "Mike Goldberg, with special help from **Ajit Jain**,
Dinos Iordanou, and the National Indemnity managerial team," has built the capability
to write large and unusual risks. That understated debut belies what follows — across
the 1990s and 2000s Buffett devotes ever-greater praise to Jain, crediting him with
building from scratch a reinsurance business worth many billions, and telling
shareholders that if they ever see Jain, Munger and himself in a sinking boat, they
should "swim to Ajit." This page will track that arc as the letters develop it.

## Evolution
- [1988] First mention: credited (with Dinos Iordanou and the National Indemnity team)
  for helping [[Mike Goldberg]] position Berkshire's insurance operation to write large
  risks and build an exceptional float-to-premium ratio.
- [1989] Named again in [[Mike Goldberg]]'s management team (with Rod Eldred, Dinos
  Iordanou, Phil Urban, Don Wurster) as Berkshire makes its **super-catastrophe** debut —
  writing and retaining up to $250M of CAT coverage after Hurricane Hugo and the
  California earthquake. This large-risk, retain-don't-lay-off reinsurance is the business
  Jain would go on to build into Berkshire's reinsurance powerhouse.
- [1992] The praise turns emphatic: "**Ajit Jain, who runs our reinsurance operation, is
  simply the best in this business.**" Buffett ties Berkshire's discipline to Jain's
  judgment — the operation **rejects more than 98%** of the business it is offered ("a fool
  and his money are soon invited everywhere"), and "the bulk of our float… comes from large
  transactions developed by Ajit," with further float growth expected. This is the year Jain
  moves from a supporting mention to the named engine of the super-cat franchise (see
  [[Super-Catastrophe Reinsurance]]).
- [1993] "In Ajit Jain, we have **by far the best manager in this business**." Buffett pairs
  Jain's judgment with Berkshire's balance-sheet strength (net worth 10–20× competitors') to
  explain why Berkshire can offer super-cat limits no rival can match — and why each of the
  world's four largest reinsurers buys cover from Berkshire.
- [1994] "Ajit Jain, our super-cat manager, whose underwriting skills are the finest. **His
  value to us is simply enormous.**" His judgment-driven underwriting (super-cat policies being
  "small in number, large in size and non-standardized") lets Berkshire write single policies up
  to **$400–500M** that no rival will touch.
- [1995] "**The guiding genius of our super-cat business**," who "writes important non-cat
  business as well." His versatility is illustrated by three single-risk 1995 deals: insuring
  **Mike Tyson's life** (a sum declining fight-by-fight), **Lloyd's** against more than 225 "names"
  dying in the year, and the **launch + a year of orbit of two Chinese satellites**. Berkshire
  offered a **$1 billion** policy (largest accepted: $400M) on the strength of his underwriting and
  Berkshire's balance sheet.
- [1996] "**The best person in the world to run our super-cat business … whose value to Berkshire
  is simply enormous.**" Buffett contrasts Jain's discipline with the "disastrous propositions" he
  himself "embraced all too many of … in the 1970s" and GEICO's foolish early-1980s reinsurance
  runoff — "Ajit, I can assure you, won't make mistakes of this type."
- [1997] Credited with having **developed the super-cat business "from scratch"** — "he has
  contributed to Berkshire's success in a variety of other ways as well." Buffett distills his
  edge: "both the **discipline to walk away** from business that is inadequately priced and the
  **imagination** to then find other opportunities … Ajit would have been a star in whatever
  career he chose; fortunately for us, he enjoys insurance."
- [1998] Buffett reveals the scale of Jain's contribution: "over the last decade [he] has
  **personally accounted for a significant portion of Berkshire's operating earnings**." Jain also
  developed Berkshire's new online **annuity** business — "While Charlie and I sleep, Ajit keeps
  thinking of new ways to add value to Berkshire."
- [1999] **The fullest tribute yet**, set against General Re's terrible year: "It's simply
  **impossible to overstate Ajit's value to Berkshire**. He has from scratch built an outstanding
  reinsurance business … [that] now holds **$6.3 billion of float**" at an underwriting profit over
  his tenure. Buffett enumerates the rare combination: "the **intelligence** to properly rate most
  risks; the **realism** to forget about those he can't evaluate; the **courage** to write huge
  policies when the premium is appropriate; and the **discipline** to reject even the smallest risk
  when the premium is inadequate … For one person to have them all is remarkable." His super-cat
  book is inherently volatile — "to date, we have benefitted from good luck."
- [2000] The **retroactive-reinsurance and large-risk** machine is "**almost single-handedly the
  work of Ajit Jain** … It is impossible to overstate how valuable Ajit is to Berkshire. **Don't
  worry about my health; worry about his.**" The year's deals showcase his range: a **$2.4 billion
  reinsurance premium** ("perhaps the largest in history") retroactively covering a major U.K.
  company; a disability policy on the Texas Rangers' **Alex Rodriguez** ($252M contract — "probably
  also set a record"); and the **Grab.com** Internet $1 billion prize ($170M present value; no one
  won). Buffett's framing of why others won't compete: such one-off policies can't be laid off via
  standard reinsurance, so any rival CEO risks "a horrible quarterly earnings number" — "Charlie and
  I, however, like any proposition that makes compelling mathematical sense, regardless of its
  effect on reported earnings."
- [2001] With **only 18 associates**, Jain "manages one of the world's largest reinsurance
  operations measured by assets, and the **largest** based upon the size of individual risks
  assumed." Against General Re's discipline collapse, Buffett draws the contrast sharply: "I have
  known the details of almost every policy that Ajit has written since he came with us in 1986, and
  **never on even a single occasion** have I seen him break any of our three [[Insurance Underwriting Discipline|underwriting rules]]." Post-9/11, Jain wrote a string of jumbo terrorism
  and property policies retained entirely for Berkshire's account — a South American refinery
  ($578M xs $1B), $1B of airline third-party terrorism cover, a North Sea oil platform, the Sears
  Tower, the World Cup, the 2002 Winter Olympics — carefully avoiding correlated aggregation. "No
  one can match the speed with which Ajit can offer huge policies… It's impossible to overstate his
  value to Berkshire."
- [2002] "**The major reason our float cost us so little last year.** If we ever put a photo in a
  Berkshire annual report, it will be of Ajit. In color!" His operation had amassed **$13.4B** of
  float "from a standing start in 1986," with a workforce of only ~20, and — most important —
  produced a **$534M underwriting profit**, even after a **$428M** charge amortizing the
  retroactive-reinsurance "deferred charges" asset ($3.4B at year-end; ~80% of Berkshire's $6.6B
  asbestos/environmental reserves sit under *capped* contracts). "We assume risks… far larger than
  those retained by any other insurer in the world… we would rather earn a lumpy 15% over time than
  a smooth 12%. **If you see Ajit at our annual meeting, bow deeply.**"
- [2003] Staff of **23**; "he continued to pour it on," with his non-retroactive book earning a
  **$1,434M underwriting profit**. The signature 2003 deal: a **$1 billion** policy on **PepsiCo**'s
  prize drawing (present-value exposure ~$250M; "I helpfully suggested that any winner be paid $1 a
  year for a billion years, but that proposal didn't fly") — no winner on Sept 14, and Pepsi renewed
  for 2004. "You can be confident of a terrific result from this **one-of-a-kind manager**."
- [2004] Amid four Florida hurricanes (Berkshire's $1.25B share of ~$25B industry losses), Jain
  "continues to successfully underwrite huge risks that no other reinsurer is willing or able to
  accept. **Ajit's value to Berkshire is enormous.**" His B-H Reinsurance book held $15.3B of float
  and earned a $417M underwriting profit.
- [2005] B-H Reinsurance took a **−$1,069M** underwriting loss in the record hurricane year (Berkshire's
  total hurricane losses $3.4B; float $16.2B). With Joe Brandon at Gen Re, Jain shared the decision
  to write mega-cat coverage only at "**prices far higher than prevailed last year**" given climate
  uncertainty (the [[Super-Catastrophe Reinsurance|Pascal's-wager]] caution). Applied Underwriters
  joined Berkshire via a Jain reinsurance contract.
- [2006] A banner, hurricane-free year: B-H Reinsurance earned a **+$1,658M** underwriting profit
  (float $16.9B). Jain and Buffett reviewed and wrote the landmark **[[Equitas]]** retroactive
  policy ($7.12B premium / $13.9B cover) — "Ajit and I think the odds are in our favor." Buffett
  again names him at the head of his insurance "Hall of Fame roster," and warns that betting "**that
  evolving atmospheric changes are benign… would be a huge mistake.**"
- [2007] "**Since joining Berkshire in 1986, Ajit Jain has built a truly great specialty reinsurance
  operation from scratch. For one-of-a-kind mammoth transactions, the world now turns to him.**" The
  **[[Equitas]]** transfer ($7.1B premium) is performing well at this "very early date"; the London
  team under Scott Moser "is first-rate." B-H Reinsurance underwriting profit **+$1,427M** (float
  $23.7B).
- [2008] **"One of the most remarkable businesses in the world."** Jain's Stamford operation —
  **just 31 employees** — earned a **+$1,324M** underwriting profit (float $24,221M). "From year to
  year, Ajit's business is never the same. It features very large transactions, incredible speed of
  execution and a willingness to quote on policies that leave others scratching their heads. When
  there is a huge and unusual risk to be insured, Ajit is almost certain to be called." Buffett
  retells the recruiting joke — after Jain arrived in 1986, "I wrote his parents in New Delhi and
  asked if they had another one like him at home… There isn't anyone like Ajit." His crew also took
  on the new muni-bond insurer **[[Berkshire Hathaway Assurance|BHAC]]** ("Charlie and I decided it
  was high time for them to start doing a full day's work").
- [2009] **"Swim to Ajit."** Buffett retells the origin — Jain walked into the Omaha office on a
  Saturday in 1985 (discovered by [[Mike Goldberg]], "now elevated to St. Mike"), was put in charge of
  National Indemnity's "small and struggling reinsurance operation," and built it into "a one-of-a-kind
  giant." Now ~30 people. In 2009 he negotiated a **life-reinsurance contract that could produce $50
  billion of premium** over ~50 years. "If Charlie, I and Ajit are ever in a sinking boat — and you
  can only save one of us — **swim to Ajit.**" BH Reinsurance underwriting profit **$349M** (float
  $26,223M).
- [2010] **"Even kryptonite bounces off Ajit."** From "a standing start in 1985," Jain has built an
  operation with **$30 billion of float** and significant cumulative underwriting profits — "a feat
  that no CEO of any other insurer has come close to matching." In 2010 he greatly expanded **life
  reinsurance**, developing ~**$2 billion of annual premium that will repeat for decades.** "He never
  exposes Berkshire to risks that are inappropriate in relation to our resources." BH Reinsurance
  underwriting profit **$176M** (float $30,370M).
- [2011] **"Charlie would gladly trade me for a second Ajit. Alas, there is none."** Float reached
  **$34 billion** "from a standing start in 1985." A heavy global-catastrophe year (Japan/NZ
  earthquakes, Thai floods) gave BH Reinsurance a rare underwriting **loss of −$714M** (float
  $33,728M) — but Buffett underscores the conservatism: even a **$250 billion industry mega-cat**
  ("about triple anything it has ever faced") "would likely [leave] Berkshire… a moderate profit"
  thanks to its diverse earnings streams, while rivals "would be far in the red, and some would face
  insolvency."
- [2012] BH Reinsurance rebounded to a **+$304M** underwriting profit (float $34,821M); "if you meet
  Ajit at the annual meeting, **bow deeply.**" Float reached $35B.
- [2013] **"Ajit's mind is an idea factory."** Float **$37B** (UW profit **$1,294M**); in June he
  launched **[[Berkshire Hathaway Specialty Insurance]]** (BHSI), Berkshire's leap into commercial
  insurance (Peter Eastwood), "instantly accepted" on the strength of Berkshire's balance sheet.
  Buffett repeats the $250B mega-cat illustration — Berkshire would stay "awash in cash, looking for
  large opportunities" while rivals reel.
- [2014] **"His underwriting skills are unmatched."** BH Reinsurance float **$42,454M** (UW profit
  $606M); BHSI expanded internationally under Peter Eastwood. Jain wrote a **$3 billion single-premium**
  policy — second only to the 2007 Lloyd's $7.1B deal; "**all eight**" P/C policies in history with a
  premium over $1B "were written by Berkshire." In his Golden Anniversary essay, **Munger names Jain
  (with [[Greg Abel]]) "world-leading"** and "in some important ways… a better business executive than
  Buffett," predicting he would never leave and would preserve [[The Berkshire System]].
- [2015] BH Reinsurance float **$44,108M** (UW profit $421M). Buffett retells the origin with a fresh
  flourish: when Ajit "entered Berkshire's office on a Saturday in 1986, **he did not have a day's
  experience in the insurance business**," yet [[Mike Goldberg]] "handed him the keys to our
  reinsurance business… With that move, Mike achieved **sainthood**: Since then, Ajit has created tens
  of billions of value for Berkshire shareholders." (Soft reinsurance pricing and low rates make the
  next decade tougher for the segment.)
- [[2016]] **"Make the trade!"** BH Reinsurance, "first by float size," earned **$822M** of underwriting
  profit (float **$45,081M**). Buffett's tribute reaches a new pitch: Jain "insures risks that no one else
  has the desire or the capital to take on… combines capacity, speed, decisiveness and, most important,
  brains in a manner unique in the insurance business," yet "never exposes Berkshire to risks that are
  inappropriate in relation to our resources." The kicker: "If there were ever to be **another Ajit** and
  you could swap me for him, don't hesitate. **Make the trade!**" He restates the $250B-mega-cat
  illustration (Berkshire would likely still book a profit while rivals face insolvency), and again
  credits [[Mike Goldberg]] with "**sainthood**" for handing Jain the reinsurance keys in 1986.
- [[2017]] **Named Berkshire Vice Chairman of Insurance.** "I've saved the best for last": early in 2018
  the board elected Jain (and **[[Greg Abel]]**) a **director and Vice Chairman**, with Jain "**responsible
  for insurance operations**" while Buffett and Munger turn to "investments and capital allocation." The
  formal elevation of the man Buffett told shareholders to "swim to." His operation also booked the
  record **$10.2B [[AIG retroactive reinsurance]]** policy in January 2017 (float past $100B), though
  the year's hurricanes (Harvey/Irma/Maria) drove Berkshire's first underwriting loss in 15 years. See
  [[The Berkshire System]].
- [[2018]] **The structure validated.** A year into his role over all insurance, Buffett calls the change
  "really good news": "**Berkshire is now far better managed than when I alone was supervising
  operations. Ajit and Greg have rare talents, and Berkshire blood flows through their veins.**"
- [[2021]] **"Nobody's perfect."** Buffett retells the **1986 hiring** that built the insurance Giant: at
  their first Saturday meeting he asked Ajit's insurance experience — "**None**" — and replied, "Nobody's
  perfect," and hired him. "That was my lucky day… he continues to be [perfect] — 35 years later." Much of
  Berkshire's insurance value (float now **$147B**) "is attributable to Berkshire's good luck in my 1986
  hiring of Ajit Jain."
- [[2023]] **"Cooperstown."** With insurance "carrying the day" in a record year (underwriting profit
  $5,428M; investment income $9,567M), Buffett reaffirms: "**our position would not be what it is if Ajit
  Jain had not joined Berkshire in 1986**." Jain's lineup of P/C managers "is to P/C insurance what
  Cooperstown's honorees are to baseball." He shares the 2024-meeting stage as one of the three who now
  steer Berkshire (with [[Greg Abel]]; Charlie absent).
- [[2024]] **"From an obscure Omaha-based company into a world leader."** In the P/C essay Buffett credits
  Jain with building Berkshire insurance into a global power "renowned for both its taste for risk and its
  Gibraltar-like financial strength." With GEICO reshaped by [[Todd Combs]], the segment drove the record
  year (underwriting $9,020M; investment income $13,670M). Jain shares the annual-meeting stage with
  **[[Greg Abel]]** as Buffett's tenure winds down.
