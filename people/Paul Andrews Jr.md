# Paul Andrews Jr.

The entrepreneur who built **[[TTI]]**, the Fort Worth electronic-components distributor, from
**$112,000 of sales to $1.3 billion** over 35 years, and sold ~80% of it to Berkshire in 2006
([[2006]]). Having seen "how disruptive the death of a founder can be," he deliberately chose his
company's owner in advance rather than leaving an auction to "a trust officer or lawyer," explicitly
rejecting a synergy-seeking "strategic" buyer and a debt-loading private-equity firm — "**That left
Berkshire.**" He and Buffett met the morning of November 15 "and made a deal before lunch."

## Evolution
- [2006] Sells TTI to Berkshire; the founder-succession reasoning that makes Berkshire the buyer of
  choice.
- [[2021]] **Died — "A Wonderful Man and a Wonderful Business."** Buffett devotes a section to Andrews'
  life. The founding tale: fired by General Dynamics in 1971, he bet **$500** on himself (Tex-Tronics,
  first-year sales **$112,000**). He sold to Berkshire in 2006 — "**After a year of pondering… I want to
  sell to Berkshire because you are the only guy left**" — in "One meeting; one lunch; one deal." Under
  Berkshire, **[[TTI]]** grew from 2,387 to **8,043 employees** with earnings up **673%** (annual volume
  now $7.7B). Buffett would annually offer to raise his salary; Andrews would defer ("I'm too busy now").
  The breadth of his quiet generosity to Fort Worth was "extraordinary… In all ways, Paul was a class
  act." Crucially, the TTI relationship led Berkshire's 2009 board to Fort Worth — and thus to the
  **[[Burlington Northern Santa Fe|BNSF]]** acquisition (via mutual friend **John Roach**): "**TTI was
  soon to lead Berkshire to its most important acquisition.**"
