# Pete Liegl

The owner-manager who sold **[[Forest River]]** (the RV maker) to Berkshire in 2005 ([[2005]]) via a
two-page fax and a one-week handshake. Liegl is a Buffett archetype: a hands-on entrepreneur who had
earlier sold a smaller version of the business to an **LBO operator**, chafed when the buyer "began
telling him how to run the place," left, watched it go bankrupt, and **bought it back** — an
experience that made Berkshire's hands-off ownership ("I won't be telling Pete how to manage his
operation") exactly what he wanted. His line, in a *Wall Street Journal* profile: "It was easier to
sell my business than to renew my driver's license."

## Evolution
- [2005] Sells Forest River to Berkshire; the LBO-survivor entrepreneur who prizes Berkshire's
  operating autonomy.
- [[2024]] **Died in November at 80, "still working" — "One of a Kind."** A full tribute in the letter.
  The 2005 deal retold: Liegl named his own price and, asked to set his own salary, replied "**I looked at
  Berkshire's proxy statement and I wouldn't want to make more than my boss, so pay me $100,000 per
  year**" (plus a 10%-of-incremental-earnings bonus). "**During the next 19 years, Pete shot the lights
  out. No competitor came close to his performance.**" Buffett's lesson on hiring: "I never look at where
  a candidate has gone to school. Never!… a very large portion of business talent is **innate, with nature
  swamping nurture. Pete Liegl was a natural.**"
