# John Burr Williams

John Burr Williams was the economist whose 1938 book *The Theory of Investment Value* gave
Buffett the formal definition of [[Intrinsic Business Value]]. In the [[1992]] letter
Buffett condenses Williams' equation: "**the value of any stock, bond or business today is
determined by the cash inflows and outflows — discounted at an appropriate interest rate —
that can be expected to occur during the remaining life of the asset.**"

Buffett uses Williams to collapse the supposed opposition between "value" and "growth"
investing (see [[Value and Growth]]): the formula "is the same for stocks as for bonds,"
the only hard difference being that an equity analyst must *estimate* the future "coupons"
that a bond states explicitly — and that management quality dramatically affects those
equity coupons. The cheapest investment by this discounted-cash test is the one to buy,
"irrespective of whether the business grows or doesn't… or carries a high price or low in
relation to its current earnings and book value."
