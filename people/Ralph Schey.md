# Ralph Schey

Ralph Schey was the longtime CEO of [[Scott Fetzer]], which Berkshire acquired at
the end of 1985. Introduced in the [[1985]] letter as a manager with a "superb"
operating and capital-allocation record: he had pruned Scott Fetzer from 31
businesses down to a focused group, while astutely buying World Book in 1978. Buffett
came to regard Schey as one of his model managers, running a multi-business operation
with high returns on capital and shipping the excess cash to Omaha — exactly the
[[Capital Allocation]] discipline Berkshire prizes.

## Evolution
- [1985] CEO of newly-acquired Scott Fetzer; praised for rationalizing the company
  and for superb capital allocation. *(Coverage gap 1986–1991 — see [[Scott Fetzer]];
  to be backfilled at lint.)*
- [1992] "**Ralph Schey hit a grand slam**": [[Scott Fetzer]] earned a record **$110M
  pre-tax on only $116M of equity** — without leverage — and has distributed >100% of
  earnings to Berkshire over seven years while growing them. "Ralph just keeps on outdoing
  himself, and Berkshire shareholders owe him a great deal." Buffett hopes Schey, like
  [[Rose Blumkin|Mrs. B]], stays "with us at 99 also."
- [1994] The Scott Fetzer worked example crowns Schey: his managerial expertise (not cyclical
  peak, monopoly, or leverage) explains an ROE that would rank **#1 on the Fortune 500**. "Ben
  Graham taught me… it is not necessary to do extraordinary things to get extraordinary results…
  What a manager must do is handle the basics well and not get diverted. That's precisely
  Ralph's formula." "Forthright about problems and… self-confident without being self-important."
  Over 65 — and Buffett, keeping George Foreman's picture on his desk, vows his "scorn for a
  mandatory retirement age will grow stronger every year." Schey's pay (Scott-Fetzer-based,
  money's-not-free) is the model in the year's [[Executive Compensation]] essay.
- [1998] "**Little did I realize that he was just warming up.**" Schey's [[Scott Fetzer]] earned a
  new record **$96.5M after-tax on $112M of net worth**, essentially unleveraged — Buffett's lead
  example that Berkshire's managers are "truly legends in their industries," delivering earnings "at
  the very top of what conditions in their industries allow."
- [2000] **Retired at year-end after 15 years** running [[Scott Fetzer]] (he was 61 when Berkshire
  bought the company in 1985 — "Most companies, focused on the calendar rather than ability, would
  have benefited from Ralph's talents for only a few years"). His scorecard: **$1.03 billion
  distributed to Berkshire on a $230M net purchase price**, redeployed into other businesses, so his
  "contributions to Berkshire's present value extend well into the billions." Buffett welcomes him to
  **"Berkshire's Hall of Fame."**
