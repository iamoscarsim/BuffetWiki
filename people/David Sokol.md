# David Sokol

David Sokol is the CEO of **[[MidAmerican Energy]]**, introduced in the [[1999]] letter as
"talented and entrepreneurial" when Berkshire made its first utility investment. He runs
the operating business in which Berkshire took ~76% of the equity (but <10% of the vote),
alongside controlling shareholder and director **[[Walter Scott Jr]]**.

Sokol would become one of Berkshire's most prominent operating managers over the following
decade — building MidAmerican (later Berkshire Hathaway Energy) into a giant through
utility and pipeline acquisitions, and at times mentioned as a possible Buffett successor —
before his abrupt **2011 departure** over a personal stock-trading controversy involving
Lubrizol, an episode later letters will record. This page tracks that arc from its modest
1999 beginning.

## Evolution
- [1999] Introduced as MidAmerican's "talented and entrepreneurial CEO" at Berkshire's
  first electric-utility investment.
- [2002]–[2010] Built MidAmerican into a regulated-utility/pipeline giant with partner [[Greg Abel]]
  (the [[Constellation Energy]] rescue, wind leadership). In **August 2009** he also took over the
  troubled **[[Executive Jet|NetJets]]** as CEO, cutting debt and turning a $711M loss into a $207M
  pre-tax profit by 2010 — "**I can't overstate the breadth and importance of Dave Sokol's
  achievements**." Widely viewed as a leading Buffett-successor candidate.
- [2011] **Abrupt departure.** Sokol resigned after it emerged he had **bought [[Lubrizol]] shares
  before pitching the acquisition to Buffett** — a breach of Berkshire's conduct standards. The 2011
  letter, focused on the Lubrizol deal itself, notes MidAmerican now run by **[[Greg Abel]]** alone
  and NetJets by Jordan Hansell; Sokol's exit ended his run as a possible successor.
