# Malcolm Chace

Malcolm G. Chace, Jr. embodies the deepest institutional continuity in Berkshire's
history — the bridge from the old textile company to Buffett's control. He went to
work for Berkshire Fine Spinning Associates in 1931; that firm merged with Hathaway
Manufacturing Co. in 1955 to form the present **[[Berkshire Hathaway Textiles|Berkshire Hathaway]]**, and two years later Chace became its Chairman. He still held that role in
early 1965 when "he made it possible for Buffett Partnership, Ltd. to buy a key block of
Berkshire stock owned by some of his relatives" — the purchase that gave the partnership
**effective control** of the company ([[1991]]).

The Chace family kept its stock, holding for the next 27 years the second-largest
position in Berkshire, trailing only the Buffett family. In the 1991 letter Buffett
announces that Malcolm, then 88, would not stand for re-election as a director, but that
the relationship would continue: his son **Kim Chace** (Malcolm III) was nominated to
replace him. Buffett called Malcolm "a joy to work with."
