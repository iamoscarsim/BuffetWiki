# Bill Snyder

Bill Snyder is the Chairman of [[GEICO]] who, with investment chief [[Lou Simpson]],
ran the company after [[Jack Byrne]] left in 1985 to head [[Fireman's Fund]]. He
embodies the cost-discipline half of GEICO's success — the operating counterpart to
Simpson's investing.

Snyder appears in management alongside Byrne in the early 1980s and becomes Chairman
when Byrne departs. In the [[1986]] letter Buffett uses him to introduce the
**[[Economic Moat]]** metaphor: "No one understands this moat-around-the-castle
concept better than Bill Snyder... He continually widens the moat by driving down
costs still more, thereby defending and strengthening the economic franchise." Under
Snyder GEICO's total expense ratio fell from 24.1% to 23.5% of premiums, and Buffett
expects it "almost certain to drop further" — the engine of GEICO's low-cost edge.

## Evolution
- [1982] Joins [[Jack Byrne]] in GEICO's management; "Let Jack Do It" the corporate creed.
- [1985] Becomes Chairman as Byrne leaves for [[Fireman's Fund]], with [[Lou Simpson]]
  as Vice Chairman.
- [1986] The face of GEICO's cost discipline; the [[Economic Moat]] passage — driving
  the expense ratio from 24.1% to 23.5% and "continually widening the moat."
