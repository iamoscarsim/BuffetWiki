# Harold Alfond

Harold Alfond founded [[Dexter Shoe]], the Maine shoemaker Berkshire acquired in November
1993 ([[1993]]). He "started working in a shoe factory at 25 cents an hour when he was 20"
and founded Dexter in **1956 with $10,000** of capital; in 1958 his nephew **[[Peter Lunder]]**
joined him. Together they built a business producing over 7.5 million pairs of shoes a year
and supplying Nordstrom and J.C. Penney to award-winning standard. Buffett ranked Dexter
among "the best-managed companies Charlie and I have seen in our business lifetimes."

Alfond and Lunder accepted **Berkshire stock** (not cash) for Dexter, a tax-free exchange
that Buffett praised at the time for letting them swap a single concentrated business for a
diversified ownership stake they could hold or gift. They stayed on to run Dexter — "at
Berkshire, we do not tell .400 hitters how to swing." (The stock-for-business structure later
made Dexter Buffett's costliest mistake; see [[Dexter Shoe]].)

## Evolution
- [1993] Introduced as Dexter's founder (1956, $10,000); sold to Berkshire for stock and
