# Don Keough

Don Keough was President of [[Coca-Cola]] and [[Roberto Goizueta]]'s partner in the
company's 1980s revival — and, by a small-world coincidence Buffett relishes, "once my
across-the-street neighbor in Omaha." He appears in the [[1989]] Coca-Cola essay as
half of the management duo that made the investment compelling.

Buffett credits Keough, with Goizueta, for having "first rethought and focused the
company's policies and then energetically carried them out," turning the world's most
ubiquitous product into "a shareholder's dream" as overseas sales exploded. The Omaha
connection is part of Buffett's gentle self-mockery in the essay — he had a front-row
view of the people who would transform Coca-Cola and still took 52 years (and until
"the summer of 1988") to buy the stock.

## Evolution
- [1989] Introduced as Coca-Cola's President and Buffett's former Omaha neighbor; with
  [[Roberto Goizueta]], the management behind the company's revival and Berkshire's
  investment.
- [1993] **Retired from Coca-Cola** (his retirement, as he put it, "lasted about 14 hours").
  Buffett's warmest tribute yet: "one of the most extraordinary human beings I've ever known…
  a man who brings out the absolute best in everyone." He fills in the backstory — Keough
  bought the house across the street in Omaha in 1958 as "a coffee salesman with a big family
  and a small income," and Buffett's read of him "was a factor in my decision to have Berkshire
  make a record $1 billion investment in Coca-Cola." With [[Roberto Goizueta]], Keough took Coke
  from $4.4B to **$58B** of market value in under 13 years.
