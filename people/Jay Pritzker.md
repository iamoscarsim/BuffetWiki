# Jay Pritzker

The "business genius" behind the **[[Marmon Group]]** and the Pritzker family's vast holdings, and a
figure in Buffett's life from **1954**. As a 24-year-old at Graham-Newman, Buffett was sent to a
Rockwood Chocolate shareholders' meeting where Pritzker — who had taken control of the company —
had launched a one-of-a-kind tender offering **80 pounds of cocoa beans per share**, a tax-efficient
maneuver Buffett later described in the [[1988]] arbitrage essay ("the possibilities… escaped all the
other experts… including my bosses, Ben and Jerry"). Pritzker "gave me an education on the 1954 tax
code," and Buffett "avidly followed [his] business dealings… many and brilliant" thereafter.

Jay died in **1999**; his brother **Bob** ran Marmon for ~50 years. When the family decided to sell,
Berkshire's 2007 purchase of Marmon was "**done in the way Jay would have liked**" — priced off the
financials, no advisors, no nit-picking, "**a deal is a deal**."

## Evolution
- [1988] The Rockwood Chocolate cocoa-bean tender — Buffett's case study in tax-efficient arbitrage
  (see [[Arbitrage]]); a formative 1954 lesson from a 24-year-old's encounter with Pritzker.
- [2007] His legacy returns: Berkshire buys the Pritzkers' [[Marmon Group]] "in the way Jay would
  have liked."
