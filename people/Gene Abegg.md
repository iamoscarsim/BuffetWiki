# Gene Abegg

Gene Abegg founded the [[Illinois National Bank]] of Rockford in 1931 with
$250,000 and ran it for decades as one of the most profitable banks in America. In
the [[1977]] letter he is 80 years old, "still running a banking operation without
peer," and has asked that a successor (Peter Jeffrey) be brought in while he
continues as Chairman. Abegg is an early entry in Buffett's gallery of admired
owner-operators — managers who run their businesses with thrift and skill long
past conventional retirement age.

## Evolution
- [1977] Age 80, still leading the bank he founded in 1931; arranges succession.
- [1980] **Died July 2, 1980, at age 82.** Buffett's eulogy: "From the time we first
  met, Gene shot straight 100% of the time." He laid all negative factors "face up on
  the table" in negotiations and for years afterward kept disclosing *undiscussed
  items of value*. At the 1933 bank holiday he held enough cash to pay all depositors
  in full — "Gene never forgot he was handling other people's money." Buffett uses him
  to make a broader point about the test of character when a seller stays on to run
  the business as an employee.
