# Carl Reichardt

Carl Reichardt was the CEO of [[Wells Fargo]] when Berkshire bought its ~10% stake in
[[1990]], and the manager whose quality made Buffett willing to invest in a banking
industry he otherwise distrusts. With his partner Paul Hazen, Reichardt is Buffett's
model of disciplined bank management.

Buffett likens the Reichardt–Hazen pairing to [[Tom Murphy]] and Dan Burke at
[[Capital Cities]]: each "stronger than the sum of its parts because each partner
understands, trusts and admires the other"; both "pay able people well, but abhor having
a bigger head count than is needed"; both "attack costs as vigorously when profits are
at record levels as when they are under pressure"; and both "stick with what they
understand and let their abilities, not their egos, determine what they attempt." He
calls Reichardt "the dean" of bank managers — "more avidly recruited than any others in
the banking business," yet unhirable: paying twice as much for a comparable bank
wouldn't get you a Carl Reichardt to run it.

## Evolution
- [1990] Introduced as Wells Fargo's CEO (with Paul Hazen) — "the best managers in the
  business," the reason Berkshire invested. The Watson "smart in spots" discipline; "the
  dean" of banking no rival can poach.
- [1994] **Retired** as CEO of [[Wells Fargo]]. Buffett thanks him alongside [[Dan Burke]]:
  both "encountered very tough industry conditions" yet left their companies "with record
  earnings, added luster, and bright prospects… in outstanding hands. We owe them our
  gratitude."
