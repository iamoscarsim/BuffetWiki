# Katharine Graham

Katharine ("Kay") Graham was CEO of the [[Washington Post]] Company and the manager
Buffett credits for the extraordinary returns on Berkshire's 1973 investment. In the
[[1985]] letter he details the "triple dip" the Post delivered and attributes the
extra ~$160M of gain (versus other media companies) to "the superior nature of the
managerial decisions made by Kay" — chiefly her courage to **repurchase huge
quantities of stock at bargain prices** during the mid-1970s, and her skill in
growing business value. Buffett served on the Post's board (resigning in 1986 when
the Cap Cities purchase created an FCC conflict) and was a close advisor and friend
to Graham. "Her stunning business success has in large part gone unreported but among
Berkshire shareholders it should not go unappreciated."

## Evolution
- [1985] Credited for the Washington Post's value creation — especially bargain-
  priced buybacks — turning a $10.6M investment into $221M.
- [1993] **Retired as chairman** of [[Washington Post]] (having given up the CEO title three
  years earlier) and was elected by *Fortune's* Board of Editors to the **Business Hall of
  Fame**. Buffett notes all his 1973 judgments were validated — especially the bet that Kay
  "would treat all shareholders honorably" despite the Post's dual-class structure "that we've
  seen some managers abuse." "On behalf of our shareholders, Charlie and I had long ago put her
  in Berkshire's Hall of Fame."
