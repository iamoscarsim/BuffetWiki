# Don Wurster

The CEO of **[[National Indemnity Company]]** since 1989 and, in Buffett's [[2004]] telling, the
"current managerial star — make that **superstar**" of Berkshire's flagship primary insurer. Long a
quiet name in the "other primary" insurance cluster (mentioned across the 1990s letters alongside
Rod Eldred, John Kizer, Tom Nerney and Don Towle), Wurster became the human face of the [[2004]]
**"Portrait of a Disciplined Underwriter"** essay.

He embodies **[[Insurance Underwriting Discipline]]**: NICO's written premium slid from $366M
(1986) to ~$55M (1999) under his watch because he "**will accept a walk rather than swing at a bad
pitch**" (Buffett's Barry Bonds comparison), pricing only to make a profit rather than to match the
most optimistic competitor. By 2004 he had amassed **~$950M of float** that "is almost certain to be
proved the negative-cost kind." He is also, Buffett notes, "the graduate" — the only one of NICO's
four CEOs to have finished college ("extraordinary business ability is largely innate").
