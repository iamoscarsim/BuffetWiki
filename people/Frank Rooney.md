# Frank Rooney

Frank Rooney is the CEO of [[H. H. Brown]], the work-shoe maker Berkshire acquired
on July 1, 1991 ([[1991]]). His path to Berkshire is a small parable about Buffett's
managers. Rooney married Frances Heffernan, a daughter of Brown's founder
[[Ray Heffernan]] — who, before the wedding, "sternly advised" his future son-in-law
"to forget any ideas he might have about working for his father-in-law." Rooney
instead became CEO of **Melville Shoe** (later Melville Corp.), where over 23 years
(1964–1986) earnings averaged more than 20% on equity and the stock rose from $16 to
$960. After Heffernan fell ill, he asked Rooney to run Brown.

Much of Buffett's enthusiasm for the Brown purchase came from Rooney's willingness to
stay on as CEO. Like most Berkshire managers, "he has no financial need to work but
does so because he loves the game and likes to excel." Buffett frames his own role
toward such people in his recurring metaphor: managers of this stripe "cannot be
'hired' in the normal sense"; what Berkshire must do "is provide a concert hall in
which business artists of this class will wish to perform." Rooney is one of the
operating "masters" whose competence let Buffett take the [[Salomon]] interim
chairmanship without Berkshire missing a beat.

## Evolution
- [1991] Introduced as CEO of newly acquired [[H. H. Brown]]; former Melville Corp.
  CEO; the "concert hall" passage attaches to him.
- [1993] "**Did double duty**": led H. H. Brown to **record profits (+35%** over 1992) *and*
  was "key to our merger with [[Dexter Shoe]]" — having known [[Harold Alfond]] and
  [[Peter Lunder]] for decades, he made the introduction and assured them Berkshire would be
  an ideal "home." At **72**, "he's setting an even faster pace at Berkshire… when he swings,
  the ball disappears far over the fence."
- [1998] **The matchmaker again** — this time for an *acquisition*. It was Rooney, a delighted
  NetJets customer, who four years earlier told Buffett about the fractional-jet program and
  suggested he meet [[Rich Santulli]] — the introduction that eventually led to Berkshire's purchase
  of **[[Executive Jet]]**.
