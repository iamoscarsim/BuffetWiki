# Al Ueltschi

Al Ueltschi is the founder and CEO of **[[FlightSafety International]]**, the
pilot-training company Berkshire acquired in 1996. A lifelong aviator — he "actually
piloted Charles Lindbergh," barnstormed in the 1930s, then worked for Pan Am's legendary
chief Juan Trippe — Ueltschi founded FlightSafety in **1951** while still at Pan Am and
built it into a worldwide simulator manufacturer and trainer of pilots.

He was **79** when Berkshire bought the company, but Buffett wrote that he "looks and
acts about 55" and would "run operations just as he has in the past: We never fool with
success." Buffett met him on September 18, 1996 and "in about 60 seconds … knew that Al
was exactly our kind of manager" — the archetype of the owner-operator Berkshire seeks
(see [[Acquisition Criteria]], [[Decentralization]]). The famous quip: since Berkshire
won't split its stock, "we will split his age 2-for-1 when he hits 100."

## Evolution
- [1996] Founder/CEO of [[FlightSafety International]] at its acquisition; emblem of
  Berkshire's preference for older, proven owner-managers ("difficult to teach a new dog
  old tricks").
- [1999] Still running [[FlightSafety International]] — "**a remarkable manager** who [has] no
  financial need to work but thrive[s] on helping [his] company grow and excel." Buffett notes he
  "started FSI in 1951 with $10,000"; the company is now "five times or so the size of its nearest
  competitor," operating 222 simulators.
- [2000] **83 and "continues to operate at full throttle."** Buffett, no fan of stock splits, jokes
  he is "planning to split Al's age 2-for-1 when he hits 100. (If it works, guess who's next.)"
  FlightSafety spent $272M on simulators under his leadership, with "growth … as far as the eye can
  see."
