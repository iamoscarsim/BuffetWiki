# Susan Jacques

Susan Jacques was named President and CEO of **[[Borsheim's]]** in early 1994, at age 34,
having risen "in 11 years from a $4-an-hour job that she took at the store when she was 23"
([[1993]]). Her promotion is a small parable of the Berkshire meritocracy — talent advancing
from the sales floor to the top job — and she becomes the long-running steward of Berkshire's
flagship jewelry operation. This page will track her tenure as later letters report on it.

## Evolution
- [1993] Introduced as the new President and CEO of Borsheim's, promoted from a $4/hour
  starting job 11 years earlier.
- [1995] At 36, "a terrific leader" — [[Borsheim's]] sales rose **11.7%**; Buffett notes she
  "had an even better year, giving birth to her second son at the start of the Christmas season."
- [2007] [[Borsheim's]] sales rose **15.1%** (a 27% gain over Shareholder Weekend) after a remodel
  and expansion Jacques pushed for "two years ago" over Buffett's skepticism — "I was skeptical, but
  **Susan was right**." Buffett restates the meritocracy lesson: she "came to Borsheim's 25 years ago
  as a **$4-an-hour saleswoman**… She's smart, she loves the business… **That beats having an MBA
  degree any time.**"
