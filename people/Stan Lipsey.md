# Stan Lipsey

Stan Lipsey is the longtime publisher of the [[Buffalo Evening News]] (later the
Buffalo News) and one of the operating managers Buffett singles out for keeping a
dominant newspaper both editorially strong and tightly run. He appears in the
Sources-of-Earnings era from the early 1980s and became publisher on Henry Urban's
retirement around 1983.

In the [[1986]] letter — the paper's tenth anniversary under Berkshire — Buffett
credits Lipsey with "a truly superb managerial job": a third consecutive year of
falling man-hours and tightly controlled costs, lifting operating margins even as the
News raised ad rates more modestly than the industry. Crucially, the cost discipline
never came at the expense of the product: the paper keeps a **50% "news hole,"** the
highest of any dominant U.S. paper its size, and Buffett vows it would not cut that
ratio even if margins shrank. Lipsey's stewardship, alongside editor Murray Light's,
is the human side of Buffett's case that a dominant newspaper is one of the best
businesses in the world.

## Evolution
- [1982–84] Credited (with Henry Urban, Murray Light) during the News's turn to
  profitability; became publisher on Urban's retirement; praised for cost control.
- [1986] "A truly superb managerial job" — third straight year of lower man-hours and
  improved margins while preserving the 50% news hole.
- [2012] **Retirement — and Buffett's full tribute.** At year-end Lipsey retired as publisher of the
  [[Buffalo Evening News|Buffalo News]]. "It's no exaggeration… to say that the News might now be
  extinct were it not for Stan." Buffett recounts the 1977 Sunday-edition launch, the antitrust suit
  and harsh district-court ruling (reversed 3-0 after 17 months), the four "very dark days" years of
  hemorrhaging money, and the 1982 competitive victory — Lipsey having left "his comfortable way of
  life in Omaha" (where he'd helped a small weekly win a 1973 Pulitzer) to rescue the paper. "As both a
  friend and as a manager, Stan is simply the best."
