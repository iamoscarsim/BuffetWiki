# Rich Santulli

Rich Santulli is the CEO of **[[Executive Jet]]** (NetJets), which Berkshire bought in
1998. He "**created the fractional ownership industry in 1986**, by visualizing an
important new way of using planes," then "combined guts and talent to turn his idea into a
major business" — building EJA into by far the largest operator in its field.

Buffett, an enthusiastic NetJets customer before he was an owner, calls Santulli "a
terrific executive" who "is fun" and — "like most of our managers … has no economic need
whatsoever to work." Santulli "spends his time at EJA because it's his baby — and he wants
to see how far he can take it." Asked how far that is, "We both already know the answer …
to the ends of the earth." It took Santulli "about 15 minutes to sell" Buffett a quarter-
share of a Hawker 1000 years before the acquisition.

## Evolution
- [1998] Introduced as EJA's founder-CEO at acquisition; creator of the fractional-jet
  industry (1986); the owner-operator who runs the business "because it's his baby."
- [1999] Still driving [[Executive Jet]]'s growth — now "larger than its next two competitors
  combined," expanding into Europe, with customers owning >$2B of planes and $4.2B on order. One of
  the founder-entrepreneurs "who have no financial need to work but thrive on helping their
  companies grow and excel."
- [2000] "**Rich Santulli is the dynamo behind this business.**" EJA's recurring revenue grew 49%;
  Buffett applauds his "insistence on unusually high amounts of pilot training (an average of 23 days
  a year)" and single-model crewing — a safety standard that matters personally, since Buffett's
  family and most Berkshire directors fly NetJets.
