# Bertie Buffett

Roberta ("Bertie") Buffett, Warren Buffett's sister, whom he adopts in the [[2023]] letter as his **"perfect
mental model"** of the Berkshire shareholder he writes for — a long-term owner who "trust[s] Berkshire with
their savings without any expectation of resale." She is "smart, wise," follows business news ("reading
four newspapers daily") but is no economic expert, and "instinctively know[s] that pundits should always
be ignored… In short, she is nobody's fool." Throughout the letter Buffett tests his explanations against
whether **"Bertie will find them useful."**

She doubles as a parable for [[The American Tailwind|patient, do-nothing investing]]: after trading
actively for two decades, in **1980 (age 46)** Bertie put her money into Berkshire and a mutual fund and
"**made no new trades during the next 43 years**," becoming "very rich, even after making large
philanthropic gifts (think nine figures)" — "millions of American investors could have followed her
reasoning, which involved only the common sense she had somehow absorbed as a child in Omaha."

## Evolution
- [[2023]] Introduced as the model "lifetime" shareholder and the imagined reader of the letter; her
  43-year buy-and-hold record is offered as proof of patient investing and the "Omaha Effect."
- [[2024]] **Back at the meeting, at 91.** Buffett's affectionate coda: Bertie (now 91) returns to the
  annual gathering with two daughters; they talk "regularly on Sundays using old-fashion telephones,"
  comparing canes — a warm reprise of the 2023 "model shareholder" framing as Buffett's own tenure closes.
