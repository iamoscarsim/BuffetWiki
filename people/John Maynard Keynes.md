# John Maynard Keynes

The economist John Maynard Keynes appears in the letters not for his macroeconomics
but as a **practicing investor** whose conclusions mirror Buffett's own. In the
[[1991]] letter Buffett quotes — "as time goes on, I get more and more convinced that
the right method in investment is to put fairly large sums into enterprises which one
thinks one knows something about and in the management of which one thoroughly
believes" — from Keynes's August 15, 1934 letter to his business associate F. C.
Scott. Keynes's point that "it is a mistake to think that one limits one's risk by
spreading too much between enterprises about which one knows little," and that there
are "seldom more than two or three enterprises… in which I personally feel myself
entitled to put *full* confidence," is one of Buffett's favorite outside authorities
for [[Concentration]] and the [[Circle of Competence]]. Buffett admires Keynes's
"brilliance as a practicing investor" as the equal of his brilliance in thought.

## Evolution
- [1991] Introduced via the 1934 Keynes-to-Scott letter, marshaled in support of
