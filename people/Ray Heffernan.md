# Ray Heffernan

Ray Heffernan was the founder-builder of [[H. H. Brown]]. In 1927, a 29-year-old
Heffernan bought the company — then located in North Brookfield, Massachusetts — for
$10,000 and ran it for a **62-year career**, a longevity Buffett relished noting (at
age 90 he "was still joining new golf clubs") ([[1991]]). By his retirement in early
1990, Brown had three U.S. plants and one in Canada, employed close to 2,000 people,
and earned about $25M pre-tax annually.

Heffernan's daughter Frances married [[Frank Rooney]] — whom Heffernan had warned off
ever working for him, "one of Mr. Heffernan's few mistakes," since Rooney went on to a
distinguished career at Melville and ultimately back to run Brown at Heffernan's own
request. After Heffernan died late in 1990, his family decided to sell the company,
leading to Berkshire's 1991 acquisition. The managerial group Heffernan developed is
part of what made Brown able to "prosper" in a brutal industry.
