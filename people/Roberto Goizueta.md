# Roberto Goizueta

Roberto Goizueta became CEO of [[Coca-Cola]] in 1981 and, in Buffett's telling, "a new
company" was born — the transformation that made Coca-Cola the investment Berkshire
bought in 1988–89. Introduced in the [[1989]] letter's Coca-Cola essay, Goizueta is the
manager whose "truly rare blend of marketing and financial skills" Buffett credits for
the stock's appeal.

Where most consumer-products CEOs let either marketing or finance dominate "at the
expense of the other discipline," Buffett writes, "with Roberto, the mesh of marketing
and finance is perfect and the result is a shareholder's dream." Working with
**[[Don Keough]]** (Buffett's former across-the-street Omaha neighbor), Goizueta
"rethought and focused the company's policies and then energetically carried them out,"
driving explosive overseas growth in "what was already the world's most ubiquitous
product." Buffett's only regret was not buying sooner — he should have started "soon
after Roberto and Don began running things."

## Evolution
- [1989] Introduced as the CEO (from 1981) whose marketing-and-finance mastery
  rejuvenated Coca-Cola and underpinned Berkshire's investment; partnered with
  [[Don Keough]].
- [1993] Quantified: Goizueta (CEO from 1981) and [[Don Keough]] "took hold of a company that
  had stagnated during the previous decade and moved it from **$4.4 billion of market value to
  $58 billion in less than 13 years** — what a difference a pair of managers like this makes,
  even when their product has been around for 100 years." (Keough retired in 1993.)
- [1997] **Died in October 1997.** Buffett's tribute: he reread "every one of the more than 100
  letters and notes he had written me during the past nine years" — "a guidebook for success in
  both business and life" — praising Goizueta's "brilliant and clear strategic vision … always
  aimed at advancing the well-being of Coke shareholders" and "a burning sense of urgency." The
  note he quotes: "what she refers to as an **obsession**, you call **focus**. I like your term
  much better." Goizueta had prepared "a seamless succession" to **Doug Ivester** — "the Coca-Cola
  Company will be the same steamroller under Doug as it was under Roberto."
