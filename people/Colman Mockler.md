# Colman Mockler

Colman Mockler, Jr. was the CEO of [[Gillette]] when Berkshire bought its $600M
convertible preferred in [[1989]], and one of the managers Buffett cited as the real
basis for the investment — part of the group (with [[John Gutfreund]], Ed Colodny, and
Andy Sigler) who "meet [the] test in spades" of people Berkshire wants to "link up
with… like, admire, and trust."

Buffett singled out Gillette as the one of the four preferred investments whose
business and management he truly understood and admired, and he joined its board.
Mockler led Gillette through the launch of the **Sensor** razor and a defense against
hostile takeover bids, steering the franchise toward the growth that made Berkshire's
stake enormously profitable. Buffett's regard for him deepened in later letters; Mockler's
sudden death in early 1991 (at his desk, shortly after a major Gillette success) is
mourned in a later letter as the loss of an exemplary manager.

## Evolution
- [1989] Introduced as Gillette's CEO; the management reason behind Berkshire's $600M
  preferred. Buffett expects Gillette "under Colman's management" to "far exceed" an
  average-business return.
- [1990] **Died in January 1991**, mourned in Buffett's eulogy: "no description better
  fitted Colman than 'gentleman' — a word signifying integrity, courage and modesty,"
  coupled with humor and "exceptional business ability." A *Forbes* cover story days
  before his death praising Gillette's quality-driven culture "in fact painted a portrait
  of Colman." His Gillette preferred was being converted to common as he died.
