# Susan Buffett

Susan Buffett — Warren Buffett's wife — is introduced in the [[1990]] letter as the
nominee to succeed the retiring [[Ken Chace]] on Berkshire's board, and as the linchpin
of Buffett's succession plan. She was already "the second largest shareholder of
Berkshire" and, Buffett notes, "if she outlives me will inherit all of my stock and
effectively control the company."

The passage is one of the clearest early statements of Buffett's intentions for
Berkshire after him (see [[Permanent Ownership]]): Susan "knows, and agrees, with my
thoughts on successor management and also shares my view that neither Berkshire nor its
subsidiary businesses and important investments should be sold simply because some very
high bid is received." Neither his nor her estate plan is "designed to preserve the
family fortune"; both aim at "preserving the character of Berkshire and returning the
fortune to society." Were Buffett to die "tomorrow," he assures shareholders, no stock
would be sold and "both a controlling shareholder and a manager with philosophies
similar to mine would follow me." (Susan Buffett died in 2004, predeceasing him.)

## Evolution
- [1990] Nominated to the board (succeeding [[Ken Chace]]); named as the controlling
  shareholder-to-be and guardian of Berkshire's character — the fortune destined for
  society, not the family, and the businesses never to be sold for a high bid.
- [1993] Her role is set within the formal **[[Corporate Governance]]** framework: after
  Buffett's death his stock passes to Susie (or, if she predeceases him, a foundation),
  moving Berkshire into governance "mode 3" — a vitally interested but non-management
  controlling owner. Son **[[Howard Buffett]]** joined the board in 1993 to represent that
  interest. "All in all, we're prepared for 'the truck.'"
