# Barnett Helzberg

Barnett Helzberg Jr. owned [[Helzberg's Diamond Shops]] — the Kansas City jewelry chain his
grandfather founded in 1915 — and, as an owner of four Berkshire shares who'd attended the
Annual Meeting, **stopped Buffett on a New York street corner** (58th and Fifth, May 1994) to
mention he had a business that might interest him ([[1995]]). The encounter became Berkshire's
emblem of "ABWA — acquisitions by walking around."

Then 60, Barnett "loved the business but also wanted to feel free of it," having brought in
CEO **[[Jeff Comment]]** in 1988; he also wished to diversify his family's holdings out of "a
single, very competitive industry." Berkshire — a tax-free stock buyer with a "buying to keep"
reputation — "was made to order for him." Buffett notes that Barnett, "under no obligation,"
shared a meaningful part of his sale proceeds with many associates: "When someone behaves that
generously, you know you are going to be treated right as a buyer."

## Evolution
- [1995] Introduced as the seller of Helzberg's Diamond Shops via the street-corner ABWA
  meeting; sold for stock and shared proceeds with associates.
- [2000] **The referral network compounds.** A friend of **Ed Bridge**, Barnett gave Berkshire "a
  strong recommendation" when the Bridge family decided to sell **[[Ben Bridge Jeweler]]** — leading
  to Berkshire's acquisition of the West Coast jeweler. A satisfied seller becoming a source of the
  next deal, exactly as Buffett describes his "wait-for-the-phone-to-ring" model.
