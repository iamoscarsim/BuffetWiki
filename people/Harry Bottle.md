# Harry Bottle

Harry Bottle is the turnaround manager Buffett and [[Charlie Munger]] have twice
called on to fix a troubled Berkshire-orbit business — "whose special knack was never
forgetting the fundamental." He appears in the [[1987]] letter's *déjà vu* close as a
small parable about managerial talent and Munger's network.

In 1962, facing "severe managerial problems" at **Dempster Mill Manufacturing Co.** (a
pump and farm-implement maker controlled by Buffett Partnership), Buffett went to
Munger, who suggested his California friend Harry Bottle. Bottle was running Dempster
within a week and "our problems disappeared almost immediately"; Buffett named him "Man
of the Year" in his 1962 partnership letter. Twenty-four years later, when Berkshire's
small **K&W Products** (automotive compounds, overseen by Munger) stumbled in 1985–86,
Munger again called Bottle — now 68 — made him CEO, and "sat back to await the
inevitable." In 1987 K&W's profits set a record, **up more than 300%**, while capital
employed (receivables and inventories) fell ~20%. "If we run into another managerial
problem ten or twenty years down the road, you know whose phone will ring."

## Evolution
- [1987] The Dempster Mill (1962) and K&W Products (1986–87) episodes recounted: a
  fundamentals-first fixer summoned via [[Charlie Munger]]; lifted K&W profits 300%.
- [1989] Still going at **70**: attended a bankruptcy auction and "for a pittance"
  acquired a product line "a natural for K&W," potentially lifting that company's profits
  another ~50%. "Watch this space for future bulletins on Harry's triumphs."
