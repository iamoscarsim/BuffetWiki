# Chuck Huggins

Chuck Huggins ran [[See's Candies]] from its 1972 purchase, and Buffett credits
him by name in the [[1977]] letter for the company's "good progress" — pre-tax
earnings nearly tripling since acquisition with little added capital. Huggins
became the long-tenured steward of one of Berkshire's most cherished franchises
and a recurring example of the kind of devoted operating manager Buffett seeks.

## Evolution
- [1977] Credited for See's strong results since the 1972 purchase.
- [1986] Credited for stabilizing See's same-store poundage and holding margins on
  minimal price increases. Buffett notes Huggins had run See's for 15 years and "has
  never been to Omaha" — a token of Berkshire's hands-off [[Decentralization]].
- [1999] Credited for [[See's Candies]]' record **24% operating margin** and its $857M of cumulative
  pre-tax profit since 1972. Buffett coins **"Huggins' Law"**: See's pre-tax profit in $ millions ≈
  Huggins' age expressed as a percent — about 10% when he took charge at 46, and **100% now that
  he's 74** — so "Charlie and I now become giddy at the mere thought of Chuck's birthday." His
  "fanatical insistence on both product quality and friendly service has rewarded customers,
  employees and owners."
- [2005] **Retires** after a **34-year** tenure at [[See's Candies]] in which profits grew
  **more than tenfold** in a flat industry. Buffett and Munger, "new at the game of selecting
  managers" in 1972, "hit a home run with this appointment"; **[[Brad Kinstler]]** succeeds him.
  "America's number one candy maker… a richly-deserved round of applause."
