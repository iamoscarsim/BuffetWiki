# Lorimer Davidson

Lorimer Davidson — "Davy" — is the [[GEICO]] executive (later its CEO) who gave the
20-year-old Buffett his foundational education in insurance, and one of Buffett's lifelong
heroes ([[1995]]). On a cold Saturday in January 1951, the Columbia student (drawn to GEICO
only because his teacher **[[Ben Graham]]** was its chairman) took the train to GEICO's
closed Washington headquarters, pounded on the door, and was shown to the one man working —
Davidson, then Assistant to the President. "Davy graciously spent four hours or so showering
me with both kindness and instruction. No one has ever received a better half-day course in
how the insurance industry functions." It was Davidson who made clear that GEICO's **direct
marketing** gave it a cost advantage agency-based rivals could never abandon — the insight
behind Buffett's lifelong conviction about GEICO.

"Berkshire would not be where it is today if Davy had not been so generous with his time."
Davidson, **93** in 1995, "remains my friend and teacher," and "warmly supported" the
full-acquisition transaction even though it cost him a large personal tax. Buffett used the
1995 letter to thank him publicly "on behalf of Berkshire's shareholders."

## Evolution
- [1995] Recalled as the GEICO executive who taught Buffett the insurance business (and
  GEICO's direct-marketing edge) in 1951; thanked at 93 as a lifelong hero and teacher.
- [1997] **Turned 95.** Buffett restates "the enormous debt that you and I owe" to Davy, "my friend
  and teacher for **47 years**" — "had I not met Davy, I might never have grown to understand the
  whole field of insurance, which over the years has played such a key part in Berkshire's success."
  He and [[Tony Nicely]] hoped to coax the travel-limited Davidson to the annual meeting so
  shareholders could thank him.
- [1999] **Died last November, days after his 97th birthday.** "For GEICO, Davy was a business
  giant who moved the company up to the big leagues. For me, he was a **friend, teacher and hero**
  … my life would have developed far differently had he not been a part of it." Buffett, [[Tony Nicely]] and [[Lou Simpson]] had visited him in August and "marveled at his mental alertness —
  particularly in all matters regarding GEICO." "He was the company's number one supporter right
  up to the end, and we will forever miss him."
