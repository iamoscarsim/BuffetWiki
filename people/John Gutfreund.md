# John Gutfreund

John Gutfreund was the CEO of [[Salomon]] Inc when Berkshire bought its $700M
convertible preferred in [[1987]]. Buffett's investment was, by his own account, less
a bet on investment banking than on Gutfreund personally — "Charlie and I like, admire
and trust John."

The trust traced to 1976, when Gutfreund "played a key role in [[GEICO]]'s escape from
near-bankruptcy," and to repeated instances Buffett had witnessed of Gutfreund steering
clients away from unwise transactions even when doing so cost Salomon a fee — "service-
above-self behavior [that] is far from automatic in Wall Street." That admiration would
be sorely tested in 1991, when a Treasury-auction bidding scandal at Salomon Brothers
forced Gutfreund out and drew Buffett in as interim chairman to save the firm — an arc
later letters will record.

## Evolution
- [1987] Introduced as Salomon's CEO; the reason Berkshire made its $700M preferred
  investment. Known to Buffett since the 1976 GEICO rescue; praised for integrity.
- [1991] **Forced out** in the wake of [[Salomon]]'s Treasury-auction bidding scandal,
  precipitating Buffett's election as Interim Chairman on August 18, 1991. The 1991 letter
  refers shareholders to Buffett's separate Salomon-annual-report letter for details rather
  than recounting "the events that led to my appointment." (The earlier admiration for
  Gutfreund's integrity now sits against the firm's crisis.)
