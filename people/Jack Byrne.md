# Jack Byrne

Jack Byrne took the helm of [[GEICO]] in 1976 when the company stood "at the very
edge of bankruptcy," and supplied the "managerial brilliance" needed for its
resuscitation. In the [[1980]] letter Buffett credits Byrne with the rescue while
making the careful distinction that GEICO was never a true turnaround — its
low-cost franchise was intact "although submerged in a sea of financial and
operating troubles," like a patient with "a localized excisable cancer" needing a
skilled surgeon. Buffett's enthusiasm is unreserved: "There aren't many Jack Byrnes
in the managerial world, or GEICOs in the business world. What could be better than
buying into a partnership with both of them?" Byrne later led other insurance
ventures (Fireman's Fund, White Mountains) that intersect with Berkshire's story.

## Evolution
- [1980] Credited with rescuing GEICO from near-bankruptcy; the surgeon to GEICO's
  "excisable cancer."
- [1985] **Left GEICO mid-year to become Chairman/CEO of [[Fireman's Fund]]** —
  prompting Berkshire's FFIC quota-share ("Never one to let go of a meal ticket, we
  have followed Jack Byrne"). Praised for leaving GEICO in the able hands of Bill
  Snyder and [[Lou Simpson]].
- [1995] Recalled in the [[GEICO]] full-acquisition history as the CEO who "came in… in 1976
  and took drastic remedial measures," saving GEICO from the bankruptcy its early-1970s
  underpricing nearly caused — the rescue that justified Berkshire's original purchase.
